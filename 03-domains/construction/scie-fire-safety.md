# SCIE fire-safety fraud & AL conversions

> **Client sees:** freshly renovated AL-ready apartment, new drywall, new doors. | **Actual issue:** standard drywall where fire-rated board is mandatory; uncertified doors; narrowed escape routes. | **Typical era:** flip/AL conversions 2015+. | **Tool needed:** eyes — gypsum core colour, hinge-side door tags, corridor tape measure. | **Legal weapon:** [[dl-67-2023|DL 67/2023]] + ASAE complaint; life-safety = Tier 1 walk-away if fraudulent in an AL.

## The fraud patterns (module 7.3; regime: DL 220/2008 + Portaria 1532/2008)

1. **Drywall bait-and-switch.** Escape routes and shafts require fire-resistant board (Type F, pink/brown core). Flippers install standard gray/white Type A — zero fire resistance, fails in minutes.
2. **Escape-route encroachment.** Kitchens, cladding, and built-ins narrowing corridors below the mandatory 1.10–1.20m.
3. **Fire-door fraud.** Standard interior doors where EI30/EI60 certified doors are required; certified doors wedged permanently open, voiding compartmentalization.
4. **Self-certification loophole.** Technicians sign the SCIE *termo de responsabilidade* knowing materials will be substituted on site; the Câmara has no fire engineers to verify — the [[dl-10-2024-simplex|Simplex]] pattern in miniature.

## Field checks (P4 interior scan)

Exposed/damaged drywall core: gray/white = illegal in escape routes, pink/brown = compliant · metal certification tag on the hinge side of fire doors — no tag, no door · emergency lighting hardwired + battery-backed, not decorative · demand *Termo de Responsabilidade SCIE* before CPCV.
