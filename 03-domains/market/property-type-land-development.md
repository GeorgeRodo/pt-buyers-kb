# Property type — land & development projects

The most specialist segment a buyer's agent handles: self-builders, small developers, and investors in a future project. DD intensity is very high because a plot's value is set not by what's on it but by what can legally be built on it — and Portugal's urbanistic regime (PDM zoning, urbanistic parameters, construction licensing) is detailed, regionally variable, and frequently ambiguous. An agent not deeply familiar with the *Plano Director Municipal* (PDM) regime for the specific *concelho* should refer the buyer to a specialist land broker or the buyer's lawyer for the urbanistic work.

## Distinctive due diligence

- **PDM zoning** — plot classification: *urbano* (urban), *urbanizável* (urbanisable), or *rústico* (rustic). Only *urbano* and *urbanizável* are generally buildable; *rústico* has very limited buildability.
- **Urbanistic parameters** — *índice de utilização* (building ratio), *índice de implantação* (footprint ratio), *altura máxima* (max height), *taxa de ocupação do solo*, setbacks, minimum plot size.
- **Access** — public road frontage vs. private easement; road classification; maintenance responsibility; right-of-way documentation.
- **Utilities** — water, electricity, sewage, telecom at the plot boundary; if absent, extension cost often €10k–100k+ with its own timeline.
- **Topography** — slope, retaining-wall requirements, cut/fill volume, geotechnical conditions, drainage.
- **Environmental** — Natura 2000 protected habitat, watercourse protection, contaminated former-industrial land, forestry restrictions.
- **Subdivision rules** — whether the plot can be subdivided; minimum lot size; *condóminio horizontal* rules.
- **Construction licence feasibility** — pre-application enquiry to the Câmara; typical 6–18 month permit timeline.
- **Project feasibility study** — architect's concept design within the parameters, indicative construction cost, ROI model.

## Common red flags

- Plot classified *rústico* — very limited buildability, usually only agricultural support structures.
- No public road access — the easement may be unclear, contested, or revocable.
- Utilities >100m from the plot — extension cost can exceed the plot's value.
- Plot in a Natura 2000 zone — an EIA may be required, extending the permit timeline to 12–24 months.
- Slope >30% — earthworks and retaining-wall cost may render the project uneconomic.
- Seller cannot produce a *caderneta rústica* or a definitive PDM extract — title or zoning may be unclear.

## Recommended specialists

Urbanistic lawyer with documented Câmara experience in the specific *concelho*; topographer (*topógrafo*) for boundary survey; geotechnical engineer for soil bearing capacity and foundation input; architect for pre-application concept and feasibility.

## Negotiation tactics

Aggressive discount (15–25%) supported by urbanistic constraints and infrastructure cost; a condition precedent on the Câmara pre-application enquiry result gives the buyer a walk-away if buildability comes in below assumption; extend the DD period to 90–120 days to allow topographic and geotechnical work.
