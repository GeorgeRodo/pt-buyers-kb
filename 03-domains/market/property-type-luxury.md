# Property type — luxury (€1.5m+)

Quinta do Lago and Vale do Lobo in the Algarve; Cascais, Estoril, and Sintra's prime areas; Lisbon's Príncipe Real, Lapa, Chiado, and Avenida da Liberdade; Porto's Foz do Douro; the branded residences of Comporta and Melides. This segment operates under different dynamics from the general market: international buyer competition is intense, transaction sizes justify deeper DD investment, and the buyer profile (HNW, time-poor, expects concierge service) demands a different service model entirely.

## Distinctive due diligence

- **Title** — often complex (corporate structures, multi-jurisdiction, family trusts); deeper title-chain verification; beneficial-ownership disclosure.
- **Tax structure** — optimal acquisition structure (individual vs. company vs. holding); AIMI implications; pre-acquisition tax structuring is essential, not optional.
- **Privacy & security** — discreet marketing, non-disclosure agreements, security assessment (gating, alarm, CCTV, staff vetting).
- **Amenities & service** — concierge, security staff, housekeeping, pool/garden maintenance, staff quarters, service entrance.
- **Lifestyle proximity** — golf, marina, equestrian, international school, private healthcare, airport.
- **International competition** — buyer often competing globally, multi-currency, time-pressured; specialist luxury networks matter.

## Common red flags

- Title held in an opaque offshore structure — exposed to penal tax treatment under Portuguese anti-abuse rules.
- Hidden encumbrances (rights of way, neighbour view protections) — material to privacy and use.
- Recent unpermitted alterations to a character property — regularisation may require demolition.
- Inadequate security for the buyer's profile — significant retrofit cost.
- Condominium/community rules incompatible with the buyer's intended use (events, staff, vehicles).

## Recommended specialists

Senior lawyer with cross-border and HNW experience; tax advisor with Portuguese and buyer's-home-jurisdiction expertise; security consultant where material; concierge service provider for ongoing lifestyle support.

## Cost-of-ownership nuances

AIMI surcharge on high-value residential property held by individuals; higher staffing costs (concierge, security, housekeeping); higher insurance for high-value contents and specialty cover; higher condominium fees for luxury amenities.

## Negotiation tactics

Personal rapport with the seller is critical — HNW sellers often value discretion and certainty over maximum price. Discounts are modest (3–5%, comparable-supported); larger discounts are rarely achieved on prime unique stock. Concessions on contents (art, furniture), staff continuity, or closing timeline are frequently more valuable than price.
