# Property type — resale / traditional homes

The workhorse of the Portuguese residential market: 2- and 3-bed apartments and houses in modern (post-1990) buildings — Lisbon's Avenidas Novas, Telheiras and Lumiar; Porto's Antas and Campanhã; Cascais and Sintra suburban stock; the Algarve's established resort apartments. The DD profile here is closest to the "standard" case running through the rest of the wiki — and this is the segment where **condominium health is the single most reliable predictor of post-purchase satisfaction**.

## Distinctive due diligence

- **Condominium health** — reserve fund per unit, owner-arrears rate, recent/pending works, insurance, owner-occupier ratio, recent litigation. See [[condominio-law]].
- **Community rules** — pet restrictions, AL restrictions, commercial use, quiet hours, renovation restrictions, visitor parking.
- **Common areas** — lift condition, entrance, garage, terrace, pool/garden, recent refurbishment history.
- **Building age band** — 1990–2010: typically sound structure, weaker energy performance. 2010–2020: better energy, often higher condo fees. 2020+: often still under builder warranty.
- **Recent refurbishment** — verify it was licensed and the *ficha técnica* updated.

## Common red flags

- Condominium reserve fund <€500/unit — a special assessment within 24 months is likely.
- Owner arrears >15% — chronic under-funding, recent or pending litigation likely.
- Recent unpermitted works in the unit — the buyer inherits regularisation liability.
- Condominium regulation prohibits the buyer's intended use (AL, pet, home office).
- Lift >25 years old with no scheduled replacement — special assessment likely.

## Recommended specialists

Standard surveyor for building condition; condominium document audit (agent + lawyer); for older resale (pre-1990) treat as [[property-type-historical|historical]] and bring in a structural engineer.
