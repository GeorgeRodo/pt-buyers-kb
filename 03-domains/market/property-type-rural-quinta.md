# Property type — rural / quinta / farm

Quintas, *herdades*, and *casais* in the Alentejo, central Portugal, the Silver Coast interior, the Algarve *serra*, Madeira, and the Azores. This segment appeals to lifestyle retirement buyers, rural-tourism investors, and agricultural purchasers. It is the most property-type-specific DD profile of any segment: water rights, access, forestry compliance, and habitational licensing are routinely misunderstood, and they produce the most expensive post-purchase surprises in the entire market.

## Distinctive due diligence

- **Water rights** — source (borehole/*furo*, well/*poço*, spring/*mina* or *nascente*, cistern, mains), depth, flow rate, potability test, DRA feeding licence, irrigation rights from *ribeira*/*rega*. **Water is the single most valuable asset on a rural property** and the most commonly undocumented.
- **Access** — public road frontage vs. easement (*servidão*), road condition, maintenance responsibility, winter passability, right-of-way documentation.
- **Habitational licence** — the main residence must hold a *licença de utilização*; rural ruins without one cannot be reconstructed under the simplified regime — verify before purchase.
- **Agricultural subsidies** — PAC (Common Agricultural Policy) entitlements attached to the land, transfer formalities, entitlement value.
- **Forestry compliance** — eucalyptus clearance, firebreak obligations, protected species (*sobreiro*, *azinheira*), ICNF restrictions.
- **Land classification** — *rústico* vs. *urbano*, agricultural vs. forestry vs. mixed; building rights are very limited on rustic land.
- **Boundary** — cadastral verification (DG Território), marker inspection, neighbour encroachment.
- **Septic & utilities** — *fossa séptica* condition; mains or solar/wind+battery electricity; mains or borehole water; telecoms.
- **Environmental** — Natura 2000, protected habitats, watercourse protection, contamination from prior agricultural use (nitrates, pesticides).

## Common red flags

- No documented water rights.
- Access via a verbal easement only — a neighbour can revoke or block it; demand a registered *servidão*.
- A rural ruin marketed as "rebuildable" — verify with the Câmara; many ruins cannot be reconstructed under current rules.
- Property classified *rústico* with no urbanistic viability for the buyer's intended construction.
- Eucalyptus within 50m of a habitable building — mandatory fire-risk clearance; non-compliance carries penalties.
- No *fossa séptica* licence or a non-compliant system.

## Recommended specialists

Agricultural/rural property lawyer, not a generalist Lisbon lawyer; hydrologist for water-source assessment; surveyor with rural experience; forestry consultant where eucalyptus, pine, or protected species are present.

## Cost-of-ownership nuances

Lower IMI (rustic rate 0.8% on typically low VPT); higher maintenance cost (access road, fencing, water systems, septic, forestry); higher insurance (rural fire risk, isolated property, slower emergency response); higher management cost for non-resident owners (caretaker, gardener, security).

## Region-specific notes

Alentejo: large *herdades*, water scarcity, cork/olive tradition, emerging wine tourism. Silver Coast interior: smaller quintas, mixed agricultural, closer to the coast for second-home use. Algarve *serra*: cooler, wetter, eucalyptus/pine, rural tourism potential. Madeira: steep terrain, *levadas* (water channel) rights, extreme construction cost. Azores: dispersed rural, seismic and volcanic risk, high construction cost, pasture-land value.
