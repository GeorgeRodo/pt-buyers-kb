# Condomínio law & shared-building risk

Buying an apartment means buying into a legal collective. Civil Code arts. 1315–1438-A (horizontal property). Prompt-chain module 8 covers the socio-legal failure modes.

## The four traps

1. **The *colunas montantes* timebomb.** 1960s–80s cast-iron/galvanized risers embedded in slabs corrode internally; failure floods multiple floors and repair requires demolishing walls in every apartment of the vertical stack.
2. **The *Fundo de Reserva* illusion (art. 1428).** Reserve funds are legally required but historically underfunded; when the riser fails, the special assessment (*obra extraordinária*) is unpayable and repair stalls for years.
3. **The unanimity trap (art. 1427).** 2/3 or unanimous votes required for fundamental structural/façade repairs — one obstructive owner can block a safety-critical fix indefinitely.
4. **Administrative fraud.** Developer-retained "sweetheart" administrators hiding construction defects for the first 3–5 years; illegal appropriation of common areas that the new buyer inherits the obligation to litigate; pending condomínio litigation binding new owners.
5. **Downstream collective liability under [[dl-108-2026|DL 108/2026]].** Law 8/2022 protects the buyer from common-part works *approved* before their purchase — the previous owner bears that cost. It does not protect them from common-part non-compliance that goes *undetected* until after purchase: an illegally enclosed balcony, an unapproved facade change, a rooftop extension without proper *comunicação prévia*. Because municipal *fiscalização administrativa* on common parts is exercisable *a todo o tempo* (never expires), a buyer can inherit a demolition or restoration order — split by permillage quota across every owner — years after buying into a building that looked clean at DD time. This is an *obrigação propter rem* (Art. 408 CC): it follows the unit, not the person who caused it. A 2007 Tribunal da Relação do Porto precedent already holds that common-part defects are the condominium's problem, not the seller's — so the buyer's DD-time recourse against the seller under [[dl-67-2023]] doesn't reach this risk; only the *actas* review and reserve-fund health check do.

Read 2–3 years of *actas* (minutes) for repeated leak complaints, unapproved accounts, collapsed turnout · demand reserve-fund statement and any pending special assessments · confirm no pending litigation · declaração de não dívida before closing.
