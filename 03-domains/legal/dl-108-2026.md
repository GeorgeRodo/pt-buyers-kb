# DL 108/2026 — the verification-shift cascade

Published 29 May 2026, **in force from 3 August 2026**. The second, sharper phase of the Simplex reform this KB already tracks as [[dl-10-2024-simplex|DL 10/2024]] — DL 108/2026 doesn't reverse DL 10/2024, it finishes what it started: after removing state *technical* inspection of new builds, it now removes state *documentary* verification at the point of sale too. The habitation license (*licença de utilização*) is no longer required to execute a deed. A notary must instead declare in the deed whether an urban title exists, whether the seller claims it exists without producing it, or whether the seller admits it doesn't.

## Why this is the sharpest confirmation yet of the founding thesis

Both platforms exist because the state stopped verifying what it used to verify, while keeping the power to punish non-compliance indefinitely. DL 10/2024 did this for construction quality; DL 108/2026 does it for legal/documentary status. The buyer who can't tell the difference between a tacitly-approved property and a materially compliant one is exactly who both [[platform-home-inspection|inspection]] and [[platform-buyers-agency|agency]] exist to protect — and DL 108/2026 widens that gap right as this venture is getting started.

## The mechanism

- **Tacit approval, not compliance.** Municipal silence for 20/30/45 days (works/architectural project/subdivision) constitutes approval. But "successive control" — the municipality's one-year window to audit paperwork — is distinct from "*fiscalização administrativa*" (physical site inspection), which **never expires**. A tacitly-approved project can be embargoed or ordered demolished years after the fact.
- **One-year buyer annulment window (Art. 4 §14).** If a property is sold without a proper urban title, the buyer may annul the transaction, reclaim the full price, and pursue damages — but only within one year. This is a buyer weapon and a seller time-bomb simultaneously.
- **Solidary liability (republished Art. 100-A RJUE).** Promoter, owner, project designer, and work director are jointly liable for non-conforming urban operations — fines to **€450,000** for legal entities, coercive demolition, and criminal exposure (document falsification, Art. 256 Penal Code) for a falsified *livro de obra*. This is the state's answer to Portugal's "phoenix company" contractor-insolvency pattern: when the contractor dissolves, the fine lands on whoever is still solvent, almost always the owner.
- **Strict "reconstruction" definition.** Exempt from licensing only if it restores the exact prior state — same footprint, same height, same volume. Anything more is an "extension" requiring full regularization. This is the legal line the [[instagram-renovation-detection|flip-detector]] pattern already polices technically; DL 108/2026 gives it a hard statutory edge.

## Market effect: the three-tier stratification

| Tier | Profile | Price effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Gold Standard | Pre-1951 with municipal exemption certificate, or new builds with third-party audited compliance dossier | +5–10% premium, full bankability |
| 2 — Simplex-Approved | Tacit approvals, unverified prior communications, pending successive control | −3–8% discount, residual risk priced in |
| 3 — Unregularized | Explicit notary declaration of no urban title | −20–40%, cash-only buyer pool |

Banks are becoming the *de facto* regulator the law tried to remove: BdP confirms lenders can still demand license-equivalent documentation for collateral risk assessment even though the deed no longer requires it, and BdP is separately tightening DSTI from 50% to 45%. The net effect for retail (mortgage-dependent) buyers is **deceleration**, not the velocity the law promises — time saved at the municipal desk is spent instead on private legal/technical due diligence and bank compliance friction.

## Condominium downstream liability (new risk vector — see [[condominio-law]])

A buyer of an apartment unit inherits collective liability for common-part non-compliance they had no way to detect pre-purchase — an illegally enclosed balcony, an unapproved facade change, a rooftop extension without *comunicação prévia* — because *fiscalização administrativa* on common parts is exercisable at any time, and restoration costs are split by permillage quota across all owners, including new ones. Law 8/2022 protects the buyer only from works *approved* before their purchase; ongoing conservation and future municipal restoration orders are the buyer's problem regardless of when the underlying non-compliance was created.

## Legal weapon

For sellers: presumption of culpability under Art. 913–914 CC (*vícios ocultos*, same regime as [[dl-67-2023|DL 67/2023]]) is *strengthened*, not replaced — the seller can no longer point to a municipal stamp as a compliance proxy, so "I didn't know" is a weaker defense than it was under DL 10/2024 alone. For agents: Art. 17, Law 15/2013 liability for concealing known defects, against a professional-liability insurance floor (€150,000, unchanged since 2013) that a single claim can now exhaust given median property values.
