# CPCV — the promissory contract

The CPCV, not the deed, is where the transaction is actually won or lost: the escritura merely executes what the CPCV fixed. Playbook Ch. 7.2–7.3 and Appendix C hold the protective clause library.

## Protective architecture (buyer side)

- **Contingencies as conditions precedent:** financing, due-diligence outcome, licensing (licença de utilização issued), condomínio debt clearance.
- **Sinal mechanics:** deposit sizing; statutory default symmetry (seller default → return of double the sinal); escrow discipline.
- **Defect clauses:** remediation schedules with holdbacks; DD-derived defects listed with agreed price adjustments; *reservas* language pre-agreed for the handover record ([[dl-67-2023]]).
- **Timeline mechanics:** completion date with defined extension triggers, not open-ended "when documentation is ready".

## Red flags

- Pressure to sign a developer's "reservation contract" within 48 hours before DD.
- CPCV drafted solely by the seller's lawyer with no buyer contingencies.
- Área declared in CPCV inconsistent with certidão predial / caderneta (>2% discrepancy = illegal works risk).

> [!warning] Appendix C.4 of the playbook gives a different threshold for the same check — "actual area differs from caderneta by >5%" → unpermitted-alteration flag. The 2% figure above traces to the prompt-chain source's registry cross-check step ([[phase-4-due-diligence]]); the 5% figure is the playbook's own CPCV-stage red flag ([[registo-predial|full detail]]). Both are sacred numbers from their respective sources and neither has been reconciled — treat 2% as the tighter internal-audit trigger and 5% as the harder walk-away line until Paul resolves which applies where.

## Protective clause library (Appendix C.3)

Ten modular clauses, each a template for the buyer's lawyer to adapt — pick the ones applicable to the deal:

| Clause | Mechanism |
|---|---|
| Deposit escrow | Sinal held in the buyer's lawyer's escrow account, released only on signing + identity/capacity confirmation + no condition precedent triggered; escrow agent retains funds ≥3 business days post-signing to allow certidão predial and condominium-debt verification |
| DD condition precedent | Contract voidable within a defined DD period (playbook example: 60 days) if legal/technical DD doesn't confirm clean title, valid licensing, no encumbrances, zero condo arrears, and no undisclosed material fact |
| Financing condition precedent | Voidable if a formal mortgage offer isn't obtained within a defined period (example: 45 days) despite a complete, bona fide application to at least one bank |
| Seller default / double deposit | Seller failing to execute the deed without justification owes double the sinal (Civil Code Art. 442); buyer retains the option of specific performance instead |
| Penalty for delay | Daily penalty for either party's unjustified delay beyond the agreed deed date, capped, after which the non-defaulting party may treat the contract as repudiated |
| Survival of warranties | Seller's representations/warranties survive the *escritura* for a defined period (example: 5 years) — buyer's indemnification right is not extinguished by registration |
| Inventory and fixtures | Listed items must be present and working at handover; missing/broken items replaced, repaired, or deducted from the balance due |
| Off-plan completion guarantee | Restates the DL 156/2005 bank guarantee/insurance obligation directly in the CPCV, with a draw right on developer default, insolvency, or non-delivery |
| Off-plan late delivery penalty | Daily penalty (example: 0.1%/day, capped at 10% of price) for late delivery; force majeure suspends but doesn't extinguish the obligation |
| AL licence condition precedent | Voidable (or price-adjustable) if RNAAL registration isn't valid/current/renewable — protects buyers whose case depends on short-term-letting income |
| Urban-title disclosure ([[dl-108-2026|DL 108/2026]]) | CPCV must explicitly document whether an urban title exists, mirroring the notary's mandatory deed declaration (Art. 4 §14). Doing this at CPCV stage rather than leaving it to the deed neutralizes the buyer's own one-year annulment right if the absence was disclosed and priced in — critical for protecting a seller who is knowingly selling Tier 2/3 stock, and equally critical for a buyer's agent to insist on for anything not obviously Tier 1 |
