# Thread — "The 7-second test that exposes a fake renovation"

**1/**
"Fully renovated" listings in Lisbon and Porto often mean one thing: new paint and a kitchen laid over a structure nobody touched. Here's the sequential field scan a forensic inspector runs to tell the difference — no tools most buyers don't already own.

**2/**
Start with a hammer tap on the new plaster and baseboards. Hollow sound = plasterboard installed directly over degraded *reboco* or *tabique* — the wall behind the finish was never repaired, just covered.

**3/**
Open the kitchen cabinets under the sink. Renovators redo the visible cabinetry but rarely touch the carcass behind it. Damp, unrenovated masonry there means the "renovation" stopped at the surface.

**4/**
Run a pin moisture meter at exterior-wall bases and behind heavy furniture. Above 15–20% wood-moisture-equivalent signals active rising damp or condensation — a number that doesn't lie the way a fresh coat of paint does.

**5/**
Check upper corners of exterior walls behind curtains and wardrobes for black *bolor* (mold) — the signature of a thermal-bridge failure that repainting hides but never fixes.

**6/**
Electrical panel: silver conductors stamped "Al" or "1A/2A" mean aluminum wiring — a recognized fire hazard, and a five-minute check before you'd ever consider an offer.

**7/**
Plumbing: gray or orange pipe stamped "PB"/"Polibutileno" signals polybutylene — a flood risk when hidden in-wall, and typically a €15–25k negotiation item once documented.

**8/**
This scan feeds directly into [[dl-67-2023|DL 67/2023]] hidden-defect protection — but only if it's run and documented before you sign. Cosmetic renovation is a viewing-stage problem; catching it there is what a forensic inspection is for.
