# Thread — "Your defect claim has a 30-day fuse"

**1/**
Under DL 67/2023, a hidden defect (*vício oculto*) in a Portuguese property purchase isn't an open-ended claim. Once you discover it, you have 30 days to formally notify the seller. Miss the window, lose the case — regardless of how real the defect is.

**2/**
This is why snagging inspections exist as a distinct service, not an optional add-on. The clock starts at discovery, not at handover — so the sooner a forensic inspection surfaces the defect, the sooner (and more safely) you can act.

**3/**
The single most common buyer mistake: signing a clean *Auto de Receção Provisória* at handover. If a known defect isn't recorded as a formal *reserva* on that document, you weaken the notification mechanism that DL 67/2023 depends on.

**4/**
Generic complaints don't hold up. "There's damp on the wall" loses. A phenolphthalein test proving carbonation, or an ISO 16283 acoustic field measurement showing an RRAA failure, settles cases — because it's evidence a court (or a negotiating seller) can't dismiss.

**5/**
This is the mechanism behind *Perícia Extrajudicial*-grade reporting: technically articulated, standard-referenced findings, produced inside the window that actually matters legally, not after it's closed.

**6/**
Bottom line: DL 67/2023 gives buyers real leverage — but only if the evidence and the timing are both handled correctly. That's the gap a forensic inspection is built to close.
