# Thread — "That gorgeous Lisbon flip might be a paint job over a problem"

**1/**
That "fully renovated" apartment with the beautiful listing photos? Renovation often means new paint, a new kitchen, and nothing else. The structure, the wiring, the plumbing — untouched since the 1970s. Here's how buyers get caught out, and how not to be one of them.

**2/**
The tell is always in the places sellers don't stage: under the kitchen sink, behind the curtains, behind heavy furniture. That's where the real condition of the building lives, and it's exactly what a flipper's photographer never shoots.

**3/**
A hollow sound when you tap the new plaster means it's covering, not replacing, a degraded wall. A hammer and ten minutes tells you what six listing photos won't.

**4/**
Aluminum wiring, polybutylene plumbing, black mold behind a wardrobe — none of these are dealbreakers by themselves. They're each a specific, priced item: a €15–25k plumbing replacement, a rewire, a damp remediation. That's not a red flag, that's a negotiating number.

**5/**
Under [[dl-67-2023|DL 67/2023]], defects like these are only leverage if they're found and documented before you sign — not discovered six months after moving in, when the clock has already started running against you.

**6/**
The buyers who win aren't the ones who skip the pretty flip listings. They're the ones who walk in with someone who knows exactly where to look — and turns what they find into money off the price, not a nasty surprise after closing.
