# Thread — "Legally occupied doesn't mean structurally safe"

**1/**
Here's something most buyers assume without ever checking: if a building is legally occupied in Portugal, it must be structurally sound. That assumption is wrong for a huge slice of the housing stock, and almost nobody tells buyers why.

**2/**
The law only requires a seismic-safety assessment for buildings over 28m, hospitals, schools, and large residential complexes. Thousands of ordinary 1970s–80s apartment blocks fall below that line. No one is ever required to check them — not the owner, not the state, not you, unless you ask.

**3/**
That open, airy ground-floor café or shop under an older apartment block? It's often there because structural walls were removed to open up the retail space — the exact renovation pattern that creates the highest-risk failure mode in an earthquake.

**4/**
This isn't about scaring you off older Lisbon or Porto buildings — most are fine, most people never experience the worst case. It's about the fact that this specific risk is invisible on a listing, invisible in a standard survey, and only shows up if someone is specifically looking for it.

**5/**
The honest version of due diligence on pre-1980s construction isn't "does it have a habitation license." It's a five-minute visual check for diagonal cracks, exposed rebar, and structural walls that look like they used to be somewhere else. That's the actual question worth asking before you fall in love with a building.

**6/**
None of this shows up in a property's certification paperwork. It only shows up if someone who knows what a soft-story failure looks like walks the building with you before you sign anything.
