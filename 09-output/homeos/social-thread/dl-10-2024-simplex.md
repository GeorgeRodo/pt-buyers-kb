# Thread — "The government used to check this. It doesn't anymore."

**1/**
If you're eyeing a new-build in Portugal, here's something most buyers never find out until it's too late: since 2024, the municipality no longer technically inspects new construction. The developer's own engineers sign off on their own work.

**2/**
That's not a small procedural shift. It means the one independent check that used to exist between "what was promised in the brochure" and "what actually got poured and wired" — is gone, unless you personally arrange for one.

**3/**
This is exactly the gap where corners get cut quietly: a cheaper fire-rated material swapped in, an acoustic spec certified on paper but never actually built to. Nobody catches it, because nobody's required to look.

**4/**
And it's compounding: a newer law taking effect August 2026 removes even the paperwork check at the deed stage — so the documentary trail buyers used to lean on for reassurance is thinning too.

**5/**
The asymmetry here is simple: the seller and developer know exactly what was and wasn't done. You, the buyer, don't — unless you bring in someone whose only job is to find out before you sign, not after you've moved in.

**6/**
This is the actual reason "just get an inspection" stopped being optional advice for new-builds and started being the only advice that matters.
