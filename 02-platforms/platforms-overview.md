# Platforms overview

Two platforms monetize the buyer-side ecosystem. They are **views onto the same decomposition**, not separate businesses with separate knowledge: the same defect page, the same legal page, the same regional dossier serves both. That shared knowledge base is this wiki.

| Platform | Journey coverage | Revenue model | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[platform-buyers-agency\|Buyer's agency]] | Phases 1–8 (full span) | % fee or fixed fee per mandate; ancillary services | Defined — playbook is the operating manual |
| [[platform-home-inspection\|Home inspection]] | Phases 3–4 core; 7–8 (pre-closing, snagging) | Per-inspection fees; tiered products; B2B (lawyers, agencies) | Defined — prompt-chain KB is the technical engine |

## How they reinforce each other

- **Shared funnel.** [[content-strategy|Content]] and [[lead-magnets|lead magnets]] built from the knowledge base feed all platforms; a buyer who downloads the Pre-CPCV Defense Kit is a lead for both agency and inspection.
- **Cross-sell along the journey.** Agency clients need inspections (P3–4, 7–8); inspection-only clients discover they need representation (P6 negotiation with the report in hand).
- **Shared authority position.** One brand thesis: *the only side of the table that works for the buyer.* Every platform inherits it.
- **Shared B2B channel.** [[b2b-partnerships|Lawyers and premium agencies]] buy inspection reports as litigation evidence and refer agency mandates.

## Design rule

New knowledge is filed once, in the domain layer, then linked from platform pages — never duplicated per platform. If a platform needs a client-facing rendering of a page (checklist PDF, script, SOP), that rendering lives in [[sops|SOPs]] or [[lead-magnets|lead magnets]] and links back to the canonical page.
