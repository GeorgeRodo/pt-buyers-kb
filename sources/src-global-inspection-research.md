# Source — Global home inspection market research

**Raw files:** `Global_Home_Inspection_Service_Workflow.md` (workflow architecture) · `global_home_inspection_report.md` (94KB — the primary document: an 8-market strategic research report) · `global_home_inspection_reports_inventory.md` (a 30+-entry bibliography of sample inspection reports from 10+ countries, with URLs — a resource list for report-template design, catalogued here but not individually extracted) · **Ingested:** 2026-07-20.

## What it is

Market research benchmarking the global home-inspection industry ($5.2B, 2024 estimate) across pre-purchase, snagging, mold, and structural/seismic inspection types in the US, UK, Australia, Canada, Northern Europe, Southern Europe (Portugal, Spain), Japan, and UAE — built explicitly to inform InspectOS's operational and pricing design, not as neutral research.

## Core claims

- **Pricing benchmarks across 9 regions** for the four inspection types, e.g. US $350–$700+ (region-dependent), UK RICS L2 £400–700/L3 £600–1,500+, Australia AUD $350–1,000+, Portugal €470–800 base / €800–1,200 combined, Spain €500–1,500. Snagging separately: UK £300–600, Dubai $400–2,200, Portugal "starting at €980" (cited as InspectOS's own published price — yet another data point in the pricing-reconciliation problem flagged on [[inspectos-pca-pcs-strategy]]).
- **Snagging is called the highest-margin, fastest-growing global segment** — driven by new-construction booms in UAE, UK, Australia, and Portugal's own Golden-Visa-era new-build corridor (Algarve, Lisbon coast).
- **Japan's seismic evaluation framework (JBDPA Is-index)** is presented as the most mature globally: a numerical seismic-capacity score (≥1.5 safe, 0.7–1.0 possible collapse, <0.7 high collapse risk), validated by the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake — pre-1981 buildings (before Japan's 1981 "shin-taishin" code revision) overwhelmingly failed; post-1981 buildings overwhelmingly survived. Government subsidies cover 50–80% of retrofit costs. Directly relevant to [[seismic-risk]]'s existing RAVS-gap argument — a stronger regulatory contrast to cite.
- **Structural warranty periods by jurisdiction**: UK 10 years (NHBC), Dubai 10 years (major defects), Australia 6yr3mo–6yr, **Portugal 5 years structural / 2 years systems (Decreto-Lei 122/90)** — this Portuguese figure conflicts with [[dl-108-2026]]'s account of DL 122/2021 extending the structural-defect guarantee to 10 years; the two decrees may not be describing the same regime (122/90 vs 122/2021), but this needs checking before either number is used, not silently reconciled.
- **Market-entry prioritization explicitly ranks Portugal #1** of six evaluated markets — low regulatory barrier (no licensing regime), high demand growth (Golden Visa era + DL 10/2024 compliance pressure), low competition — ahead of Spain (#2), UK (#3), Australia (#4), unlicensed US states (#5), with Japan as a seismic-assessment niche play for international buyers.
- **Business model innovations**: pay-at-closing (reduces buyer friction, requires title-company partnership), subscription/retainer models for ongoing monitoring, agent partnership programs (with an explicit ethical caveat: US association codes like InterNACHI prohibit referral fees to agents — worth checking whether an equivalent restriction exists or should be self-imposed in the Portuguese context), and franchise models.
- Introduces the term **"Property Condition Data Layer (PCDL)"** alongside PCS — not otherwise defined in this source or cross-referenced against [[inspectos-pca-pcs-strategy]]'s three-tier PCG/PCA/PCS terminology; flagged as a possible fourth term needing reconciliation if it recurs in future sources.

## Why this matters

Independent, non-InspectOS-authored validation (largely) of the Portugal market-entry thesis, plus genuinely new content: the Japan seismic-scoring benchmark, structural-warranty-period comparison table, and specialist-niche economics (mold, seismic, snagging) that the existing [[construction-defects-hub]] pages don't currently cite.
