## What it is

An impact assessment of **Decree-Law No. 108/2026**, published 29 May 2026, **entering into force 3 August 2026** — i.e. inside two weeks of this ingest date. It amends the RJUE (Regime Jurídico da Urbanização e Edificação) and is explicitly the *second, more aggressive phase* of the same "Simplex Urbanístico" reform this KB already tracks as [[dl-10-2024-simplex|DL 10/2024]] — not a contradicting law, an escalation of the same one.

## Core claims

- **Habitation license fully removed as a deed requirement.** The notary must instead declare in the deed whether an urban title exists, whether the seller claims it exists without presenting it, or whether the seller admits it doesn't exist (Art. 4 §14).
- **One-year buyer annulment window**: if a property is transacted without a proper urban title, the buyer can annul the sale, get full restitution, and pursue damages, within one year of the deed.
- **Solidary liability (republished Art. 100-A RJUE)**: promoter, property owner, project designer, and work director are all *jointly* liable for infractions on non-conforming urban operations — fines to €450,000 for legal entities, coercive demolition, and potential criminal charges (document falsification, Art. 256 Penal Code) for false *livro de obra* declarations.
- **Tacit approval generalized**: 20 days (building works) / 30 days (architectural project) / 45 days (subdivision) — municipal silence = approval. But "successive control" (1-year document audit window) is distinct from "administrative supervision" (*fiscalização administrativa*), which **never expires** — a tacitly-approved project can still be embargoed or demolished years later.
- **Strict "reconstruction" redefinition**: exempt from licensing only if it restores the exact prior state with **no increase in area, height, or volume**; anything more is an "extension" requiring full regularization.
- **Three-tier market stratification** emerging: Tier 1 "Gold Standard" (pre-1951 exemption certificate, or audited new builds) commands a 5–10% premium; Tier 2 "Simplex-approved" (unverified prior communications) takes a 3–8% discount; Tier 3 "Unregularized" (explicit no-title declaration) trades 20–40% below market, cash-only.
- **Banks as de facto regulators**: BdP confirms banks can still demand habitation-license-equivalent documentation for mortgage risk assessment even though the law no longer requires it at the deed — creating a credit bottleneck that offsets the law's stated velocity goal. BdP is separately tightening DSTI from 50% to 45%.
- **Condominium downstream liability** (new to this KB): under Law 8/2022 and a 2007 Tribunal da Relação do Porto precedent, buyers of apartment units are NOT liable for common-part works approved before their purchase, but ARE liable (quota-share) for ongoing conservation, urgent repairs, and municipal restoration orders on common-part non-compliance discovered after purchase — including modifications the buyer had no way to detect pre-sale. This is an *obrigação propter rem* (Art. 408 CC) that follows the property regardless of who caused it.
- **PEPU platform gap**: the digital backbone mandatory for all 308 municipalities since 5 Jan 2026 is, per named practitioners cited in the source, "difusa... parte digital, parte analógica, parte informal" — the verification burden the law shifts to the private sector has no reliable digital infrastructure to run on yet.
- **Interacts with DL 122/2021's 10-year building-defect guarantee**: properties sold under the new tacit-approval framework that later reveal defects can trigger decade-long seller liability windows.
- **Quantified baseline**: IMPIC complaints against mediation agencies quadrupled 549 (2021) → 2,847 (2025); agent professional liability insurance minimum frozen at €150,000 since 2013 despite median property values roughly tripling; ~60% of PT transactions are already cash-only.
- **International analogues cited as probability anchors**: Spain's SAREB (63% avg. discount on impaired assets), Ireland's NAMA (37% residual NPL ratio even after mass loan transfer), UK's Grenfell/Building Safety Act 2022 (cascading multi-party construction liability).
- **New 4-phase DD compliance protocol** proposed for agents/brokers: (1) documentary baseline — caderneta vs. physical reality, certidão permanente; (2) DL 108/2026-specific — pre-1951 exemption certificate, strict reconstruction-definition check, comunicação prévia + fee receipt + termo de responsabilidade, PEPU status; (3) stakeholder vetting — contractor IMPIC *Alvará* status, insolvency screening; (4) foreign-buyer-specific — translated compliance summary, independent (non-agency) legal counsel, written acknowledgment.

## Why this matters to the thesis

This is not adjacent content — it is the sharpest confirmation yet of the KB's founding information-asymmetry thesis ([[inspection-in-the-buyer-journey]], [[municipal-variance-and-the-thesis]]). DL 10/2024 removed the state's technical inspection function; DL 108/2026 removes the state's *documentary* verification function at the point of sale too, while keeping its policing power indefinitely exercisable. The gap between "state stopped checking" and "state can still punish you" is exactly the gap both platforms monetize.
