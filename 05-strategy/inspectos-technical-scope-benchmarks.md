# What inspections technically include — five-market benchmark

A comparison of how the UK, France, Spain, the Netherlands, and Germany structure property condition inspection, used to argue Portugal has no equivalent unit-level mandatory inspection regime and to design InspectOS's own service-tier scope.

## UK — RICS Home Survey Standard (the benchmark)

Three graduated levels: **Level 1 (Condition Report)** — ~1 hour, visual/cursory only, one window per elevation, no fabric testing, 10–15 page report, £300–500 (€360–600). **Level 2 (HomeBuyer Report)** — 2–4 hours, non-invasive but thorough, roof-space entry, explicit "risk of hidden defects" statement, 20–30 pages, £400–800 (€480–960) — this is the level InspectOS's current €595 standard tier maps to. **Level 3 (Building Survey)** — 4–8 hours, investigative and invasive where safe: lifting floor coverings, lifting roof insulation, opening all windows, lifting drainage chamber covers, observing services in normal operation; 40–100 pages, £600–1,500 (€720–1,800). The traffic-light (Green/Amber/Red) condition-rating system and the explicit "risk of hidden defects" transparency statement at Level 2 are flagged as worth adopting directly.

## France — Dossier de Diagnostic Technique (DDT)

The only mandatory, **seller-funded** comprehensive diagnostic regime in Europe — up to eleven separate diagnostics (energy, asbestos, lead, electrical, gas, natural-risk zones, termites, septic conformity, building survey for condo units) bundled and required before the *compromis de vente*, technicians COFRAC-accredited and capped at 1,000 inspections/year for quality control. Costs €300–700, non-negotiable. The strategic read: mandatory seller-funded diagnostics create market pull without buyer friction — directly validating InspectOS's own bundled "Compliance Package" approach (pre-purchase + EPC + gas inspection at a discount).

## Spain — ITE (Inspección Técnica de Edificios)

A mandatory *periodic* inspection triggered by building age (20–80 years depending on autonomous community) but critically evaluates the **building block, not the individual unit** — structure, façades, roofing, common drainage only. No mandatory pre-purchase unit-level inspection exists in Spain at all; a buyer still needs a separate private valuation or inspection for their specific apartment. This is read as validating that Portugal's DL 10/2024 liability shift creates exactly the gap ITE-style regimes don't close.

## Netherlands — Bouwkundige Keuring

Not legally mandatory but *practically* mandatory through mortgage conditions: NHG and most banks require a building report if deferred maintenance exceeds 10% of market value, or standardly for pre-1970 homes regardless of visible condition. €400–600. The read: lender-driven demand is a more powerful adoption mechanism than regulatory mandate — this is the evidentiary basis for InspectOS's own bank/insurer-integration strategy in [[inspectos-pca-pcs-strategy]].

## Germany — Baugutachten

No unified national standard, but civil-engineer-led "Baugutachten" reports are the de facto standard for serious buyers, with particular depth on moisture/mold (*Feuchtigkeit*/*Schimmel*) and soil/foundation analysis. €800–2,500+. Read as validating an engineer-led (not generalist-surveyor-led) positioning — directly parallel to the OE-registration requirement already central to this KB's [[hiring-quality-gates|inspector-vetting]] pipeline.

## The eight-dimension scope gap for Portugal

Structure (Portugal: visual crack assessment only, vs. full foundation/settlement analysis elsewhere), roof (visual-only vs. roof-space entry and insulation assessment), envelope (visual vs. thermal-bridge and moisture mapping), MEP (visual condition vs. operational testing), drainage (no testing at all in Portugal vs. chamber-lifting and flow observation), energy (static SCE certificate vs. EPC accuracy verification), mold/moisture (visual-only vs. proactive mapping and air sampling), and life safety (not assessed in Portugal at all vs. fire/egress/emergency-lighting checklists elsewhere). Each gap is framed as a specific tier-differentiation opportunity for InspectOS's Standard/Premium service levels.
