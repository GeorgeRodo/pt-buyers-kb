# ISO/IEC 17020:2026 accreditation — the structural moat

Without formal accreditation, a property inspection is legally just an engineer's individual opinion — challengeable in court, ignorable by regulators, and indistinguishable in apparent authority from a competitor's contradictory report. IPAC-accredited **Type A inspection body** status changes what the report structurally *is*: an internationally recognized conformity assessment, auditable and reproducible, carrying the same evidentiary framework that underpins pharmaceutical testing and construction product certification across the EU (via IPAC's ILAC/EA mutual-recognition signatory status).

## Why Type A, specifically

ISO/IEC 17020:2026 defines three inspection-body types by independence level: **Type A** (fully independent of all parties with interests in what's inspected — required), Type B (serves only its parent organization), Type C (organizationally tied to inspected items). Type A is not a preference here but a requirement: IMPIC cannot mandate a report produced by a body with a commercial interest in the outcome — that's legally and politically untenable — and civil courts weight an accredited body's auditable methodology structurally higher than an individual engineer's opinion.

## The nine-clause gap, ranked by closure speed

| Clause | Requirement | Est. closure time |
|---|---|---|
| 7.5–7.6 Complaints & appeals | Documented process, independent review | 1 day |
| 7.3 Inspection records | Retention policy, retrievability, traceability | 1 week |
| 7.4 Inspection reports | Standardized content, unique ID, signature | 1 week |
| 4.1 Impartiality | Risk register, top-management commitment | 1 week |
| 5 Structural requirements | Legal entity, org chart | 1–2 weeks |
| 8 Management system | Full Quality Manual and control procedures | 4–6 weeks |
| 7.1 Inspection methods | Documented per-system procedures | 2–4 weeks |
| 6.2 Facilities & equipment | Calibrated equipment register | 4–8 weeks |
| 6.1 Personnel competence | Hiring, training, 50 supervised inspections/inspector | 6–12 months |

Personnel competence is the critical path — every other gap is a documentation problem; competence requires actual people and time. An internal self-assessment estimated the current Portuguese inspection market sits at roughly **31% average compliance** against the full standard, with the widest gaps in complaints/appeals (~85% gap) and inspection records (~80% gap) — not a criticism of individual engineers, but evidence that no informal practitioner can maintain a calibrated-equipment register, an impartiality risk process, and a documented complaints panel alone; it requires organizational infrastructure that doesn't yet exist for residential inspection in Portugal.

## The IPAC pathway (five phases, ~15 months)

Preparation (Months 1–3: gap analysis, legal entity formation) → documentation (Months 4–6: quality manual, procedures, competency framework) → implementation (Months 7–10: inspector training, pilot inspections, internal audit) → IPAC assessment (Months 11–14: DIC002/DIC004 application, document review, on-site witness audits — assessors observe live inspections to confirm inspectors actually follow documented procedure, not just that procedure exists on paper) → accreditation (Month 15+: nonconformity closure, certificate issued, annual surveillance, four-year cycle).

## What accreditation unlocks (the actual argument)

| Stakeholder | Without accreditation | With IPAC accreditation |
|---|---|---|
| IMPIC | Can suggest PCA as good practice | Can mandate "a certified PCA" in regulation without naming InspectOS |
| Civil courts | One expert opinion among others | Structured evidentiary weight — scope and methodology are auditable |
| Banks | Cannot integrate into underwriting without internal validation | Can cite accredited-body status directly in credit policy |
| Insurers | Commercial product from a startup | Data actuaries can reference in product filings |
| Foreign buyers | Trust varies by individual inspector | ILAC MRA recognition — UK/German/French buyers recognize the framework immediately |

The load-bearing insight: IMPIC mandating a specific company's product triggers procurement and competition law. IMPIC mandating "assessment by an IPAC-accredited Type A inspection body" doesn't — and if InspectOS is the only such accredited body for residential property condition assessment, it becomes the de facto mandatory provider without ever being named in the regulation. No current Portuguese competitor (Bureau Veritas, Dekra, ISQ, individual OE engineers) holds this accreditation for residential scope; accreditation cannot be shortcut below ~15 months, and legacy 2012-standard holders face their own mandatory transition to the 2026 edition (deadline ~March 2029) before they can even start pursuing a new scope — a structural head start for a competitor building natively to the 2026 standard from day one.
