# The InspectOS talent market

The addressable talent pool is not "civil engineers" — it's specifically **OE-registered civil engineers with active status and signing authority**, since only they (or OA-registered architects) can issue court-admissible inspection reports. Portugal's Ordem dos Engenheiros (OE) has 64,261 total members, of whom 30,499 are civil engineers (47.3% of membership) — this is the whole addressable pool, not a filtered subset of it.

## Structural shortage

Unemployment sits at a historic low of 6.4% (2024), engineering occupies Portugal's official shortage-occupations list, and CEDEFOP flags engineering professionals as a priority shortage across the Norte, Centro, and Alentejo regions — driven mainly by retirements outpacing graduate supply, not by graduate volume itself (Portugal ranks 2nd in the EU for engineering-graduate share of higher education). Talent bleeds out through three corridors: Madrid (+20–30% salary, no language friction, mid-career PMs), Amsterdam/Rotterdam (+40–50%, offshore wind specialists), and London (+60–80%, executive tier). The practical implication for InspectOS: hire now, before energy-sector demand (REN's grid investment, offshore wind projects) absorbs the passive candidate pool further.

## The Brazilian pipeline (the real advantage)

The **CREA-to-OE reciprocity pathway** (Termo de Reciprocidade, signed 2015, supplemented February 2025) lets Brazilian engineers with active CREA registration and 3,600+ coursework hours transfer to OE membership **without diploma revalidation** — a process the standard foreign-degree-recognition route would otherwise price at €2,000–€5,000 and a year-plus. CONFEA processes in up to 90 days for a R$ 59.42 (~€11) fee; total end-to-end CONFEA→OEP admission runs 90–180 days. With ~500,000 Brazilians already resident in Portugal, this is an operational channel most Portuguese employers underuse — InspectOS's stated strategy is to lead with it as a differentiated sourcing channel rather than treat it as a fallback.

Tecnólogos, técnicos, geólogos, geógrafos, and meteorologistas are explicitly excluded from the reciprocity agreement — only engineering degrees qualify.

## The Angolan pipeline (different shape)

Angola has no CONFEA-equivalent reciprocity agreement with OEP; Angolan engineers face the standard DGES degree-recognition process (Decreto-Lei 66/2018), 6–12+ months and €2,000–€5,000. The practical route InspectOS uses instead: Angolan site engineers with deep practical experience (concrete pathology, structural assessment, tropical-climate defects) work as **contractor support under the direct supervision and signing authority of an OE-registered senior inspector** — they contribute field observation and documentation; the OE-registered senior retains legal responsibility and signs.

## Compensation benchmarks (2025–2026 market data)

| Profile | Lisbon | Porto |
|---|---|---|
| Junior civil engineer (0–3 yrs) | €18,000–€24,000 | €16,000–€22,000 |
| Mid-level (3–7 yrs) | €24,000–€35,000 | €22,000–€32,000 |
| Senior with OE signing authority (7+ yrs) | €35,000–€50,000 | €32,000–€45,000 |
| Freelance day rate, standard inspection | €250–€350 | — |

InspectOS's own per-inspection pricing (€595 pre-purchase, €650+ diagnostic) sets the ceiling contractors can be paid while the platform stays margin-healthy: a contractor at €300/day doing 12–15 inspections/month grosses €3,600–€4,500/month, competitive with senior full-time pay at greater schedule autonomy.

## Competitive landscape

Direct inspection competitors: ISQ (Portugal's largest inspection/certification body, hundreds of engineers, institutional client relationships), InspectOne (newer residential-inspection platform), and hundreds of individual OE-registered sole practitioners at €150–€500/inspection. Indirect talent competitors pulling from the same OE pool: Teixeira Duarte (~2,500 staff in Portugal), Mota-Engil (top-25 European construction group), and the energy utilities REN/EDP/Galp, which have raised specialist compensation 22–25% after losing engineers to Madrid. InspectOS's counter-positioning: schedule autonomy, local-only travel, lower stress, technology exposure, and equity participation — things a large employer structurally can't offer.

## Geographic priority

Lisbon and Porto are treated as **equal priority** (P1), not capital-first — FEUP alone enrolls 8,000+ students across 63 engineering programs, giving Porto a talent-rich, lower-compensation-expectation market. Coimbra and Braga/Aveiro are P2 (university pipeline); Algarve is P3 (seasonal); Madeira/Azores are P4 (future expansion).
