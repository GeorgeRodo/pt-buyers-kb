# Onboarding & retention

A four-phase, 90-day onboarding journey designed to bring four different candidate profiles — OE veterans, Brazilian transfers, Angolan support engineers, and fresh graduates — to a common inspection-competence standard, plus a retention system built around career progression rather than compensation alone.

## The 90-day journey

**Phase 1 — Immersion (Days 1–14):** compliance and tools (OE insurance verification, platform training, report-template walkthrough), then shadowing two different senior inspectors across 6 inspections total. A 10-question shadow evaluation, scored 1–5, must average ≥4.0 to proceed. **Phase 2 — Supervised solo (Days 15–30):** the candidate leads 5 inspections while a senior observes; real-time feedback within 15 minutes, written feedback within 24 hours. **Phase 3 — Quality calibration (Days 31–60):** 10 independent inspections with 25% random audit; ≥80% required to maintain independent status. **Phase 4 — Independence assessment (Days 61–90):** 15+ total inspections, ≥85% quality audit average, ≥4.0/5.0 client satisfaction, zero substantiated complaints, and a signed-off career-path discussion — only then is full inspector status granted.

## Training curriculum (five modules)

InspectOS reporting standards and the 100+ checkpoint template; Portuguese building-code essentials (DL 10/2024, RTIEBT, DL 97/2017, DL 101-D/2020, RSEI, RCCTE); DGEG/ADENE energy certification (the pathway to inspectors issuing EPCs themselves — a high-margin add-on service at €150–€350/certificate, requiring 5+ years experience and an ADENE assessor qualification); client communication and de-escalation; and the digital tool stack (platform, Slack/WhatsApp, thermal camera, moisture meter, laser measure).

## Quality assurance loop

Random audit frequency scales down with seniority: 100% for trial/Phase 1–2 inspectors, 25% for newly independent (0–3 months), 15% for established (4–12 months), 10% for senior (12+ months). Any report scoring below 85% triggers peer review by a second senior inspector. Every client gets a 3-question post-inspection satisfaction survey feeding a per-inspector quality dashboard.

## Retention system

The career ladder is the primary retention lever, not compensation alone: Trainee/Intern → Junior Inspector (€24k–€28k FT or €150–€200/inspection) → Inspector (€28k–€35k, 12+ months) → Senior Inspector (€35k–€45k, 24+ months, ADENE-qualified) → Team Lead (€45k–€55k, manages 5+ inspectors) → Regional Manager (€55k–€70k, full P&L). This is layered with OE continuing-education sponsorship (mandatory for OE members to retain their title anyway, so the cost is largely unavoidable and might as well be a retention lever), recognition programs (Inspector of the Month, referral bonuses), and — for the distributed field team — weekly video standups, monthly in-person meetups, and a quarterly all-hands.

Target retention: 85% at 12 months for full-time employees, 70% at 12 months for contractors — both above the stated industry benchmarks (75–80% and 60–65% respectively).
