# Global inspection market benchmarks

An 8-market comparison (US, UK, Australia, Canada, Northern Europe, Southern Europe, Japan, UAE) used to benchmark InspectOS's pricing and specialist-service strategy against the mature end of the global inspection industry. Complements [[inspectos-technical-scope-benchmarks]] (which covers *inspection scope*, i.e. what's checked) with *pricing and business-model* benchmarks.

## Portugal ranks #1 for market entry

Of six evaluated markets, Portugal is explicitly ranked the highest-opportunity entry point: no licensing regime (low regulatory barrier), high demand growth from the Golden-Visa-era construction boom plus [[dl-10-2024-simplex|DL 10/2024]] compliance pressure, and low existing competition. Spain ranks second on similar logic (international-buyer corridor with a supply gap); the UK, Australia, and unlicensed US states rank lower due to higher regulatory barriers or established competition; Japan is a seismic-assessment niche play for international buyers rather than a general market.

## Pricing benchmarks (context, not a source of truth)

Pre-purchase inspection base pricing: US $350–700+ (region-dependent), UK RICS Level 2 £400–700, Australia AUD $350–1,000+, Portugal €470–800 base / €800–1,200 combined, Spain €500–1,500. **Snagging** is called out as the highest-margin, fastest-growing segment globally, tracking new-construction booms (UAE, UK, Australia, and Portugal's own Algarve/Lisbon new-build corridor) rather than resale transaction volume — this source cites InspectOS's own published snagging price as "starting at €980."

> [!warning] This is a fourth, non-matching data point in the pricing landscape already flagged unresolved on [[inspectos-pca-pcs-strategy]] (which lists three internally conflicting InspectOS pricing schemes). Do not treat any single figure across these sources as settled.

## Specialist niches and their economics

Mold assessment: health-driven demand (occupant symptoms, not transaction timelines), $300–600 visual/moisture-mapping rising to $800–1,500 with lab sampling, defensible through equipment investment and certification. Structural/seismic: the most technically demanding segment, 4–8 hours on-site, engineering-grade fees, and — where government retrofit mandates exist — a durable tailwind. Snagging: adversarial dynamic with developers rather than the collaborative agent relationship of pre-purchase work, but the highest per-hour pricing and a growth trajectory tied to construction pipelines rather than resale volume, with recurring-revenue potential across pre-handover, defects-liability-period, and warranty-expiration touchpoints.

## Japan's seismic evaluation standard — the strongest available international benchmark

The JBDPA (Japan Building Disaster Prevention Association) **Is-index** methodology produces a numerical seismic-capacity score: ≥1.5 = collapse unlikely, 1.0–1.5 = generally safe, 0.7–1.0 = possible collapse (partial retrofit required), below 0.7 = high collapse risk (comprehensive retrofit required). This was empirically validated by the 2011 Tōhoku earthquake: buildings built before Japan's 1981 "shin-taishin" seismic code revision overwhelmingly failed; post-1981 buildings overwhelmingly survived. Japan subsidizes 50–80% of retrofit costs for qualifying buildings. This is a materially stronger evidentiary anchor than anything currently cited on [[seismic-risk]] for the argument that pre-code buildings carry quantifiable, not just theoretical, collapse risk — worth citing there directly if the page is revised.

## Structural warranty periods by jurisdiction

UK 10 years (NHBC Buildmark), Dubai 10 years for major defects, Australia 6yr3mo (Queensland) to 6yr (NSW), **Portugal 5 years structural / 2 years systems per this source (citing Decreto-Lei 122/90)**.

> [!warning] This conflicts with [[dl-108-2026]]'s account of DL 122/2021 extending Portugal's structural-defect guarantee to 10 years (effective January 2022). The two decree numbers (122/90 vs. 122/2021) may simply be describing different, non-contradictory regimes at different points in time — but this hasn't been checked, and the two sources give materially different warranty-length answers to the same buyer-facing question. Needs reconciliation before either figure is used in client-facing content.
