# Why municipal variance doesn't threaten the founding thesis

**The question:** the venture rests on three national decrees — DL 118/2013 (mediation regime), DL 10/2024 (Simplex, end of state inspection), DL 67/2023 (hidden-defect liability). Given that Câmaras Municipais can introduce local variance (permitting timelines, heritage overlays, PDM zoning specifics — see e.g. [[property-type-historical|historical]] and [[property-type-land-development|land]] property-type pages), is the unified national framing overconfident?

**Paul's answer:** no, and for a reason that reframes the question rather than answering it head-on. The venture's thesis is not "these three decrees are the complete and sufficient explanation for the lack of buyer representation in Portugal." Paul has done the broader research and concludes there are *many* contributing reasons — cultural, structural, historical — and the three decrees are the sharpest, most citable, most legally actionable subset of that broader picture, not a claim to exhaustiveness. Municipal variance in permitting or zoning doesn't touch the core mechanism the business exploits: **information asymmetry**. A buyer without dedicated representation is disadvantaged regardless of which Câmara's PDM applies to their specific plot — the *mediador*'s statutory alignment with the seller (DL 118/2013), the absence of state technical inspection (DL 10/2024), and the tight defect-notification clock (DL 67/2023) all operate identically nationwide, on top of whatever local variance exists.

**Practical implication for the wiki:** regional/municipal variance is real domain content — it belongs in [[regions-hub|regional dossiers]] and property-type pages as *operational detail* (permit timelines, heritage overlays, zoning parameters). It should never be treated as a threat to or a caveat on the founding thesis in [[Home]] or [[platforms-overview]]. Don't hedge the thesis language with "except where municipal variance applies" — that confuses a business's causal claim (structural seller bias + asymmetry) with an operational fact (local permitting varies). If a future source suggests municipal-level buyer-protection variance strong enough to change deal economics in a specific region, file that as a regional-dossier update, not a thesis revision.
