# Why inspection is a subset of the buyer journey, not the frame

Paul's founding instruction: *"I wouldn't build it from home inspection — I would decompose the buyer's side, and home inspection is a part of that."* This page records why that decomposition is correct, because it determines the structure of everything else.

## The argument

1. **The pain is journey-shaped, not inspection-shaped.** A buyer's exposure runs from signing an adversarially-drafted BRA-less search (P1) to missing a 30-day defect deadline (P8). Inspection addresses only the technical-verification slice; framing the venture around it would blind the knowledge base to seven-eighths of the monetizable surface.
2. **Inspection value is realized in other phases.** A defect found in P4 is worthless until it becomes a price reduction in P6, a *reserva* at P8 handover, or evidence in litigation. The inspection report is an input to negotiation and law — phases the inspection frame doesn't contain.
3. **The platforms share one funnel.** Content, lead magnets, and B2B relationships serve agency and inspection simultaneously only if they hang off a common frame. That frame is the journey.
4. **Recurrence lives outside inspection.** The venture's dominant economics (5-year client value, referrals at 50–70% of mandates) sit in P8 relationship management — invisible from an inspection-first map.

## Where inspection actually plugs in

Four insertion points, each a distinct product: P3 viewing companion scan → P4 full forensic + NDT → P7 pre-closing re-verification → P8 snagging + *Perícia Extrajudicial*. See [[platform-home-inspection|the product ladder]].
