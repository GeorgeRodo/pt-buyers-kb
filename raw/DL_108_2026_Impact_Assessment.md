# **Impact Assessment of DL 108/2026 on Real Estate Liability and Market Dynamics**

## *Portugal Real Estate Systemic Risk Audit — The Verification-Shift Cascade*

---

**TL;DR:** Decree-Law No. 108/2026 does not simplify the Portuguese real estate market — it **privatizes its risk**. By eliminating mandatory habitation licenses at the deed, expanding tacit approvals, and shifting from *ex-ante* municipal control to *ex-post* administrative supervision, the State has structurally transferred the burden of compliance verification to sellers, agents, notaries, brokers, appraisers, and ultimately buyers. The quantified exposure is severe: **IMPIC complaints quadrupled from 549 (2021) to 2,847 (2025)**; **professional liability insurance for agents remains capped at €150,000 (unchanged since 2013)** despite median property values exceeding €310,000 for foreign buyers; and **approximately 60% of transactions are already cash-only**, a proportion that will rise as bank collateral requirements tighten. The litigation cascade will unfold in three phases: adaptation (2026-2027), friction shock (2027-2028), and potential cascade (2028-2030), with Tribunal da Relação precedents on *vícios ocultos* (Art. 913 CC) and solidary liability (Art. 100-A RJUE) providing the legal architecture for mass buyer recourse. The can-kicking pattern is clear: the State reduces its verification obligation, private parties absorb downstream risk, and the Bank of Portugal's tightening macroprudential framework (DSTI reduction to 45%, enhanced appraisal requirements) creates the **de facto regulatory bottleneck** that DL 108/2026 pretends to eliminate.

## **I. Legislative Context: The Can-Kicking Architecture**

### **1.1 From Simplex 2024 to DL 108/2026: The Pattern**

Decree-Law No. 108/2026, published on 29 May 2026 and effective from 3 August 2026, represents the second and more aggressive phase of Portugal's urban licensing reform under the *Construir Portugal* program axis  [(Bases Jurídico-Documentais)](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrc.nsf/c3fb530030ea1c61802568d9005cd5bb/cfea61fe0673c5dd80258d9b00546e7f?OpenDocument) . It amends the Regime Jurídico da Urbanização e Edificação (RJUE) and consolidates the *Simplex Urbanístico* initiated by DL 10/2024 in January 2024. The central innovation is the generalization of **tacit approval** (*deferimento tácito*): municipalities face truncated deadlines of 20 days for building works, 30 days for architectural project assessment, and 45 days for subdivision operations — and silence constitutes approval  [(belzuz.com)](https://belzuz.com/pt/publicacion/o-futuro-da-intermediacao-de-credito-em-portugal-uma-analise-a-revisao-do-rjic-anunciada-pelo-banco-de-portugal/) . The property can then be transacted via a notary deed without the historical safeguard of a municipal habitation license (*licença de utilização*). The State's logic is velocity: reduce bureaucratic friction, unlock illiquid assets, accelerate housing supply. The reality is displacement: **the friction removed from municipal desks reappears as due diligence burden across the entire transaction chain**  [(Arhivix | Vaša dokumentacija na dohvat ruke)](https://arhivix.com/pt/blog/pepu-livro-obra-digital-2026-construcao) .

The can-kicking pattern is not incidental — it is structural. The State created tolerance (Simplex 2024 expanded *comunicações prévias*), reinforced it (DL 108/2026 expanded tacit approvals), and deferred verification to private parties. Municipalities retain "successive control" for one year post-approval and "administrative supervision" (*fiscalização administrativa*) exercisable *a todo o tempo* — but these are *ex-post* mechanisms, not *ex-ante* filters  [(belzuz.com)](https://belzuz.com/pt/publicacion/o-futuro-da-intermediacao-de-credito-em-portugal-uma-analise-a-revisao-do-rjic-anunciada-pelo-banco-de-portugal/) . The buyer who discovers, six months post-purchase, that their "tacitly approved" extension violates the Municipal Master Plan (PDM) cannot appeal to the municipality for pre-transaction verification. Their recourse is civil litigation against the seller, the agent who marketed the property, and potentially the notary who executed the deed. The State has privatized the verification function while retaining the policing power — a classic regulatory asymmetry that concentrates risk in private hands and concentrates enforcement in public hands, with a temporal gap between transaction and intervention that guarantees accumulated exposure  [(Ricardo Gama - Advogado)](https://ricardogama.com/licenca-de-habitabilidade-quando-obrigatoria/) .

### **1.2 The One-Year Annulment Window: A Double-Edged Sword**

Article 4, paragraph 14 of DL 108/2026 mandates a new protocol for property transactions: the notary must explicitly declare in the deed whether the urban title exists, whether the seller declares its existence without presenting it, or whether the seller explicitly states they do not possess it  [(Bases Jurídico-Documentais)](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrc.nsf/c3fb530030ea1c61802568d9005cd5bb/cfea61fe0673c5dd80258d9b00546e7f?OpenDocument) . This procedural transparency is paired with a statutory one-year annulment window: if a property is transacted without the proper urban title, the buyer may annul the transaction within one year  [(Get Golden Visa)](https://getgoldenvisa.com/portugal-golden-visa-program) . On its face, this protects buyers. In practice, it creates a **one-year sword of Damocles** over sellers and a litigation invitation over the entire transaction chain. A buyer who discovers that their newly acquired property has unregularized works can demand annulment, restitution of the full purchase price, and potentially punitive damages — all within that first year. For developers and promoters, capital acquired from property sales cannot be considered secure revenue until this window closes  [(Bases Jurídico-Documentais)](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrc.nsf/c3fb530030ea1c61802568d9005cd5bb/cfea61fe0673c5dd80258d9b00546e7f?OpenDocument) .

The annulment right interacts destructively with the tacit approval mechanism. A developer relying on a *comunicação prévia* that received tacit approval may have marketed and sold units based on that approval. If the municipality exercises its successive control within the one-year window and discovers documentary non-conformity, the buyer gains an immediate annulment pathway. The developer faces simultaneous exposure: municipal fines (up to €450,000 for legal entities), potential demolition orders, and buyer annulment claims  [(Ricardo Gama - Advogado)](https://ricardogama.com/licenca-de-habitabilidade-quando-obrigatoria/) . This is not a theoretical risk — Tribunal da Relação jurisprudence has already established that the absence of legal compliance constitutes a fundamental "defect" under the *venda de coisa defeituosa* regime of Article 913 of the Civil Code  [(CNN Portugal)](https://cnnportugal.iol.pt/governo/habitacao/governo-justifica-dispensa-de-licenca-na-escritura-com-controlo-previo/20240202/65bc9c86d34e65afa2fa577e) .

### **1.3 Solidary Liability: The Privatization of Enforcement**

To counterbalance the relaxation of *ex-ante* controls, DL 108/2026 introduces a draconian expansion of solidary liability through republished Article 100-A of the RJUE  [(Ricardo Gama - Advogado)](https://ricardogama.com/licenca-de-habitabilidade-quando-obrigatoria/) . Under the new regime, **promoters, property owners (*donos da obra*), technical project designers, and work directors are all solidarily responsible** for infractions committed during urban operations executed without requisite titles or in material non-conformity. The Portuguese construction sector's well-documented volatility — characterized by high corporate insolvency rates and persistent "phoenix companies" that accumulate debt, dissolve, and re-emerge under new registrations — makes this solidary liability particularly dangerous  [(Ricardo Gama - Advogado)](https://ricardogama.com/licenca-de-habitabilidade-quando-obrigatoria/) . When a contractor deviates from approved plans to reduce material costs and then dissolves, the municipality will enforce embargoes and fines against the most solvent party available: almost always the property owner or institutional promoter. Fines reach **€450,000 for legal entities**, coercive demolition may be ordered, and criminal charges (crime of disobedience, document falsification under Article 256 of the Penal Code) become possible  [(Ricardo Gama - Advogado)](https://ricardogama.com/licenca-de-habitabilidade-quando-obrigatoria/) .

## **II. Stakeholder Liability & Risk Mapping**

### **2.1 The Seller (Promotor/Owner)**

#### **Civil Liability Under the Vícios Ocultos Regime**

The removal of the habitation license as a deed requirement fundamentally alters the seller's exposure under Article 913 of the Civil Code (*venda de coisa defeituosa*). Portuguese jurisprudence has consistently held that a "defect" in property is not limited to physical structural flaws — the absence of legal compliance that impairs the asset's intended economic utility constitutes a fundamental defect  [(CNN Portugal)](https://cnnportugal.iol.pt/governo/habitacao/governo-justifica-dispensa-de-licenca-na-escritura-com-controlo-previo/20240202/65bc9c86d34e65afa2fa577e) . The Tribunal da Relação de Coimbra has established that sellers who "deliberately oculted" illegalities in construction can be held liable for *dolo* (fraud) under Article 253 CC, triggering annulment rights and indemnification claims  [(stj.pt)](https://juris.stj.pt/2111%2F21.0T8STB.E1.S1/Z4jXueDBWDzgrxNCz1D7bKAiL20?search=k_t06Ks2i5E3zej8Z5M) . Critically, the seller bears a **presumption of culpability** under Article 914 CC: the seller must prove they did not know and could not have known about the defect without culpa. This presumption is difficult to rebut when the seller was the sole inhabitant and proprietor of the property, as courts consistently presume their knowledge of existing conditions  [(Bases Jurídico-Documentais)](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrc.nsf/8fe0e606d8f56b22802576c0005637dc/87d171b98847b19a80258d42004b3577?OpenDocument) .

The causal link standard operates on two levels. For **known defects**, strict liability applies — the seller who knowingly conceals unregularized works or structural non-compliance commits *dolo* and faces full liability for reparation, price reduction, or contract resolution  [(stj.pt)](https://juris.stj.pt/2111%2F21.0T8STB.E1.S1/Z4jXueDBWDzgrxNCz1D7bKAiL20?search=k_t06Ks2i5E3zej8Z5M) . For **constructive knowledge** ("should have known through reasonable verification"), the standard is more nuanced. The courts apply the *bonus pater familias* test: would a reasonably diligent seller have discovered the defect? In the context of DL 108/2026, this test becomes more demanding because the seller can no longer rely on municipal documentation as a proxy for compliance. A seller who failed to verify whether recent "reconstruction" works actually complied with the strict new definition (no increase in area, height, or volume) may be found negligent even if they had no actual knowledge of the non-compliance  [(Tribunal da Relação de Coimbra)](https://trc.pt/venda-civil-de-coisa-defeituosa-anomalias-em-imovel-vendido-no-estado-de-usado-onus-da-prova-do-defeito-presuncao-de-culpa-do-vendedor-defeito-oculto-responsabilidade-do-mediador-imobiliario/) .

#### **Tacit Approval Vulnerabilities and Financial Exposure**

The bifurcated municipal oversight structure creates a complex temporal matrix of liability. Under successive control, the municipality has one year from fee payment or building use commencement to verify documentary conformity. If this window lapses without audit, the municipality loses its right to invalidate the project based on paperwork alone  [(belzuz.com)](https://belzuz.com/pt/publicacion/o-futuro-da-intermediacao-de-credito-em-portugal-uma-analise-a-revisao-do-rjic-anunciada-pelo-banco-de-portugal/) . However, administrative supervision — physical inspection of construction site conformity with law and submitted projects — **never expires** and can be exercised at any time  [(belzuz.com)](https://belzuz.com/pt/publicacion/o-futuro-da-intermediacao-de-credito-em-portugal-uma-analise-a-revisao-do-rjic-anunciada-pelo-banco-de-portugal/) . A developer relying on a tacitly approved *comunicação prévia* may therefore face embargo, coercive demolition, or severe financial sanctions years after project completion, during physical supervision. The timeframe for administrative declaration of nullity of licensing acts has been compressed to three years (unless the infraction constitutes a crime), creating overlapping and potentially conflicting limitation periods  [(belzuz.com)](https://belzuz.com/pt/publicacion/o-futuro-da-intermediacao-de-credito-em-portugal-uma-analise-a-revisao-do-rjic-anunciada-pelo-banco-de-portugal/) .

The financial exposure is quantifiable and severe. Corporate fines reach €450,000. Coercive demolition orders require the owner to bear full demolition and restoration costs. Criminal charges for executing works without urban titles or proceeding with embargoed construction convert administrative infractions into penal consequences under the crime of disobedience  [(Ricardo Gama - Advogado)](https://ricardogama.com/licenca-de-habitabilidade-quando-obrigatoria/) . Making false declarations in the logbook (*livro de obra*) or terms of responsibility constitutes document falsification under Article 256 of the Penal Code  [(Ricardo Gama - Advogado)](https://ricardogama.com/licenca-de-habitabilidade-quando-obrigatoria/) . The buyer who acquires such a property and subsequently faces municipal enforcement action has clear recourse against the seller under the annulment window or *vícios ocultos* regime — but only if the discovery occurs within the applicable limitation periods.

#### **Building Defect Guarantee Regime (Decree-Law 122/2021)**

A critical but underappreciated dimension of seller liability is the guarantee regime established by Decree-Law 122/2021, which transposed EU directives on consumer protection in property sales and extended the guarantee period for structural defects from 5 to **10 years** (effective January 2022)  [(A Caixa)](https://www.cgd.pt/Site/Saldo-Positivo/casa-e-familia/Pages/casa-nova-com-defeitos.aspx?srsltid=AfmBOopFZYrVMmJ91S-JR5xydmbEo24wIm9nGWv7Ifjjuscia9NlYpW7) . This guarantee applies not only to new properties but also to used properties and goods/services supplied under construction contracts. The buyer must communicate the defect to the seller within the guarantee period, and judicial action must be initiated within 3 years of that communication  [(Prado Condomínios)](https://pradocondominios.com/defeitos-de-construcao-saiba-tudo-sobre-as-garantia-a-que-tem-direito/) . DL 108/2026 interacts with this guarantee regime in a potentially explosive way: properties sold under the new tacit approval framework that subsequently reveal construction defects or non-compliance may trigger the 10-year guarantee, exposing sellers to decade-long liability windows. The guarantee "triggers more frequently when tacit approvals fail" because the absence of municipal verification increases the probability that construction deviations go undetected until post-purchase discovery  [(A Caixa)](https://www.cgd.pt/Site/Saldo-Positivo/casa-e-familia/Pages/casa-nova-com-defeitos.aspx?srsltid=AfmBOopFZYrVMmJ91S-JR5xydmbEo24wIm9nGWv7Ifjjuscia9NlYpW7) .

#### **Risk Mitigation and Emerging Insurance Products**

Sellers' primary mitigation strategy is **pre-sale due diligence**. Platforms like ObraXRAY and ISQ's technical inspection services offer documentary and physical verification of construction compliance, creating a potential "disclosed = defended" shield  [(ISQ)](https://www.isq.pt/servicos/inspecoes-tecnicas/edificios/inspeccao-e-diagnostico-imobiliario/) . However, Portuguese law does not automatically absolve a seller who discloses defects — disclosure negates *dolo* but may not negate negligence if the disclosure was incomplete or if the seller failed to obtain proper technical verification. Representations and warranties (R&W) insurance for residential transactions remains underdeveloped in Portugal compared to Anglo-Saxon markets, though the DL 108/2026 regime may catalyze demand. Sellers can also use escrow mechanisms, retaining a percentage of sale proceeds for 12 months to cover potential annulment claims, and explicit contractual disclosures in the CPCV (*Contrato-Promessa de Compra e Venda*) documenting the presence or absence of urban titles  [(Bases Jurídico-Documentais)](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrc.nsf/c3fb530030ea1c61802568d9005cd5bb/cfea61fe0673c5dd80258d9b00546e7f?OpenDocument) .

### **2.2 The Real Estate Agent (Mediator)**

#### **The New "Reasonable Care" Standard**

For real estate mediation agencies, DL 108/2026 transforms property intake from a commercial marketing exercise into a **quasi-legal auditing process**. Historically, the physical presentation of a municipal habitation license functioned as a definitive state-sanctioned proxy for legality. Agents could confidently rely on the municipal stamp to assure buyers of structural soundness and legal compliance  [(wikipedia.org)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sareb) . With this safeguard removed, "reasonable due diligence" has been comprehensively redefined. An agent operating post-August 2026 cannot merely match the property address to the *Caderneta Predial Urbana* — they must proactively audit the physical and legal reality of the asset  [(irbnet.de)](https://www.irbnet.de/daten/iconda/CIB_DC31696.pdf) .

The specific verification requirements now include: (a) updated registry certificates (*caderneta predial*) cross-checked against physical reality; (b) physical vs. registered area matching; (c) architectural plan audit against the *último antecedente válido*; (d) IMPIC *Alvará* verification for construction companies involved in renovations; (e) *histórico de obras e autorizações* review; and (f) classification of any recent works as exempt "reconstruction" (strictly defined as restoration of the last valid antecedent state with **no increase in area, height, or volume**) or as illegal extension requiring regularization  [(spacelovers)](https://spacelovers.pt/novo-rjue-decreto-lei-108-2026/) . The operational cost of this compliance is substantial: additional time per listing (estimated 3-5 hours of verification work), technology investment for PEPU access and digital document management, training costs for agents on the new RJUE framework, and inevitable insurance premium increases  [(Arhivix | Vaša dokumentacija na dohvat ruke)](https://arhivix.com/pt/blog/pepu-livro-obra-digital-2026-construcao) .

#### **Professional Negligence Exposure and IMPIC Data**

The liability exposure is not theoretical. The Instituto dos Mercados Públicos, do Imobiliário e da Construção (IMPIC) — the regulatory body for real estate mediation — reported **2,847 complaints against mediation agencies in 2025**, a 32% year-on-year increase and more than a quadrupling from the 549 complaints recorded in 2021  [(idealista)](https://www.idealista.pt/news/imobiliario/habitacao/2026/03/12/74395-mediacao-imobiliaria-numero-de-queixas-dispara-e-atinge-maximo) . With over **11,500 registered agencies** now operating in Portugal, the complaint-to-agency ratio is rising, and the primary complaint categories (reservation payment disputes, agent service quality, CPCV non-compliance) are precisely the areas where DL 108/2026 will intensify exposure  [(idealista)](https://www.idealista.pt/news/imobiliario/habitacao/2026/03/12/74395-mediacao-imobiliaria-numero-de-queixas-dispara-e-atinge-maximo) . When an agent markets a property with severe illegal alterations that later triggers a municipal embargo or denies the buyer mortgage financing, the agent faces claims of misrepresentation, breach of fiduciary duty, and violation of the regulatory standards enforced by IMPIC under Law 15/2013  [(PÚBLICO)](https://www.publico.pt/2025/04/09/economia/noticia/bdp-propoe-proximo-governo-alteracoes-intermediacao-credito-2129158) .

Tribunal da Relação jurisprudence has already established that real estate agents are liable under **Article 17 of Law 15/2013** when they "assure qualities not held by the thing or conceal non-apparent defects of which they had knowledge"  [(Tribunal da Relação de Coimbra)](https://trc.pt/venda-civil-de-coisa-defeituosa-anomalias-em-imovel-vendido-no-estado-de-usado-onus-da-prova-do-defeito-presuncao-de-culpa-do-vendedor-defeito-oculto-responsabilidade-do-mediador-imobiliario/) . The agent's liability is contractual (arising from the mediation contract) and can be solidary with the seller's liability if both parties contributed to the buyer's detriment  [(stj.pt)](https://juris.stj.pt/2111%2F21.0T8STB.E1.S1/Z4jXueDBWDzgrxNCz1D7bKAiL20?search=k_t06Ks2i5E3zej8Z5M) . The agent's professional liability insurance — currently mandated at a minimum of **€150,000** — has been criticized as inadequate: Tiago Leal of PRA Raposo notes that this minimum "has not changed since 2013, which makes no sense in the current market, considering that prices have risen exponentially"  [(idealista)](https://www.idealista.pt/news/imobiliario/habitacao/2025/04/17/69107-agentes-imobiliarios-com-licenca-de-mediadoras-assim-funciona-o-mercado) . The Autoridade de Supervisão de Seguros (ASF) has already consulted on increasing the minimum capital for insurance mediators' professional liability by 4.03%  [(asf.com.pt)](https://www.asf.com.pt/documents/d/site-asf/relatorio-da-consulta-publica-n-3_2025) , but no comparable adjustment has been made for real estate mediators.

#### **Operational Friction: The Two-Tier Market**

DL 108/2026 is creating a **two-tier market** in property listings. Tier 1 consists of "clean" properties: pre-1951 stock with formal municipal exemption certificates, or fully documented new builds with independent compliance audits. These command liquidity and potentially a compliance premium. Tier 2 consists of "gray area" properties: post-Simplex stock with tacit approvals, pending successive control, or unregularized works. These face listing resistance, extended time-on-market, and cash-only buyer pools  [(PÚBLICO)](https://www.publico.pt/2025/04/09/economia/noticia/bdp-propoe-proximo-governo-alteracoes-intermediacao-credito-2129158) . RE/MAX Portugal president Manuel Alvarez acknowledges that his network already has **"hundreds" of listings explicitly requiring cash payment** due to lack of habitation licenses — a proportion that will grow as banks tighten collateral requirements  [(varandaseveredas.pt)](https://varandaseveredas.pt/pt/noticias/show/compra-de-casas-a-pronto-dispara-com-imoveis-sem-licenca-de-utilizacao_33/) .

The operational redesign of property intake requires agencies to implement a **mandatory DL 108/2026 Compliance Protocol** at three phases. Phase 1 (Documentary Baseline) verifies the *Caderneta Predial* against physical reality, reviews the *Certidão Permanente* for encumbrances, and checks for *averbamentos* of urban operations. Phase 2 (DL 108/2026 Specific Verification) obtains the *Certidão de Isenção* for pre-1951 properties, verifies reconstruction compliance against the strict new definition, collects *comunicação prévia* submission documents with fee payment receipts, and confirms the *Termo de Responsabilidade* signed by the architect. Phase 3 (Stakeholder Vetting) cross-references contractor NIF with the IMPIC portal for active *Alvará* status, screens for insolvency signals, and ensures the CPCV explicitly documents the presence or absence of urban titles  [(Bases Jurídico-Documentais)](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrc.nsf/c3fb530030ea1c61802568d9005cd5bb/cfea61fe0673c5dd80258d9b00546e7f?OpenDocument) .

### **2.3 The Notary (Notário)**

#### **The Execution Node Where Liability Materializes**

Notaries represent the critical execution node in the DL 108/2026 liability architecture. Historically, notaries relied on municipal documentation — specifically the habitation license — as a proxy for compliance verification. Article 1, paragraph 1 of the now-revoked Decree-Law 281/99 imposed on the notary the **duty to verify the existence of the habitation license**, and a deed executed without this license risked nullity  [(Bases Jurídico-Documentais)](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrc.nsf/c3fb530030ea1c61802568d9005cd5bb/cfea61fe0673c5dd80258d9b00546e7f?OpenDocument) . DL 108/2026 removes this verification obligation but introduces a new requirement: the notary must explicitly declare in the deed whether the urban title exists, whether the seller claims it exists without presenting it, or whether the seller admits they do not possess it  [(Bases Jurídico-Documentais)](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrc.nsf/c3fb530030ea1c61802568d9005cd5bb/cfea61fe0673c5dd80258d9b00546e7f?OpenDocument) . This procedural shift transforms the notary from a compliance gatekeeper into a **risk disclosure conduit** — but the question of liability when that disclosure proves inadequate remains unresolved.

The Bastonário of the Ordem dos Notários, Jorge Batista da Silva, issued an early and prescient warning: the reform would trigger a **"corrida para alienar imóveis ilegais"** (race to sell illegal properties), flooding the market with unlicensed assets  [(CNN Portugal)](https://cnnportugal.iol.pt/governo/habitacao/governo-justifica-dispensa-de-licenca-na-escritura-com-controlo-previo/20240202/65bc9c86d34e65afa2fa577e) . His concern was that the removal of the license requirement would incentivize owners of non-compliant properties to rush sales before buyers become fully aware of the risks. The Government's response — that "controlo prévio" mechanisms remain in place — does not address the notary's professional dilemma: they must now execute deeds on properties they know or suspect may lack proper urban titles, with only a procedural declaration to shield them  [(CNN Portugal)](https://cnnportugal.iol.pt/governo/habitacao/governo-justifica-dispensa-de-licenca-na-escritura-com-controlo-previo/20240202/65bc9c86d34e65afa2fa577e) .

#### **Professional Liability and the Tempo de Escritura**

The exposure of notarial professional indemnity insurance — mandatory under Article 89 of the Notary Statute  [(BDJUR - Base de Dados Jurídica Almedina)](http://bdjur.almedina.net/citem.php?field=item_id&value=2041878)  — is a critical but underexplored dimension. Are notaries now required to conduct independent verification beyond the seller's declaration, or can they rely entirely on seller/agent representations? The majority doctrinal view, cited in Tribunal da Relação jurisprudence, holds that the buyer is the "sociologically weaker party" in property transactions and requires protection through verification mechanisms  [(Bases Jurídico-Documentais)](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrc.nsf/c3fb530030ea1c61802568d9005cd5bb/cfea61fe0673c5dd80258d9b00546e7f?OpenDocument) . If notaries are deemed to have a duty to conduct at least minimal verification — checking PEPU for prior communications, requesting the *Termo de Responsabilidade*, or confirming fee payment receipts — the **tempo de escritura** (time to complete the deed) will inevitably lengthen, offsetting the Simplex velocity gains  [(Arhivix | Vaša dokumentacija na dohvat ruke)](https://arhivix.com/pt/blog/pepu-livro-obra-digital-2026-construcao) . If notaries limit themselves to procedural declarations without substantive verification, they risk malpractice claims when buyers discover post-purchase defects that a diligent notary "should have" flagged.

The Tribunal da Relação de Coimbra has already explored this tension, noting that Article 410 of the Civil Code (paragraph 3) still requires the habitation license for the *Contrato-Promessa de Compra e Venda* (CPCV) — the preliminary contract that precedes the deed. This creates a bifurcated regime: the deed no longer requires the license, but the CPCV still does  [(Bases Jurídico-Documentais)](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrc.nsf/c3fb530030ea1c61802568d9005cd5bb/cfea61fe0673c5dd80258d9b00546e7f?OpenDocument) . Notaries who execute deeds on properties where the CPCV lacked proper licensing documentation may face questions about whether they should have verified the chain of compliance from promise to execution. The professional indemnity framework of the Ordem dos Notários will face its first DL 108/2026 test cases within 12-18 months of the law's entry into force.

### **2.4 The Mortgage Broker & Financial Institutions**

#### **Banco de Portugal's De Facto Regulatory Bottleneck**

The Banco de Portugal (BdP) has emerged as the **de facto compliance gatekeeper** that DL 108/2026's drafters failed to anticipate. While the Simplex legislation legally permits mortgage deeds without habitation licenses, BdP has explicitly clarified that retail banks maintain the **absolute prerogative to demand these exact documents** for risk assessment purposes  [(BDJUR - Base de Dados Jurídica Almedina)](http://bdjur.almedina.net/citem.php?field=item_id&value=2041878) . This creates a paradox: the State removed the municipal requirement to accelerate transactions, but the banking supervisor's prudential mandates reintroduce substantively identical requirements through the back door. The result is not streamlined transactions but **displaced friction** — from the Câmara Municipal to the bank's compliance department  [(Arhivix | Vaša dokumentacija na dohvat ruke)](https://arhivix.com/pt/blog/pepu-livro-obra-digital-2026-construcao) .

BdP's 2026 regulatory tightening goes beyond collateral documentation. The supervisor is currently consulting banks on reducing the **Debt Service-to-Income (DSTI) ratio from 50% to 45%** for all new housing credit applicants  [(PÚBLICO)](https://www.publico.pt/2026/05/20/economia/noticia/bdp-consulta-bancos-reducao-taxa-esforco-credito-habitacao-45-2175386) . This macroprudential tightening, combined with the new Instrução 1/2026 on the Central de Responsabilidades de Crédito (which introduces enhanced reporting requirements including effective DSTI monitoring and credit intermediary tracking)  [(PLMJ)](https://www.plmj.com/pt/conhecimento/notas-informativas/Novo-regime-da-Central-de-Responsabilidades-de-Credito/34407/) , creates a more restrictive credit environment precisely when DL 108/2026 is supposed to unlock market velocity. The BdP's Novo Regime de Intermediação de Crédito (RJIC) revision, anticipated for 2026, will impose stricter transparency requirements (up to 5 comparable proposals), objective referral criteria, mandatory continuous training, and enhanced bank due diligence over intermediaries  [(belzuz.com)](https://belzuz.com/pt/publicacion/o-futuro-da-intermediacao-de-credito-em-portugal-uma-analise-a-revisao-do-rjic-anunciada-pelo-banco-de-portugal/) .

#### **Collateral Valuation and the Appraiser as Gatekeeper**

Property appraisers (*peritos avaliadores*), regulated by the Comissão do Mercado de Valores Mobiliários (CMVM) with binding opinion from BdP on qualifications  [(paulomatias.pt)](https://paulomatias.pt/perito-avaliador-imoveis-cmvm-1/) , have become the **hidden linchpin** of collateral quality. Banks routinely require CMVM-certified appraisals before approving credit, and appraisers are bound to apply valuation haircuts to properties exhibiting compliance deficits  [(PÚBLICO)](https://www.publico.pt/2025/04/09/economia/noticia/bdp-propoe-proximo-governo-alteracoes-intermediacao-credito-2129158) . A property marketed at €400,000 may be appraised at €300,000 if the appraiser cannot verify the legal status of a recently added floor or enclosed terrace — creating a €100,000 appraisal gap that forces the buyer to produce a larger down payment or abandon the transaction  [(PÚBLICO)](https://www.publico.pt/2025/04/09/economia/noticia/bdp-propoe-proximo-governo-alteracoes-intermediacao-credito-2129158) . The appraiser's own liability exposure is significant: if they value a defective property at full market value and the bank subsequently suffers collateral shortfall on default, the appraiser faces professional negligence claims and potential exclusion from BdP-approved panels  [(PVW Tinsa)](https://www.pvwtinsa.pt/avaliacao-imobiliaria-credito-habitacao-portugal/) .

The appraisal process itself has become more demanding. Banks now routinely require: architectural plans matching the physical reality of the asset; proof of *mera comunicação prévia* submission with corresponding fee payment receipts; and formal exemption declarations for pre-1951 properties issued directly by the municipality  [(Source)](https://homesglobe.com/market-insights/71) . If a seller refuses or is unable to provide these documents — relying solely on the legal waiver at the notary deed — the bank will **categorically deny financing**  [(PÚBLICO)](https://www.publico.pt/2025/04/09/economia/noticia/bdp-propoe-proximo-governo-alteracoes-intermediacao-credito-2129158) . This dynamic creates a stratified market where only cash buyers, institutional funds, or high-net-worth private investors can transact excluded assets, effectively locking out the average Portuguese family reliant on standard bank financing  [(BDJUR - Base de Dados Jurídica Almedina)](http://bdjur.almedina.net/citem.php?field=item_id&value=2041878) .

| Property Profile | Credit Rejection Risk | Buyer Exclusion |
|---|---|---|
| "Flipped" properties with undocumented structural changes | Very High | First-time buyers, high-LTV applicants |
| Rural-to-urban conversions (unregistered annexes) | High | Retail buyers, renovation financiers |
| Legacy unlicensed homes (post-1951, inherited) | Very High | All mortgage-dependent buyers |
| Properties with tacit approvals pending successive control | High | Risk-averse buyers, foreign buyers |
| AL-converted properties without use-change authorization | High | Investors seeking rental income |

#### **Broker-Specific Liability: Duty of Suitability and Commission Clawback**

Mortgage brokers operating under BdP supervision face a specific liability question: does their **duty of suitability** (*dever de adequação*) extend to property-level due diligence, or remains confined to financial product suitability? Under the RJIC regime, brokers must ensure the recommended financial products are suitable for the client's circumstances — but DL 108/2026 raises the question of whether recommending financing for a property with known (or knowable) construction irregularities violates this duty  [(servulo.com)](https://www.servulo.com/pt/investigacao-e-conhecimento/Regime-juridico-da-atividade-dos-intermediarios-de-credito/5967/) . In structurally linked firms where the agent and broker operate under the same ownership, the regulatory separation between mediation and intermediation may be pierced, exposing the broker to **joint and several liability** for property defects  [(servulo.com)](https://www.servulo.com/pt/investigacao-e-conhecimento/Regime-juridico-da-atividade-dos-intermediarios-de-credito/5967/) .

**Commission clawback risk** adds another layer. If a transaction unwinds due to construction defects discovered post-financing — whether through buyer annulment, bank collateral impairment, or municipal embargo — the broker who facilitated the loan may face clawback of commissions earned. More seriously, the broker may be exposed to buyer recourse if the buyer can demonstrate that the broker knew or should have known about the property's defects and failed to warn them. The BdP's enhanced intermediary tracking under Instrução 1/2026 (which requires reporting of intermediary involvement in all new credit operations)  [(PLMJ)](https://www.plmj.com/pt/conhecimento/notas-informativas/Novo-regime-da-Central-de-Responsabilidades-de-Credito/34407/)  creates an audit trail that makes brokers more easily identifiable in subsequent litigation.

### **2.5 The Property Appraiser (Avaliador)**

The appraiser occupies a uniquely vulnerable position in the DL 108/2026 chain. BdP, CMVM, and the Autoridade de Supervisão de Seguros (ASF) jointly oversee the appraisal framework through the Portugal FinLab initiative and regulatory coordination  [(sapo.pt)](https://jornaleconomico.sapo.pt/noticias/banco-de-portugal-cmvm-asf-avancam-com-7-a-edicao-do-portugal-finlab/) . Appraisers must have CMVM registration, meet qualification requirements (typically engineering, architecture, or related degrees), maintain professional liability insurance, and have direct contracts with financial institutions  [(PVW Tinsa)](https://www.pvwtinsa.pt/avaliacao-imobiliaria-credito-habitacao-portugal/) . The appraisal methodology — comparative, cost-based, rendimento, or residual value — must now incorporate compliance verification  [(PVW Tinsa)](https://www.pvwtinsa.pt/avaliacao-imobiliaria-credito-habitacao-portugal/) .

The appraiser's specific liability exposure under DL 108/2026 arises from the **valuation of "tacit approval" properties**. If an appraiser values a property at €400,000 based on comparable market transactions, but the property's tacit approval is subsequently reversed during municipal successive control, the collateral value may drop precipitously. The bank holding the mortgage suffers impairment; the borrower may default; and the bank may pursue the appraiser for professional negligence. Appraisal companies are reportedly developing new valuation methodologies that apply **discounts to "tacit approval" properties** — effectively pricing in the regulatory risk that DL 108/2026 creates. These discounts, while protective for the appraiser, further reduce the LTV ratio available to buyers and deepen the market stratification between compliant and non-compliant assets.

## **III. Market Dynamics & Macro Impacts**

### **3.1 Transaction Velocity vs. Transaction Safety**

The foundational premise of the Simplex reforms — that administrative delays suppress housing supply and inflate costs — is mathematically valid at the licensing stage. DL 108/2026 reduces the timeframe from project conception to construction start from an average of 120-200 days to as little as 20 days through tacit approval  [(Santander)](https://www.santander.pt/salto/lei-condominio) . The eventual revocation of the RGEU (extended to June 2026) promises further streamlining of architectural standards  [(servulo.com)](https://www.servulo.com/en/knowledge/Simplex-Urbanistico-ndash-Outros-aspetos-de-simplificacao-procedimental-uniformizacao-dos/8668/) . However, **the acceleration of licensing does not seamlessly translate to transaction acceleration**. The friction has been displaced, not eliminated  [(occ.pt)](https://www.occ.pt/pt-pt/contabilista-certificado/apoio-social-aos-membros/seguro-de-responsabilidade-civil-profissional) .

For cash transactions between sophisticated institutional players, velocity will increase. For retail transactions involving standard mortgages, the market is experiencing **net deceleration**. The time saved bypassing the municipal architect's desk is consumed by: prolonged legal due diligence (buyers and their lawyers now demand exhaustive compliance verification); independent technical auditing by engineering firms; and protracted negotiations with bank compliance officers over collateral documentation  [(Arhivix | Vaša dokumentacija na dohvat ruke)](https://arhivix.com/pt/blog/pepu-livro-obra-digital-2026-construcao) . The net time effect is negative for the retail segment: days saved on licensing are outweighed by days added on private verification. This dynamic is evidenced by the growing proportion of cash-only transactions — Manuel Alvarez of RE/MAX estimates that **only 35-40% of properties are now acquired with bank financing**, meaning 60% are cash purchases  [(varandaseveredas.pt)](https://varandaseveredas.pt/pt/noticias/show/compra-de-casas-a-pronto-dispara-com-imoveis-sem-licenca-de-utilizacao_33/) . In Lisbon and Oeiras, foreign buyers (who disproportionately pay cash) represent **60% of transactions** according to Engel & Völkers data  [(Vida Imobiliária)](https://vidaimobiliaria.com/noticias/habitacao/estrangeiros-j%C3%A1-valem-60-das-compras-de-casas-em-lisboa-e-oeiras/) .

### **3.2 The "Compliance Premium" and Two-Tier Market**

The Portuguese real estate market is aggressively stratifying into distinct pricing tiers based on compliance certainty. In an environment of information asymmetry where the State no longer guarantees legality at the point of sale, legal certainty becomes a monetizable commodity. Parallel legislation — specifically the New Urban Lease Regime (NRAU) — still requires a valid use license to execute a legal rental contract, creating a disjointed environment where a property can be legally sold but illegally rented  [(Arhivix | Vaša dokumentacija na dohvat ruke)](https://arhivix.com/pt/blog/pepu-livro-obra-digital-2026-construcao) .

| Asset Tier | Characteristics | Market Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1: Gold Standard** | Pre-1951 with municipal exemption certificate; or new builds with third-party audited compliance dossier | **Premium of 5-10%** — absolute liquidity, immediate bankability, dual resale/rental viability  [(Arhivix | Vaša dokumentacija na dohvat ruke)](https://arhivix.com/pt/blog/pepu-livro-obra-digital-2026-construcao)  |
| **Tier 2: "Simplex-Approved"** | Tacit approvals, prior communications without physical inspection, pending successive control | **Discount of 3-8%** — buyer must price in residual risk of 1-year control and infinite supervision  [(PÚBLICO)](https://www.publico.pt/2025/04/09/economia/noticia/bdp-propoe-proximo-governo-alteracoes-intermediacao-credito-2129158)  |
| **Tier 3: Unregularized** | Explicit notary declaration of no urban title; known illegal works; non-compliant structures | **Discount of 20-40%** — cash-only, restricted to investors willing to absorb legalization costs  [(PÚBLICO)](https://www.publico.pt/2025/04/09/economia/noticia/bdp-propoe-proximo-governo-alteracoes-intermediacao-credito-2129158)  |

The compliance premium is reinforced by foreign buyer behavior. Foreign investors — who accounted for **38,552 transactions in 2024** (up 19.2% vs. 2019) and represent approximately **25% of the total market**  [(Destakmov-Mediação Imobiliária, Lda)](https://www.destak-imobiliaria.com/pt/noticias/show/quem-esta-a-comprar-casa-em-portugal_269/)  — are disproportionately active in the premium Tier 1 segment. Brazilian buyers lead (20% of foreign transactions), followed by Angolans, French, and Americans  [(Destakmov-Mediação Imobiliária, Lda)](https://www.destak-imobiliaria.com/pt/noticias/show/quem-esta-a-comprar-casa-em-portugal_269/) . These buyers typically engage lawyers and conduct thorough due diligence, making them less likely to absorb Tier 2/3 risks. The result is a **bifurcated liquidity landscape**: Tier 1 assets trade with velocity and premium; Tier 2/3 assets face extended time-on-market, cash-only constraints, and progressive price erosion.

### **3.3 Foreign Buyer Vulnerability: The Information Asymmetry Shock Absorbers**

Foreign buyers represent a critical vulnerability node in the DL 108/2026 framework. The historical Golden Visa program — which channeled roughly **€7.3 billion** into Portugal between 2012-2023, with approximately 75% of the 12,718 main applicants using real estate routes  [(Get Golden Visa)](https://getgoldenvisa.com/portugal-golden-visa-program)  — created a large population of foreign property owners with limited Portuguese language skills, minimal understanding of Portuguese title law, and heavy reliance on agent/broker verification. Although the real estate Golden Visa route was eliminated in October 2023, the existing stock of foreign owners and the continued influx of non-resident buyers (driven by NHR tax regime benefits, lifestyle migration, and investment diversification) maintain this vulnerability  [(imin-portugal.com)](https://imin-portugal.com/blog/portugal-golden-visa-ending/) .

The foreign buyer's reliance on agent/broker verification becomes a critical information asymmetry under DL 108/2026. A Brazilian or American buyer purchasing a renovated Lisbon apartment has limited capacity to: verify whether the "reconstruction" works comply with the strict new definition; cross-check the *Caderneta Predial* against physical reality; confirm that the *comunicação prévia* was properly submitted with fee payment; or assess whether the contractor's IMPIC *Alvará* was active at the time of works. They are, in effect, **information asymmetry shock absorbers** — the party most likely to discover defects post-purchase and least equipped to prevent them pre-purchase. The data supports this concern: foreign buyers in the Engel & Völkers network pay an average of **€310,000** per property (up 11% YoY)  [(dn.pt)](http://dinheirovivo.dn.pt/empresas/investimento-estrangeiro-sustenta-mercado-imobilirio-da-grande-lisboa-com-transaes-a-subir-7) , placing substantial capital at risk if compliance defects emerge.

## **IV. Litigation Forecast & Cascade Scenarios**

![Market Scenario Projections and Litigation Cascade Timeline](market_scenarios.png)

### **4.1 Scenario A: Gradual Compliance (High Probability, 6-18 months)**

In this baseline scenario, agents and brokers invest incrementally in verification technology and training; transaction friction increases but remains manageable; and professional liability insurance reprices upward by 15-30% as underwriters adjust to the new risk environment. The "clean documentation" premium emerges gradually, with Tier 1 properties commanding 3-5% price advantages over Tier 2. IMPIC complaints continue rising but stabilize below 4,000 annually as agencies adapt intake protocols. Tribunal da Relação establishes initial precedents on "should have known" standards for agents and notaries, clarifying but not dramatically expanding liability. Banks tighten collateral requirements modestly, with appraisal haircuts on tacit-approval properties reaching 5-10%. Foreign buyer flows remain stable, with most engaging independent legal counsel to mitigate risk. **Transaction volumes grow 3-5% annually**, with the compliance premium absorbing rather than amplifying market disruption.

### **4.2 Scenario B: Market Friction Shock (Medium Probability, 12-24 months)**

This scenario materializes if significant pre-2026 inventory — particularly in Lisbon's historic centers, Porto's rehabilitation zones, and Algarve tourism properties — has incomplete documentation that agents are reluctant to list. Liquidity contraction in the Tier 2/3 segments spreads to the broader market as buyers become risk-averse. Foreign buyers, alerted by media coverage of annulment cases and embassy warnings, reduce inflows by 10-15%. The BdP's DSTI reduction to 45%, combined with rising interest rates, further constrains mortgage availability. Price correction hits Tier 2/3 segments first (10-15% declines), then transmits to Tier 1 as market sentiment sours. **Litigation volumes spike** as buyers who acquired properties in 2024-2025 (under the initial Simplex framework) discover defects and invoke the annulment window or *vícios ocultos* regime. Insurance exclusions proliferate, with underwriters specifically excluding DL 108/2026-related construction defects from standard policies. NPL pressure on impaired collateral remains contained (the housing NPL ratio is currently 0.2%, with 53% of loans having DSTI ≤20%)  [(idealista)](https://www.idealista.pt/news/financas/credito-a-habitacao/2026/01/05/73307-maioria-do-credito-habitacao-em-portugal-tem-taxa-de-esforco-ate-20) , but banks increase provisioning for the small subset of high-LTV, high-DSTI loans exposed to non-compliant collateral.

### **4.3 Scenario C: Litigation Cascade (Lower Probability, 18-36 months)**

The worst-case scenario unfolds if municipalities aggressively weaponize "administrative supervision" to embargo tacitly approved projects *post-facto* as a retaliatory measure against the loss of *ex-ante* control  [(belzuz.com)](https://belzuz.com/pt/publicacion/o-futuro-da-intermediacao-de-credito-em-portugal-uma-analise-a-revisao-do-rjic-anunciada-pelo-banco-de-portugal/) . Mass automated audits trigger a wave of administrative notifications, urgent rectification demands, and site embargoes as the one-year successive control window expires for early Simplex approvals. Buyers who face simultaneous municipal enforcement and property devaluation initiate mass litigation against sellers, agents, and notaries. Tribunal da Relação precedents establish broad "should have known" standards that expand constructive knowledge to encompass PEPU verification failures, area discrepancies visible on digital platforms, and contractor IMPIC status checks. Insurance exclusions become comprehensive: standard professional liability policies exclude construction-related defects, forcing agents and notaries to seek specialized (and expensive) riders. NPL pressure mounts on banks holding impaired collateral; the BdP intervenes with enhanced provisioning requirements and potential macroprudential measures. The State faces political backlash as the "simplification" narrative collapses into a litigation crisis. **Transaction volumes contract 10-15%**, with the retail market freezing and institutional capital retreating to core Tier 1 assets.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## **V. Cross-Professional Liability Chains & Can-Kicking Map**

![DL 108/2026 Can-Kicking Liability Chain](can_kicking_chain.png)

The can-kicking architecture of DL 108/2026 is not a single transfer but a **cascading chain of risk displacement**. Each party absorbs risk from upstream and kicks it downstream, with the chain breaking at different points depending on the solvency, insurance coverage, and legal defenses of each node. Understanding this chain is essential for assessing where systemic risk accumulates and where intervention is most needed.

### **5.1 The Chain Architecture**

**The State (Câmara Municipal)** kicks risk to sellers and buyers through the tacit approval mechanism. The municipality's reduced verification obligation at the entry point is defended by the argument that "controlo prévio" and *fiscalização administrativa* remain available  [(CNN Portugal)](https://cnnportugal.iol.pt/governo/habitacao/governo-justifica-dispensa-de-licenca-na-escritura-com-controlo-previo/20240202/65bc9c86d34e65afa2fa577e) . However, these are *ex-post* mechanisms that leave buyers exposed during the critical transaction phase. The chain breaks at the State level only if systemic failure triggers political backlash — a scenario that becomes more likely under Scenario C.

**The Seller** absorbs construction and licensing voids, fines up to €450,000, and potential demolition costs. The seller kicks risk to the agent (through indemnity claims for failure to advise) and to their R&W insurer (if available). The chain breaks at the seller's personal assets or, for corporate sellers, at bankruptcy. The solidary liability regime (Art. 100-A RJUE) ensures that even insolvent contractors do not shield the property owner from enforcement  [(Ricardo Gama - Advogado)](https://ricardogama.com/licenca-de-habitabilidade-quando-obrigatoria/) .

**The Agent** absorbs the risk of marketing non-compliant properties and the operational burden of verification. The agent kicks risk to the seller (via indemnity contractual clauses) and to their professional liability insurer. The chain breaks at the agent's limited personal assets and at the insurance coverage cap of €150,000 — a figure that has not been updated since 2013 and is manifestly inadequate for a market where median property values exceed €300,000  [(idealista)](https://www.idealista.pt/news/imobiliario/habitacao/2025/04/17/69107-agentes-imobiliarios-com-licenca-de-mediadoras-assim-funciona-o-mercado) . When a single negligence claim can exceed this cap (imagine a buyer seeking €100,000+ in damages for a property with unregularized works that triggered municipal embargo), the agent faces personal exposure.

**The Notary** absorbs the risk of executing an *escritura* on a questionable property. The notary kicks risk to their malpractice insurer and potentially to the Ordem dos Notários for professional discipline. The chain breaks at professional indemnity limits and at the notary's personal assets. The Bastonário's warning about a "flood of illegal properties" suggests that notaries are aware of this exposure but lack clear guidance on how far their verification duty extends  [(CNN Portugal)](https://cnnportugal.iol.pt/governo/habitacao/governo-justifica-dispensa-de-licenca-na-escritura-com-controlo-previo/20240202/65bc9c86d34e65afa2fa577e) .

**The Broker** absorbs the risk of facilitating loans on defective collateral. The broker kicks risk to the bank (via the *vinculação* contract) and, in structurally linked firms, to the agent entity under the same ownership. The chain breaks at commission clawback and at personal liability for suitability breaches. BdP's enhanced intermediary tracking under Instrução 1/2026 makes brokers more easily identifiable in litigation, increasing their exposure  [(PLMJ)](https://www.plmj.com/pt/conhecimento/notas-informativas/Novo-regime-da-Central-de-Responsabilidades-de-Credito/34407/) .

**The Bank** absorbs NPLs on impaired collateral and potential guarantee activation. The bank kicks risk to the borrower (through recourse proceedings) and to the appraisal company (for overvaluation). The chain breaks at provisioning requirements and, in extreme systemic scenarios, at taxpayer-funded bailout. The current NPL ratio of 0.2% for housing loans and the conservative LTV distribution (only 7% of stock has financing >80%) provide substantial buffers  [(idealista)](https://www.idealista.pt/news/financas/credito-a-habitacao/2026/01/05/73307-maioria-do-credito-habitacao-em-portugal-tem-taxa-de-esforco-ate-20) , but these buffers could be tested if a significant volume of non-compliant collateral simultaneously devalues.

**The Appraiser** absorbs overvaluation risk on defective properties. The appraiser kicks risk to BdP (through potential sanctions or panel exclusion) and to their professional liability insurer. The chain breaks at conservative methodology adoption — appraisers who systematically discount "tacit approval" properties may avoid liability but contribute to market stratification.

**The Buyer** absorbs the terminal risk: post-purchase defects, fines, demolition orders, and negative equity. The buyer kicks risk back up the chain through litigation against the seller, agent, and notary. The chain breaks at negative equity (when the property value falls below the mortgage balance) and at unenforceable judgments (when the seller/agent is insolvent or judgment-proof). The buyer is the **shock absorber of the entire system** — the party who ultimately pays when every other node has kicked the risk downstream.

## **VI. Insurance Market Impact**

### **6.1 Professional Indemnity Repricing**

The insurance market for real estate professionals is entering a **hard market phase** driven by DL 108/2026. Professional indemnity coverage for agents (minimum €150,000 under Law 15/2013), notaries (mandatory under Article 89 of the Notary Statute), and brokers (BdP-mandated) is facing simultaneous pressure from multiple directions. Claims frequency is rising: IMPIC complaints quadrupled in four years  [(idealista)](https://www.idealista.pt/news/imobiliario/habitacao/2026/03/12/74395-mediacao-imobiliaria-numero-de-queixas-dispara-e-atinge-maximo) , Tribunal da Relacao precedents are expanding the scope of agent liability  [(Tribunal da Relação de Coimbra)](https://trc.pt/venda-civil-de-coisa-defeituosa-anomalias-em-imovel-vendido-no-estado-de-usado-onus-da-prova-do-defeito-presuncao-de-culpa-do-vendedor-defeito-oculto-responsabilidade-do-mediador-imobiliario/) , and the removal of municipal verification increases the probability that undetected defects will generate post-transaction claims. Claims severity is also rising: with median property values in Lisbon exceeding €5,720/m²  [(Connaught Law)](https://connaughtlaw.com/portugal-golden-visa-real-estate-eliminated/)  and foreign buyer averages at €310,000  [(dn.pt)](http://dinheirovivo.dn.pt/empresas/investimento-estrangeiro-sustenta-mercado-imobilirio-da-grande-lisboa-com-transaes-a-subir-7) , a single negligence claim can exhaust the €150,000 minimum coverage.

The ASF (insurance regulator) has already acknowledged this pressure in its 2025 public consultation, noting an **increase of 4.03% in minimum capital requirements** for insurance mediators' professional liability  [(asf.com.pt)](https://www.asf.com.pt/documents/d/site-asf/relatorio-da-consulta-publica-n-3_2025) . Comparable adjustments for real estate mediators have not yet been implemented, but market participants expect increases of 15-30% in the 2026-2027 renewal cycle. More critically, **coverage exclusions are emerging**: underwriters are beginning to exclude DL 108/2026-specific risks — particularly construction defect claims arising from tacit approvals, properties without habitation licenses, and unregularized works — from standard policies. These exclusions force professionals to seek specialized riders at substantially higher premiums, or to accept uncovered exposure.

### **6.2 Representations & Warranties Insurance: An Emerging Market**

Representations and warranties (R&W) insurance for residential transactions — standard in US and UK commercial real estate but rare in Portuguese residential markets — may emerge as a viable product under DL 108/2026. R&W insurance covers breach of seller representations about property condition, title, and compliance, transferring the risk from the seller to the insurance market. The product is particularly relevant for Tier 2/3 transactions where sellers want to offer buyers assurance without exposing themselves to open-ended liability. However, the Portuguese market lacks the claims history, underwriting expertise, and broker distribution networks that support R&W insurance in more mature markets. Development of this product line will depend on insurers' willingness to price DL 108/2026-specific risks and on buyer willingness to pay the premium (typically 1-3% of transaction value).

### **6.3 The Coverage Gap**

The most dangerous dimension of the insurance impact is the **coverage gap** — risks that cannot be insured at any price. These include: (a) intentional fraud (*dolo*) by agents or sellers, which voids insurance coverage; (b) criminal liability for document falsification under Article 256 CP, which is uninsurable; (c) municipal fines and demolition costs, which fall outside standard professional liability coverage; and (d) systemic market devaluation of non-compliant properties, which is an uninsurable market risk. The coverage gap concentrates risk in the personal assets of sellers and agents, and ultimately in the banking system through NPLs on impaired collateral.

## **VII. Condominium Downstream Liability: The Hidden Second Wave**

### **7.1 Post-Purchase Collective Liability**

The DL 108/2026 analysis that focuses exclusively on the transaction chain misses a critical downstream liability vector: **condominium collective liability**. Post-purchase, buyers of apartment units face obligations that extend far beyond their individual four walls. Under Portuguese horizontal property law (Decree-Law 268/94, as amended by Law 8/2022), condominium owners are jointly responsible for the conservation and maintenance of common parts — roofs, facades, structural elements, elevators, and common installations  [(espacomunicipal.pt)](https://www.espacomunicipal.pt/media/1335/guiacondominio_capitulo2_regulamento.pdf) . The Law 8/2022 reform introduced important protections: the new owner is NOT responsible for works in common parts approved before their acquisition (the previous owner bears that cost)  [(A Caixa)](https://www.cgd.pt/Site/Saldo-Positivo/casa-e-familia/Pages/lei-do-condominio.aspx?srsltid=AfmBOoqSY2wW9LqxQhtcIilgv32uarcsqdk7aYkpYrY25lzX2htDYaMR) . However, the new owner IS responsible for ongoing conservation works, urgent repairs, and — critically — their **quota-part of common liabilities** that may stem from pre-purchase decisions  [(Condomínios com Vida)](https://condominioscomvida.pt/obras-no-condominio-de-quem-e-a-responsabilidade-de-pagamento/) .

The Tribunal da Relação do Porto established in 2007 that **defects originating in common parts** (such as facade infiltration) are the condominium's responsibility, not the seller's — meaning the buyer cannot claim against the seller for common-part defects but must instead contribute to condominium repair costs through their permillage quota  [(Bases Jurídico-Documentais)](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrp.nsf/c3fb530030ea1c61802568d9005cd5bb/031c3f6c4975520a80257393005210a8?OpenDocument) . This creates a perverse situation under DL 108/2026: a buyer who purchases an apartment in a building with unregularized common-part modifications (an illegally enclosed balcony, an unapproved facade alteration, a rooftop extension executed without *comunicação prévia*) inherits collective liability for those modifications. The municipality's *fiscalização administrativa* — exercisable *a todo o tempo* — can require demolition or restoration of these common-part modifications, with costs distributed among all owners according to their quotas  [(belzuz.com)](https://belzuz.com/pt/publicacion/o-futuro-da-intermediacao-de-credito-em-portugal-uma-analise-a-revisao-do-rjic-anunciada-pelo-banco-de-portugal/) .

### **7.2 The Obligations Propter Rem Trap**

A further layer of condominium liability arises from the principle of **obligações propter rem** — obligations attached to the property that follow it regardless of ownership changes  [(forumdacasa.com)](https://forumdacasa.com/discussion/48056/obras-em-condominio-problemas-anteriores-a-compra-da-casa-sou-obrigado-a-pagar/) . Under Article 408 of the Civil Code, these "real obligations" transfer to the new owner upon acquisition. While Law 8/2022 clarified that pre-purchase approved works are not the new owner's responsibility, obligations arising from the normal use and conservation of common parts — including deferred maintenance that becomes urgent — fall on the current owner  [(Polígrafo)](https://poligrafo.sapo.pt/fact-check/proprietarios-podem-ter-de-pagar-por-obras-do-condominio-anteriores-a-compra-da-casa/) . A buyer who acquires a unit in a building where the previous owner deferred facade repairs, roof maintenance, or elevator modernization inherits the accumulation of those deferred costs. If the condominium assembly approves urgent works post-purchase, the new owner must pay their share even if the deterioration began before their acquisition  [(Condomínios com Vida)](https://condominioscomvida.pt/obras-no-condominio-de-quem-e-a-responsabilidade-de-pagamento/) .

The interaction with DL 108/2026 is insidious. Buildings with tacitly approved modifications — perhaps a rooftop terrace added under a *comunicação prévia* that never received physical inspection — may have common-part alterations that appear legally compliant on paper but fail physical inspection during *fiscalização administrativa*. When the municipality eventually orders restoration of the original condition, the cost is distributed across all owners. A buyer who conducted thorough due diligence on their individual unit may still face substantial unanticipated liabilities from common-part compliance failures that were invisible at the time of purchase.

| Condominium Liability Type | Buyer Responsibility | Statutory Basis | DL 108/2026 Interaction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Works approved BEFORE purchase | NO (previous owner pays) | Law 8/2022  [(A Caixa)](https://www.cgd.pt/Site/Saldo-Positivo/casa-e-familia/Pages/lei-do-condominio.aspx?srsltid=AfmBOoqSY2wW9LqxQhtcIilgv32uarcsqdk7aYkpYrY25lzX2htDYaMR)  | Protects buyer from pre-existing work debts |
| Ongoing conservation/maintenance | YES (quota share) | Art. 1424 CC  [(Condomínios com Vida)](https://condominioscomvida.pt/obras-no-condominio-de-quem-e-a-responsabilidade-de-pagamento/)  | Deferred maintenance costs accumulate |
| Urgent repairs post-purchase | YES (even without assembly) | Art. 1424 CC  [(Condomínios com Vida)](https://condominioscomvida.pt/obras-no-condominio-de-quem-e-a-responsabilidade-de-pagamento/)  | Unanticipated costs from pre-existing deterioration |
| Common-part defects (infiltration, structure) | YES (quota share) | TR Porto 2007  [(Bases Jurídico-Documentais)](https://www.dgsi.pt/jtrp.nsf/c3fb530030ea1c61802568d9005cd5bb/031c3f6c4975520a80257393005210a8?OpenDocument)  | Unregularized common modifications trigger collective liability |
| Municipal restoration orders | YES (quota share) | Art. 100-A RJUE  [(Ricardo Gama - Advogado)](https://ricardogama.com/licenca-de-habitabilidade-quando-obrigatoria/)  | Tacit-approved common alterations may require demolition |
| Fire insurance (mandatory) | YES (individual + common) | DL 268/94  [(espacomunicipal.pt)](https://www.espacomunicipal.pt/media/1335/guiacondominio_capitulo2_regulamento.pdf)  | Inadequate coverage for construction defect claims |

## **VIII. Digital Infrastructure Gap: The Verification Bottleneck**

### **8.1 PEPU: Promise vs. Reality**

The Plataforma Eletrónica dos Procedimentos Urbanísticos (PEPU) was designed as the digital backbone of the Simplex reform — a unified national platform for submitting, tracking, and verifying all urban licensing procedures, mandatory for all 308 Portuguese municipalities from 5 January 2026  [(Arhivix | Vaša dokumentacija na dohvat ruke)](https://arhivix.com/pt/blog/pepu-livro-obra-digital-2026-construcao) . In theory, PEPU offers total traceability: every submission, every request for additional elements, every decision is logged with date and time stamps, making the computation of legal deadlines "objective and auditable"  [(Varandas Coelho Construções)](https://www.varandascoelho.pt/blog/guia-simplex-2026) . The platform promises interoperability with the Instituto dos Registos e Notariado (IRN), the Balcão Único do Prédio (BUPi), and the Autoridade Tributária, with future support for Building Information Modeling (BIM) submission  [(AC Arquitetos)](https://www.ac-arquitetos.com/post/novidades-legislativas-em-urbanismo-para-2026-o-que-muda-para-proprietarios-e-investidores) .

In practice, PEPU's implementation has been **diffuse and uneven**. A January 2026 assessment by legal practitioner Susana Seiby described the rollout as characterized by "silêncio estranho... implementação difusa... municípios em ritmos diferentes, técnicos ainda sem formação adequada, plataformas que não comunicam entre si, procedimentos que continuam a correr 'em paralelo' — parte digital, parte analógica, parte informal"  [(seibysusana.com)](https://www.seibysusana.com/pepu-estava-previsto-era-obrigatorio-e-agora/) . The problem, Seiby notes, is not merely technological but "estrutural e cultural. Digitalizar não é apenas criar uma plataforma; é mudar processos, responsabilidades, hábitos e poderes"  [(seibysusana.com)](https://www.seibysusana.com/pepu-estava-previsto-era-obrigatorio-e-agora/) . Mário Longras of Sérvulo & Associados similarly warns that the legislator "confia excessivamente na tecnologia para resolver problemas essencialmente jurídicos — como a definição de prazos, o alcance dos pareceres e a responsabilidade em caso de omissão eletrónica"  [(idealista)](https://www.idealista.pt/news/imobiliario/construcao/2025/10/24/72170-simplex-urbanistico-2026-impactos-para-estado-municipios-e-cidadaos) .

### **8.2 Operational Consequences of the Digital Gap**

The PEPU gap has immediate operational consequences for agents, brokers, and notaries attempting to fulfill their new verification duties. If the platform does not contain complete records of prior communications, fee payments, or municipal decisions, the agent cannot verify compliance through digital means and must resort to manual municipal inquiries — reintroducing the very bureaucratic friction that Simplex was supposed to eliminate. The Livro de Obra digital (mandatory digital construction log) creates an immutable, cryptographically sealed record of all construction activities  [(Arhivix | Vaša dokumentacija na dohvat ruke)](https://arhivix.com/pt/blog/pepu-livro-obra-digital-2026-construcao) , but if contractors fail to maintain it properly or if municipalities lack the technical capacity to verify its completeness, the audit trail breaks down. The digital infrastructure gap is not a peripheral issue — it is **the operational bottleneck that determines whether the private sector can actually absorb the verification burden** that DL 108/2026 has transferred from the State.

## **IX. International Analogues: Probability Anchors**

### **9.1 Spain: SAREB and the Valuation Correction**

Spain's post-2008 experience provides the most relevant analogue for Portugal's DL 108/2026 risk profile. Spain's *banco malo* (SAREB) acquired approximately 200,000 assets with a gross book value of €106.9 billion at an average discount of **63%** — land received a 79.5% discount, unfinished developments 63.2%, and finished housing 54.2%  [(windows.net)](https://ypfsresourcelibrary.blob.core.windows.net/fcic/YPFS/Tam%20SAREB%20(ESP%20GFC)%202020-03-17%20PRELIMINARY_DRAFT.pdf) . The European Commission's analysis found that house price falls of 10.6% in 2013 would drive NPL rates to 13.9%, while a 21.9% fall would push NPLs to 16.6%  [(Die Europäische Kommission)](https://ec.europa.eu/economy_finance/publications/country_focus/2013/pdf/cf_vol10_issue8_en.pdf) . The critical lesson for Portugal: **when collateral values are uncertain due to documentation gaps or compliance questions, the discount required to clear the market is severe**. SAREB's experience also demonstrates that bad banks work to carve out impaired assets and stabilize the financial system, but "some complexities, such as the eventual sale price of assets and funding strategy, were underestimated in its creation"  [(windows.net)](https://ypfsresourcelibrary.blob.core.windows.net/fcic/YPFS/Tam%20SAREB%20(ESP%20GFC)%202020-03-17%20PRELIMINARY_DRAFT.pdf) . Portugal does not currently need a SAREB equivalent, but the DL 108/2026 framework creates the documentation uncertainties that could necessitate one if market conditions deteriorate.

### **9.2 Ireland: NAMA and the Property Loan Clean-Up**

Ireland's National Asset Management Agency (NAMA) removed approximately **one-third of all property loans** from Irish banks' balance sheets — a substantially higher proportion than Spain's SAREB (10%)  [(europa.eu)](http://publications.europa.eu/resource/genpub/PUB_KCBD16036ENC_PDFX.1.3) . NAMA's aggressive discounting (loans transferred at average discounts of 47-58%) forced banks to recognize upfront losses but insulated them from further property price declines. The residual NPL ratio for Irish banks' commercial real estate loans was still **37%** at end-2015, demonstrating that even massive asset transfers do not immediately resolve underlying collateral quality issues  [(europa.eu)](http://publications.europa.eu/resource/genpub/PUB_KCBD16036ENC_PDFX.1.3) . For Portugal, the NAMA lesson is that **the scale of documentation-risk transfer matters**: if DL 108/2026 generates a significant volume of non-compliant properties that banks refuse to finance, the resulting liquidity gap may require public intervention to prevent market seizure.

### **9.3 UK: Grenfell and the Building Safety Liability Cascade**

The Grenfell Tower tragedy of June 2017 and the subsequent Building Safety Act 2022 demonstrate how **building safety failures can generate cascading liability** across the entire construction and ownership chain  [(GRJ)](https://www.grjcontracting.com/blog/7-years-after-the-grenfell-disaster-how-has-fire-safety-changed/) . The UK's new regime creates liability for manufacturers and suppliers of inherently defective construction products, expands duties for "higher-risk buildings" (over 18 meters), and mandates "accountable persons" who must assess and manage building safety risks  [(GRJ)](https://www.grjcontracting.com/blog/7-years-after-the-grenfell-disaster-how-has-fire-safety-changed/) . The UK Supreme Court's 2025 decision in *URS Corp Ltd v BDW Trading Ltd* confirmed that developers have wide recourse against other parties involved in defective construction, even many years after completion  [(rics.org)](https://ww3.rics.org/uk/en/journals/property-journal/building-safety-act-grenfell-inherent-defects.html) . The Portuguese parallel is clear: DL 108/2026's tacit approval regime, by reducing *ex-ante* verification, increases the probability that construction defects (fire safety, structural integrity, material compliance) will go undetected until a triggering event forces discovery. When that discovery occurs, the solidary liability regime (Art. 100-A RJUE) and the 10-year building defect guarantee (DL 122/2021) provide the legal architecture for a similar liability cascade.

| Analogue | Key Mechanism | Relevance to DL 108/2026 |
|---|---|---|
| **Spain (SAREB)** | Bad bank acquired €106.9B at 63% avg discount; NPLs reached 16.6% in adverse scenario  [(Die Europäische Kommission)](https://ec.europa.eu/economy_finance/publications/country_focus/2013/pdf/cf_vol10_issue8_en.pdf)  | Documentation uncertainty drives severe collateral discounts; market liquidity requires public backstop |
| **Ireland (NAMA)** | Removed 1/3 of property loans; residual NPLs still 37% in CRE  [(europa.eu)](http://publications.europa.eu/resource/genpub/PUB_KCBD16036ENC_PDFX.1.3)  | Scale of risk transfer determines systemic impact; upfront loss recognition is painful but necessary |
| **UK (Grenfell/BSA 2022)** | Building Safety Act creates liability cascade; developers can recover from all construction parties  [(rics.org)](https://ww3.rics.org/uk/en/journals/property-journal/building-safety-act-grenfell-inherent-defects.html)  | Reduced *ex-ante* verification increases defect discovery risk; solidary liability enables mass recourse |

## **XI. Conclusion: The Privatization of Risk**

Decree-Law No. 108/2026 represents a profound reallocation of risk in the Portuguese real estate market. The State has reduced its *ex-ante* verification obligations while retaining *ex-post* policing powers, transferring the compliance burden to private parties without transferring the corresponding authority or resources. The result is a **can-kicking mechanism** of systemic proportions: risk flows from Câmara Municipal → seller → agent → notary → broker → bank → appraiser → buyer, with each node attempting to kick it further downstream until it reaches the party least equipped to absorb it.

The quantified evidence is unambiguous. IMPIC complaints have **quadrupled in four years**  [(idealista)](https://www.idealista.pt/news/imobiliario/habitacao/2026/03/12/74395-mediacao-imobiliaria-numero-de-queixas-dispara-e-atinge-maximo) . Professional liability insurance for agents is **frozen at 2013 levels** despite median property values tripling  [(idealista)](https://www.idealista.pt/news/imobiliario/habitacao/2025/04/17/69107-agentes-imobiliarios-com-licenca-de-mediadoras-assim-funciona-o-mercado) . Foreign buyers — who represent **25% of transactions and 60% of Lisbon/Oeiras activity**  [(Destakmov-Mediação Imobiliária, Lda)](https://www.destak-imobiliaria.com/pt/noticias/show/quem-esta-a-comprar-casa-em-portugal_269/)  — are disproportionately exposed as information asymmetry shock absorbers. The Banco de Portugal's tightening macroprudential framework (DSTI reduction, enhanced intermediary tracking, stricter appraisal requirements)  [(PÚBLICO)](https://www.publico.pt/2026/05/20/economia/noticia/bdp-consulta-bancos-reducao-taxa-esforco-credito-habitacao-45-2175386)  creates a **de facto regulatory bottleneck** that offsets the Simplex velocity gains. And the digital infrastructure (PEPU) that was supposed to enable private verification remains, by the admission of practitioners and technologists alike, "difusa... parte digital, parte analógica, parte informal"  [(seibysusana.com)](https://www.seibysusana.com/pepu-estava-previsto-era-obrigatorio-e-agora/) .

The litigation architecture is already in place. Tribunal da Relação precedents on *vícios ocultos* (Art. 913 CC), solidary liability (Art. 100-A RJUE), and agent duty of disclosure (Art. 17 of Law 15/2013) provide the doctrinal foundation for mass buyer recourse  [(stj.pt)](https://juris.stj.pt/2111%2F21.0T8STB.E1.S1/Z4jXueDBWDzgrxNCz1D7bKAiL20?search=k_t06Ks2i5E3zej8Z5M) . The 10-year building defect guarantee (DL 122/2021) extends seller liability for a decade  [(A Caixa)](https://www.cgd.pt/Site/Saldo-Positivo/casa-e-familia/Pages/casa-nova-com-defeitos.aspx?srsltid=AfmBOopFZYrVMmJ91S-JR5xydmbEo24wIm9nGWv7Ifjjuscia9NlYpW7) . The condominium downstream liability regime (Law 8/2022) ensures that buyers of apartment units inherit collective obligations they cannot fully verify pre-purchase  [(A Caixa)](https://www.cgd.pt/Site/Saldo-Positivo/casa-e-familia/Pages/lei-do-condominio.aspx?srsltid=AfmBOoqSY2wW9LqxQhtcIilgv32uarcsqdk7aYkpYrY25lzX2htDYaMR) . The only question is whether these legal mechanisms will be activated gradually (Scenario A), through a friction shock (Scenario B), or in a full litigation cascade (Scenario C).

The honest assessment is that DL 108/2026 does not eliminate regulatory friction — it **privatizes it**. The success of the framework will not be measured by the speed at which municipal councils process applications, but by the efficiency with which the private market can build new infrastructures of trust to replace the definitive, yet sluggish, municipal stamp. Those infrastructures — technical verification platforms, updated insurance products, standardized due diligence protocols, interoperable digital registries — do not yet exist at the scale required. Until they do, the verification-shift cascade will continue to concentrate risk in the weakest nodes of the transaction chain, with consequences that will unfold over the next decade.
