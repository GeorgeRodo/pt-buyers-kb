# Executive Summary

The Portuguese property market in 2026 sits at a defining inflection point. After nearly a decade of intense foreign demand, successive legislative packages (Mais Habitação, the closure of the real-estate golden visa pathway, the 2023–2025 tenant-favouring rental reforms, and tighter anti-money-laundering controls) have reshaped how transactions are structured, financed, and taxed. Yet one structural feature has barely moved: the overwhelming majority of licensed agents in Portugal still operate on the seller's side of the table, paid by and answerable to the seller. In that environment, a foreign or domestic buyer who walks into a listing without dedicated representation is, in practice, negotiating against the very person who is paid to deliver them at the highest price the market will bear.

This playbook is the operational answer. It is written for the dedicated Buyer's Agent — the licensed professional who works exclusively for the buyer under a written Buyer Representation Agreement, accepts no commission from the seller, and owes full fiduciary duties (loyalty, confidentiality, disclosure, accounting, obedience, and reasonable care) only to the buyer. It maps every phase of the buyer journey from initial lead intake through long-term post-purchase relationship management, and ties each phase to the specific documents, timelines, counterparties, and risks that define the Portuguese transaction.

The structure is deliberate. Chapter 1 frames the regulatory and market context that makes buyer-only representation necessary. Chapters 2 through 9 then move through the eight operational phases in chronological order, each with subtasks, checklists, sample scripts, red-flag lists, timelines, and tool recommendations. Chapter 10 consolidates property-type-specific guidance across seven categories (historical, new build/off-plan, land/development, luxury, resale, rural/quinta, and high-yield investment stock), because the same nominal phase — due diligence, for example — produces radically different work when the asset is a 19th-century Alfama building versus a Silver Coast off-plan apartment. Chapter 11 weaves in the cross-cutting concerns that touch every transaction: fiduciary ethics, regional variations, expatriate-specific risks, risk management, the extended professional team, regulatory compliance, communication discipline, fee models, and 2026 market trends.

Three appendices supply ready-to-use operational assets: a client onboarding questionnaire and Buyer Representation Agreement template (Appendix A), a library of master checklists covering viewings, due diligence, pre-closing, handover, snagging, and off-plan completion (Appendix B), and a quick-reference collection of sample scripts, protective CPCV clause language, and a master red-flag list (Appendix C). Together these turn the playbook from a reference document into a daily working tool.

Throughout, the priority is buyer protection and value creation in a market structurally tilted toward the seller. The aim is not to be exhaustive for its own sake — it is to ensure that no buyer's agent operating in Portugal in 2026 is left improvising on a question, a clause, or a contingency that was, in fact, foreseeable.

# Chapter 1 — Foundations of Buyer Representation in Portugal


Before any phase of operational work begins, the buyer's agent must internalise why buyer-only representation exists in Portugal as a distinct discipline rather than as a default feature of the market. This chapter establishes the regulatory, structural, and ethical context within which every subsequent phase of the playbook operates. Without this foundation, the technical checklists in later chapters risk being applied mechanically, without the judgment that the Portuguese market specifically demands.


## 1.1  The Seller-Biased Reality of the Portuguese Agency Market


The single most important fact about Portuguese real estate intermediation is that the listing agent — the mediador imobiliário — is engaged, instructed, and paid by the seller. Under the standard mediación contract used across the industry, the agent's mandate is to market the property, deliver a ready, willing, and able buyer, and collect a commission deducted from the purchase price at closing. The agent's legal and economic alignment runs to the seller. There is no equivalent industry-wide presumption that the buyer is also owed a fiduciary.


This is not a deficiency of individual agents; it is the structural design of the regime established by Decree-Law 118/2013 (the regime governing the activity of real estate intermediation and the licensing of mediadores), which treats the agent as the seller's commercial counterparty. Most Portuguese buyers accept this arrangement because they grew up with it, know how to push back, and often have family or friends who have navigated the same process. Foreign buyers, who represent a very large share of the prime Lisbon, Porto, Algarve, and Silver Coast markets, generally do not have that lived context. They arrive having read British, American, or Northern European agency norms into a market that does not share those norms, and they are routinely surprised to discover that the charming agent who showed them three apartments on a Saturday morning is contractually bound to extract the highest possible price from them.


The practical consequences of this asymmetry are visible at every stage of a transaction. The listing agent selects which comparable sales to share, frames the asking price as a bargain against an unverified comparable set, controls the timing of offers, may not disclose competing bids, and — critically — drafts the promissory contract (Contrato de Promessa de Compra e Venda, CPCV) in language that protects the seller's deposit and timeline. A buyer who walks into this process without their own representative is, in effect, negotiating against their counterparty's lawyer while having none of their own.


> 🚩 **WHAT THE LISTING AGENT IS NOT**
>
> Not your agent, no matter how friendly or helpful they appear.
> Not obliged to disclose the seller's minimum acceptable price.
> Not obliged to volunteer negative information about the property unless directly asked.
> Not obliged to recommend that you commission an independent survey or legal review.
> Not prohibited from representing multiple buyers for the same property simultaneously.


## 1.2  What "Buyer's Agent" Means Under Portuguese Law


There is no separate statutory licence category in Portugal for a buyer's agent. The buyer's agent is, in regulatory terms, a licensed mediador imobiliário (or a duly registered agent acting under the supervision of a licensed mediador) who has elected to operate under a Buyer Representation Agreement with the buyer rather than — or, in some business models, in addition to — listing mandates. The licence itself, issued by IMPIC (the Institute for the Supervision of Real Estate and Construction), is the same; what changes is the contractual counterparty and the resulting fiduciary orientation.


Three legal and regulatory features define the role:

- Dual agency is permitted in Portugal but requires the informed written consent of both parties, and the agent must disclose any conflict of interest in writing before showing a property where they hold the listing. A buyer-only agent refuses dual agency on principle, because it is structurally impossible to owe undivided loyalty to two parties whose economic interests on price are diametrically opposed.
- Commission source matters. A buyer's agent may be paid by the buyer directly under a retainer, flat-fee, or success-fee structure, or may agree to share the commission that the seller has contractually committed to pay the listing agent. In the latter model — the most common in Portugal today — the buyer's agent must disclose the source and amount of the commission to the buyer in writing, and must accept that accepting seller-side commission does not change their fiduciary duty to the buyer. The cleaner model is buyer-paid, because it removes the structural pressure to close at any price.
- The AMI licence and IMPIC registration are non-negotiable. Operating without them is a violation of Decree-Law 118/2013, exposes the agent to fines, and leaves the buyer without the consumer-protection backstop of the Fundo de Garantia dos Mediadores. The buyer's agent must be able to produce their AMI number on request, and the buyer should verify the number on the IMPIC public registry before signing the Representation Agreement.

*Table 1.1 — Agent Roles in Portugal: A Comparison*


| Dimension | Listing Agent (Seller's Agent) | Dual Agent | Buyer's Agent (Buyer-Only) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paid by | Seller (commission from price) | Both sides (commission split) | Buyer (retainer/flat/success) or shared listing commission with full disclosure |
| Fiduciary duty to | Seller | Both — inherent conflict | Buyer exclusively |
| Confidentiality to buyer | None — buyer info goes to seller | Limited — must disclose conflicts | Full |
| Loyalty on price | Maximise seller price | Walk a tightrope | Minimise buyer price & terms |
| May show own listings | Yes (their mandate) | Yes (with disclosure) | Yes, but must disclose and offer independent comparables |
| Drafts CPCV | Yes — for seller | Yes — nominally neutral | Coordinates buyer's lawyer for buyer-protective CPCV |
| AMI / IMPIC licence | Required | Required | Required |
| Recommended for foreign buyers | No | No | Yes |


*Source: Decree-Law 118/2013 and standard industry practice. Fiduciary duties as developed in Portuguese jurisprudence and consumer protection principles.*


## 1.3  The Fiduciary Argument for Buyer-Only Representation


The case for buyer-only representation in Portugal rests on three converging arguments. The first is structural: in a market where the counterparty's agent is paid to extract maximum value, symmetric representation is the only credible equaliser. The second is informational: local knowledge of pricing, building stock, urbanistic constraints, and counterparty behaviour is asymmetric, and that asymmetry costs unrepresented buyers real money — typically 3–8% of purchase price in overpayment, plus avoided-but-unnecessary renovation and tax errors that can add another 2–5%. The third is procedural: the Portuguese transaction sequence (CPCV → escritura → registo) has multiple points where a buyer without an advocate can lose the deposit, miss a contingency, or accept liability for an undisclosed defect.


The fiduciary duty the buyer's agent owes is not a vague professional courtesy; it is a defined bundle of obligations adapted from the general Portuguese law of mandate (Contrato de Mandato, Articles 1157 et seq. of the Civil Code) and the consumer-protection principles that apply to all licensed intermediation activity. The six core duties are:

- Loyalty — act in the buyer's best interest even when that interest conflicts with the agent's own economic interest in closing the deal. If the property is wrong for the buyer, the agent says so.
- Confidentiality — protect the buyer's maximum budget, motivation, timeline, and competing offers as if they were the agent's own. Never disclose to the listing agent that the buyer can pay more than the offer being made.
- Disclosure — proactively surface all material information the agent knows or reasonably should know about the property, the seller, the locality, and the transaction process. Silence on a material fact is a breach.
- Accounting — handle any client funds (deposits, retainer fees) through separate, auditable accounts; provide periodic written accounting on request.
- Obedience — follow the buyer's lawful instructions even when the agent disagrees, after clearly documenting the disagreement in writing.
- Reasonable care — exercise the competence and diligence that a reasonably prudent licensed agent in the same market would exercise, including knowing when to refer the buyer to a specialist.

## 1.4  The Portuguese Transaction Sequence — Overview


A Portuguese residential transaction moves through a recognisable sequence that the buyer's agent must understand in detail, because each transition is also a risk checkpoint. The high-level flow is:


*Table 1.2 — Portuguese Property Transaction: Standard Sequence*


| Step | Document / Event | Typical Timing | Key Parties | Buyer's Agent Role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Buyer Engagement & Representation Agreement | Week 0 | Buyer, Agent | Sign BRA, set search criteria |
| 2 | Property Identification & Viewings | Weeks 1–8 (variable) | Buyer, Agent, Listing Agent | Sourcing, screening, viewing checklist |
| 3 | Offer Letter (Proposta de Compra) | When shortlist narrows | Buyer, Agent, Seller, Listing Agent | Draft, justify, transmit |
| 4 | Promissory Contract (CPCV) | 1–4 weeks after offer acceptance | Buyer, Seller, Lawyers | Coordinate, review protective clauses |
| 5 | Deposit Payment (Sinal) | At CPCV signing | Buyer → Seller (via escrow) | Confirm escrow, receipt, conditions |
| 6 | Due Diligence Period (post-CPCV) | Between CPCV and escritura (30–90 days) | Buyer's lawyer, surveyor, bank | Coordinate timeline, monitor contingencies |
| 7 | Mortgage Approval & Bank Instruction | Concurrent with Step 6 | Buyer, Bank, Notary | Liaise with bank, ensure appraisal |
| 8 | Final Deed (Escritura Pública) or PDA | On DD completion + funds ready | Buyer, Seller, Notary, Bank | Pre-deed verification, witness |
| 9 | Payment of Balance + Taxes | At deed | Buyer → Seller; Buyer → Tax Authority | Verify amounts, IMT/IS calculation |
| 10 | Land Registry (Registo Predial) | 1–10 days after deed | Buyer's lawyer / notary | Confirm registration of ownership |
| 11 | Utilities Transfer & Keys | At or after deed | Buyer, utilities, condominium | Coordinate handover checklist |
| 12 | Post-Purchase Support | Ongoing | Buyer, Agent, Vendors | Renovation, rental, retention |


*PDA = Pacto de Aquisição e Venda (private acquisition agreement with full effect, increasingly common in place of the traditional escritura). Timelines are typical; complex cases (heritage properties, off-plan, land) extend by weeks or months.*


Two features of this sequence deserve emphasis at the outset. First, the CPCV is not a draft or letter of intent — it is a binding bilateral contract that, once signed, commits both parties to the transaction under penalty of forfeiture (for the buyer) or double-the-deposit (for the seller) if either side defaults without legal justification. The deposit paid at CPCV is real money at real risk. Second, due diligence is in principle best completed before the CPCV is signed, but in practice much of it runs concurrently with the post-CPCV period; the CPCV must therefore include well-drafted condition precedents (condições suspensivas) that protect the buyer's right to walk away with the deposit intact if DD uncovers a deal-breaking issue. Chapter 7 develops these clauses in detail.


## 1.5  The 2026 Market & Regulatory Backdrop


Several regulatory and market features define the operating environment for a buyer's agent in 2026. The Mais Habitação package, phased in from late 2023, ended the real-estate golden visa pathway for new investments (existing holders retain rights), tightened local accommodation (alojamento local / AL) licensing — particularly in Lisbon and Porto historic centres — and introduced tenant-favouring rent controls that continue to shape the rental market in 2026. The cumulative effect for buyers is a market that is less speculative and more centred on owner-occupation, longer-term rental, and genuine lifestyle migration, with proportionally fewer short-term-flip investors than in the 2018–2022 peak.


The tax environment continues to evolve. The IMT (Imposto Municipal sobre as Transmissões) brackets for 2026 should be re-verified against the year's State Budget, but the structural progression — increasing marginal rates from roughly 1% to 8% on residential property — and the stamp duty (Imposto do Selo) at 0.8% on the purchase price have remained stable. The IMI (annual municipal property tax) is set by each municipality within statutory bounds. The non-habitual resident (NHR) tax regime closed to most new entrants at the end of 2023, replaced in 2024 by a more targeted regime for certain qualified research and innovation activities; buyers who are relocating for tax reasons should not assume NHR-style benefits will apply without specific professional confirmation. IRS (personal income tax) on rental income has become less attractive for non-resident landlords since the AL reforms, particularly where properties are not registered under a qualifying long-term regime.


Anti-money-laundering (AML) controls have tightened further. Bank transfers above thresholds trigger automatic reporting; the source-of-funds documentation required from non-resident buyers is more detailed than five years ago; and notaries routinely verify the provenance of funds at the deed. Buyers should expect to produce a paper trail for every euro entering the purchase, and the buyer's agent should brief clients on this expectation early — well before the bank transfer is initiated. The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) also continues to bite: the buyer's agent must hold client data under a written data-protection policy, must not share client information with the listing agent without consent, and must honour data subject access requests within statutory timelines.


On the demand side, 2026 sees continued interest from Northern European and North American buyers in the Lisbon, Porto, Silver Coast, and Algarve markets, growing interest from Brazilian buyers (often via existing community ties and tax-treaty optimisation), and a softer but still meaningful flow from Asia and the Middle East. Domestic first-time buyers, supported by the government-backed guaranteed-mortgage scheme for younger buyers (introduced in 2024 and continuing in modified form), are an increasingly competitive presence in the sub-€300k segment in and around metropolitan Lisbon and Porto. Supply remains constrained in prime central areas, with new construction hampered by heritage restrictions, infrastructure limits, and construction-cost inflation that has not fully unwound.


> ℹ️ **2026 REGULATORY SNAPSHOT**
>
> IMT: progressive rates, ~1% (lower bands) to ~8% (highest band), with a 10% top marginal rate for high-value properties and a separate regime for rural properties. Verify exact 2026 brackets against the State Budget.
> Imposto do Selo (Stamp Duty): 0.8% on the purchase price.
> IMI: 0.3%–0.45% on urban property (varies by municipality and property type); higher surcharge (AIMI) applies to high-value secondary residences owned by individuals.
> Golden visa real-estate pathway: closed to new applications since October 2023; existing permits remain valid subject to compliance.
> Alojamento Local (AL): licensing suspended and registrations restricted in designated 'containment zones' (Lisbon and Porto historic centres, parts of Algarve coast); renewals subject to municipal approval.
> NHR (Non-Habitual Resident) tax regime: closed to most new entrants; replaced by a narrow R&I regime in 2024.
> Young Buyer Mortgage Guarantee: government-backed scheme supporting first-time buyers under age 35 (subject to income and property-value caps).

# Chapter 2 — Phase 1: Pre-Engagement & Client Onboarding

## 2.1  Lead Qualification


The first contact with a prospective buyer is the qualification call. The goal is not to sell the service — it is to determine, candidly, whether the buyer is ready, willing, and able to engage, and whether the engagement is one the agent can responsibly accept. A misqualified lead absorbs enormous time and produces no transaction; worse, a wrong-fit client who does transact often becomes a liability through complaints, scope creep, or referral damage.


### 2.1.1  Qualification Framework (BANT-A)


Adapt the classic BANT framework to the Portuguese property context, with an added A for administrative readiness (NIF/bank/visa) which is the most common blocker for foreign buyers:


*Table 2.2 — BANT-A Qualification Framework*


| Dimension | Qualifying Question | Disqualifier / Caution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget | What is your all-in budget, including taxes, fees, and 12 months of carry? | Vague ranges, refusal to share, or budget incompatible with stated location/type |
| Authority | Who else is involved in the purchase decision, and how will decisions be made? | Buyer who cannot commit without absent third party (absent spouse, off-shore company) |
| Need | Why are you buying, and why now? What does success look like in 12 months? | Speculative 'maybe one day' motives with no clear trigger |
| Timeline | When do you want to be in the property, and what is the latest acceptable date? | No timeline, or timeline incompatible with the Portuguese transaction cycle (e.g., deed in 30 days from first contact) |
| Admin readiness | Do you have a NIF, a Portuguese bank account, a fiscal representative (if non-resident), and (if applicable) a visa pathway? | No NIF and no willingness to obtain one; non-EU buyer with no visa plan and no time to secure one |


> ⚠️ **COMMON QUALIFICATION PITFALLS**
>
> The 'investor' who is actually a lifestyle buyer in disguise — they will not optimise for yield and will be unhappy if you do.
> The 'cash buyer' who is actually equity-rich, cash-poor — they need to liquidate an asset before they can transact, and the timeline will slip.
> The buyer who says 'I have seen 30 properties with 5 agents' — they will likely see 30 more with you and never close. Push for clarity on what has been wrong with what they have seen.
> The buyer who demands exclusivity but refuses to sign an exclusivity agreement — a structural mismatch.


### 2.1.2  The Qualification Call — Sample Script


> **AGENT — OPENING**
>
> *"Thank you for reaching out. Before we discuss properties, I want to spend about thirty minutes understanding your situation so that I can tell you honestly whether I can help and how I would approach it. Is that okay?"*


> **AGENT — BUDGET PROBE**
>
> *"It's completely fine to give me a range. I work in all-in budgets — that is, purchase price plus taxes, notary, registry, legal, my fee, and a buffer for the first year of carrying costs. What range feels realistic for you on that basis?"*
>
> *"And separately, is that amount already liquid in a Portuguese or EU bank, or will it need to be transferred? Transfer timing matters because of AML controls."*


> **AGENT — AUTHORITY PROBE**
>
> *"Apart from yourself, who else is involved in this decision — a spouse, a partner, a parent, a financial advisor, an overseas company?"*
>
> *"Will they be on the call with me at some point? I prefer to involve them early, because in my experience the worst outcome is finding the right property and then losing it while we wait for a third party to weigh in."*


> **AGENT — NEED & TIMELINE PROBE**
>
> *"Walk me through what you want this property to do for you in five years. Is it primarily a home, an investment, a retirement base, a pied-à-terre, or some combination?"*
>
> *"What is the latest date by which you would want to have the keys in your hand? And is that date a hard deadline or a soft preference?"*


> **AGENT — ADMIN READINESS PROBE**
>
> *"Have you already got a Portuguese NIF — the tax number? If not, we can arrange that as part of onboarding."*
>
> *"Do you have a Portuguese bank account? That's a requirement for the deed in most cases."*
>
> *"If you are not resident in Portugal, do you have a fiscal representative appointed?"*
>
> *"If you are not an EU citizen, what is your visa or residency situation — will you need the property to support a residency application?"*


## 2.2  Needs Assessment & Goal Setting


The needs assessment translates the buyer's verbal description into a structured, written dossier that will drive every subsequent phase. The goal is to surface not only what the buyer says they want, but also what they actually need, what they will compromise on, and what would be a deal-breaker. The assessment is conducted across six dimensions: lifestyle objective, financial profile, location preferences, property specifications, must-haves and deal-breakers, and decision-making process.


### 2.2.1  Lifestyle Objective Typology


Portugal's foreign-buyer market segments cleanly into six lifestyle objectives, each with very different optimal property profiles. Misidentifying the objective is the single most common source of buyer dissatisfaction: a 'retirement' buyer who is sold a high-yield AL investment property, or vice versa, will be unhappy even if the property is in itself fine.


*Table 2.3 — Lifestyle Objective Typology*


| Objective | Typical Profile | Optimal Property Type | Key Risks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary residence (relocation) | Working-age professional moving with job, family, or remote business | Resale apartment or house in urban centre or suburb; 2–4 bed; close to schools/transport | Overestimating commute tolerance; underestimating summer heat in un-insulated old stock |
| Retirement base | 55+ relocating for climate, cost, safety; part- or full-year | Villa or ground-floor apartment; low-maintenance; near healthcare; mild climate | Accessibility for ageing in place; distance from family/airport; healthcare access |
| Holiday / second home | Buyer retaining primary residence elsewhere; 4–12 weeks/yr usage | Apartment in resort area or coastal town; 1–3 bed; managed building | AL income volatility; off-season isolation; management costs |
| Pure investment (yield / capital growth) | Buyer who will not personally use the property; yield-focused | New build or off-plan in high-demand rental area; 1–2 bed; AL or long-let | AL licensing risk; tenant default; oversupply in new-build zones |
| Lifestyle + light investment (hybrid) | Buyer who will use 6–10 weeks and rent the rest | 1–2 bed apartment in premium area; flexible layout; strong rental demand | Conflict between personal taste and rental optimisation; wear and tear |
| Family / education-led | Buyer relocating for children's schooling (international schools) | House near Carcavelos, Cascais, Sintra, Estoril; or Lisbon international school catchments | School-place availability; commuting from school catchment to work |


### 2.2.2  Sample Needs-Assessment Questionnaire


The questionnaire below is the structured intake form used during the second onboarding session. It is designed to be filled in collaboratively — the agent asks, the buyer answers, the agent writes. The full version appears in Appendix A; the headline questions are:

1. Budget: all-in ceiling; comfortable working range; source of funds; transfer currency; already-liquid vs. needs-sale.
1. Timeline: ideal completion date; latest acceptable date; key drivers (school year, lease expiry, retirement date, visa expiry).
1. Location: preferred regions, neighbourhoods, or postcodes; commute or proximity requirements; natural-light, view, and orientation preferences; noise tolerance.
1. Property: type (apartment, house, villa, land, mixed); minimum bedrooms/bathrooms; minimum internal area; outdoor space requirements; parking; lift; storage.
1. Condition: turnkey; minor cosmetic works acceptable; full renovation acceptable; off-plan acceptable; ruin acceptable.
1. Use: owner-occupy full-time; owner-occupy part-time; long-term let; short-term AL; mixed; commercial use.
1. Deal-breakers: features that would disqualify a property outright (no lift above 2nd floor; ground floor; busy road; leasehold; etc.).
1. Decision-making: who else is involved; how offers will be approved; how disputes between co-buyers will be resolved.
1. Risk appetite: tolerance for off-plan, for renovation, for off-market purchases, for properties with title or licensing complexity.
1. Communication: preferred channels; reporting cadence; meeting frequency; language.

## 2.3  Market & Process Education


Even sophisticated foreign buyers — and often especially sophisticated foreign buyers — arrive in Portugal with assumptions imported from their home market that simply do not hold. The market and process education session is the agent's opportunity to surface and correct these assumptions before they produce a costly mistake. The session is structured around four topics: the Portuguese agency model and the buyer's agent's role; the transaction sequence (CPCV, escritura, registo); the tax and cost environment; and the specific risks the buyer's profile creates.


### 2.3.1  Topics the Education Session Must Cover


*Table 2.4 — Mandatory Topics in the Buyer Education Session*


| Topic | What the Buyer Must Understand | Common Misconception to Correct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Agency model | Listing agent works for seller; buyer's agent works for buyer; dual agency is structurally conflicted | 'The agent at the viewing was my agent because they were nice to me.' |
| Transaction sequence | Offer → CPCV → deposit → DD → escritura → registo; each step has risks and timing | 'The offer letter is the contract — once it's accepted we're done.' |
| Deposit risk | Deposit at CPCV is forfeited if buyer defaults without legal cause; seller pays double if they default | 'The deposit is refundable if I change my mind.' |
| Taxes | IMT (1–8%), IS (0.8%), IMI (annual), AIMI surcharge for second homes; VAT on new builds | 'Property purchase tax is a flat 1% like in my home country.' |
| Title & registry | Land registry (registo predial) is the authoritative ownership record; verbal assurances mean nothing | 'If the seller says it's theirs, it is.' |
| Licensing | Properties must have a licença de utilização (or habite-se for older stock); unlicensed = un-mortgageable and uninsurable | 'The seller has lived here 30 years, so it must be legal.' |
| Condominium | Condominium fees, reserves, and minutes are material; an unhealthy condominium is a real liability | 'Condo fees are just a small monthly bill.' |
| Energy performance | Energy certificate (certificado energético) is mandatory; F/G ratings mean cold, damp, expensive homes | 'Energy rating doesn't really matter in a warm country.' |
| Heritage restrictions | Properties in classified zones (Património) have major renovation constraints | 'I'll just knock through a few walls.' |
| AML controls | Source of funds will be verified; transfers over thresholds trigger reporting | 'I'll just bring a bank draft in my suitcase.' |

## 2.4  Buyer Representation Agreement (BRA)


The Buyer Representation Agreement is the contract that defines the engagement. Without it, the agent has no enforceable right to compensation, no clear scope, and no defensible position if a dispute arises. With it, both parties have clarity on what is being done, by whom, for how long, at what cost, and on what terms. A well-drafted BRA protects the buyer as much as the agent — it specifies the agent's duties, the buyer's obligations, and the conditions under which either party can terminate.


### 2.4.1  Required Sections of the BRA


*Table 2.5 — Buyer Representation Agreement: Required Sections*


| Section | Content | Key Provisions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Parties | Buyer(s), Agent, Agency | Full legal names, AMI licence number, tax numbers, addresses |
| 2. Scope of services | What the agent will and will not do | Phase-by-phase scope (sourcing, viewings, DD coordination, negotiation, transaction management, post-purchase); inclusions and exclusions |
| 3. Geographic & property-type scope | Markets and property types covered | Regions, postcodes, property categories; explicit exclusions |
| 4. Exclusivity | Whether buyer can work with other agents | Exclusive / non-exclusive / sole; consequences of buyer-side direct purchase |
| 5. Term | Duration of the engagement | Initial term (typically 3–6 months); renewal terms; early-termination triggers |
| 6. Fees | Compensation model and trigger | Retainer, flat fee, success fee, or combination; fee trigger events; payment terms |
| 7. Termination | How either party exits | For-cause and for-convenience terms; surviving obligations post-termination (commission on transactions closed within X months) |
| 8. Fiduciary duties | Agent's duties to buyer | Loyalty, confidentiality, disclosure, accounting, obedience, reasonable care |
| 9. Buyer obligations | What the buyer must do | Provide accurate information; not negotiate directly with listing agents; honour referral of properties introduced during the term |
| 10. Conflict of interest | How conflicts are managed | Disclosure of any listing relationships; prohibition on dual agency without written consent; referral fees disclosure |
| 11. Confidentiality & GDPR | Data protection and confidentiality | What data is held, for how long, on what legal basis; data subject rights |
| 12. Dispute resolution | How disputes are resolved | Mediation first; arbitration or courts; governing law (Portuguese) |
| 13. Notices & signatures | Communication and execution | Valid notice channels; signature formalities |


### 2.4.2  Fee Models


Four fee models are in active use in the Portuguese buyer-representation market. Each has different incentive properties and suits different buyer profiles:


*Table 2.6 — Fee Models Comparison*


| Model | Structure | Best For | Risk / Caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Success fee (% of price) | 1–2.5% of purchase price, paid at deed | Standard residential; buyer wants no upfront cost | Incentive to push price up — manage by capping or fixing at offer-acceptance; conflicts with fiduciary duty if misaligned |
| Flat fee | Fixed €5k–€25k depending on scope, paid in tranches | Sophisticated buyers; transactional clarity; high-value deals where % is disproportionate | May under-incentivise complex DD work; scope creep risk |
| Retainer + reduced success fee | €1k–€5k/month retainer + 0.5–1% success | Complex/luxury/land deals requiring deep work; long timelines | Buyer bears cost even if no transaction; agent must justify with monthly deliverables |
| Value-based / savings-share | Fee = share of the saving achieved below an agreed benchmark | Sophisticated buyers; deals where the agent can demonstrably add negotiation value | Hard to benchmark; can incentivise holding out for unrealistic discounts |
| Shared listing commission | Agent accepts portion of the commission the seller pays the listing agent | Buyer wants no direct payment; common in markets where dual representation is normalised | Structural pressure on independence; must be disclosed in writing; cleaner if combined with a top-up from buyer |


> ⚠️ **FEE-MODEL CONFLICTS**
>
> A pure success fee as a percentage of price creates a structural incentive for the agent to favour higher-priced properties and to discourage the buyer from walking away. Cap the fee, fix it at offer acceptance, or use a flat-fee model for higher-priced transactions.
> Shared listing commission without a written disclosure and a buyer-side fiduciary waiver is a regulatory and ethical hazard in Portugal. If you take seller-side money, document the arrangement and reaffirm the buyer-side duty in writing.
> Retainer models must deliver monthly written work product to justify the retainer. A retainer without deliverables becomes a source of buyer resentment by month three.

## 2.5  Initial Administrative Setup


In parallel with the engagement paperwork, the buyer must complete a set of administrative prerequisites that are mandatory for any Portuguese property transaction. Foreign buyers in particular should begin these immediately, because each one has its own processing timeline and any one of them can become the bottleneck that delays a deed.


### 2.5.1  NIF (Número de Identificação Fiscal)


The NIF is the Portuguese tax identification number. It is required for every property transaction, every utility contract, every bank account, and most official interactions. A Portuguese resident obtains one at any tax office (Autoridade Tributária / AT) or via the Portal das Finanças; a non-resident must appoint a fiscal representative (see 2.5.3) and apply through them, or apply in person at a Portuguese consulate or AT office with their passport and proof of address. Processing time is typically same-day for residents and 5–15 business days for non-residents via representative. The buyer's agent should help coordinate this process and verify the NIF has been issued before the search begins in earnest — the NIF is required to make a formal offer and to open the bank account.


### 2.5.2  Portuguese Bank Account


A Portuguese bank account is required to transfer the purchase funds and to pay taxes at the deed. Most major banks (Caixa Geral de Depósitos, Millennium BCP, Santander, Novo Banco, and digital banks such as ActivoBank) open accounts for non-residents, subject to enhanced AML checks. The buyer should expect to provide: passport; proof of address (home country); proof of income or source of funds; NIF; tax residency certificate from home country (where applicable); and a meeting (in person or via video, depending on bank). Account opening typically takes 1–3 weeks; the buyer's agent should pre-introduce the buyer to a banker familiar with non-resident property buyers to streamline the process.


### 2.5.3  Fiscal Representative


Non-resident buyers (and any non-resident entity holding Portuguese property) must appoint a fiscal representative — an individual or company resident in Portugal that receives tax correspondence on the non-resident's behalf and is the contact point for the Autoridade Tributária. The fiscal representative is a legal requirement, not an optional convenience. Many lawyers, accountants, and dedicated service companies offer this for €100–€500 per year. The buyer's agent should not act as fiscal representative (conflict of interest and span-of-control concerns) but should introduce 2–3 reputable options.


### 2.5.4  Residency & Visa Pathways


For non-EU buyers, the property purchase may form part of a residency strategy, but it is essential to be clear that — since the October 2023 closure of the real-estate golden visa pathway — purchasing property no longer provides a direct route to a Portuguese residency permit for new applicants. Existing golden visa holders retain their rights subject to compliance and renewal. The principal current pathways for non-EU buyers are summarised below; the buyer's agent should refer the buyer to a specialist immigration lawyer for definitive advice and should not attempt to provide that advice directly.


*Table 2.7 — Residency Pathways for Non-EU Buyers (2026)*


| Pathway | Eligibility | Property Relevance | Indicative Timeline |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D7 (Passive income) | Non-EU with regular passive income (pensions, rentals, dividends) above Portuguese minimum wage | Property can support the address requirement; rental income can contribute to qualifying income | Application 30–60 days; residence permit 60–120 days |
| D8 (Digital Nomad) | Non-EU remote workers with income ≥ 4× Portuguese minimum wage | Property provides the Portuguese address; not a qualifying investment in itself | Application 30–60 days; residence permit 60–120 days |
| Work permit (Employer-sponsored) | Non-EU with a Portuguese employment contract | Property provides the address; not a qualifying investment in itself | Application 30–90 days; residence permit 60–120 days |
| EU Blue Card | Highly-qualified non-EU with qualifying employment | Property provides the address; not a qualifying investment in itself | Application 30–60 days |
| Family reunification | Family member of Portuguese or EU resident | Property supports the address and space requirements | Application 60–120 days |
| Existing Golden Visa holders | Holders of residence permits granted under the pre-Oct-2023 regime | Property already acquired; renewals subject to compliance | Renewal 60–120 days |

*Indicative timelines are administrative processing estimates and may vary. The buyer's agent must not provide immigration advice; refer to a specialist immigration lawyer.*


> 🚩 **PHASE 1 RED FLAGS**
>
> Buyer refuses to share budget, source of funds, or NIF — do not engage.
> Buyer insists on an extremely compressed timeline (e.g., 'I need to buy next week') — usually a sign of either unrealistic expectations or undisclosed urgency that will produce a bad decision.
> Buyer asks the agent to handle cash payments or to structure the transaction to obscure source of funds — refuse and walk away; this is an AML offence.
> Buyer wants to use an offshore company with opaque ownership — require enhanced DD and refer to a lawyer before signing the BRA.
> Buyer is being pressured by a third party (developer, foreign agent, family member) and seems unable to make independent decisions — slow down, document, and consider declining.
> Buyer requests the agent to act as fiscal representative or to receive client funds into the agent's personal account — refuse; this is a structural conflict and an AML violation.

# Chapter 3 — Phase 2: Research, Strategy & Property Sourcing


With a signed Buyer Representation Agreement, an active client dossier, and the administrative prerequisites in motion, Phase 2 turns the buyer's stated requirements into a structured market intelligence operation. The phase has three subtasks: building a deep, current picture of the relevant market segment; developing a multi-channel sourcing strategy that includes — but is not limited to — public portals; and constructing a written search brief that translates the buyer dossier into the filter the agent will apply daily. Done well, Phase 2 produces a steady, qualified pipeline of 8–20 candidate properties within four to six weeks and an off-market discovery rate of 15–30% of what is eventually shown.


## 3.1  Deep Market Analysis


The buyer's agent must enter the sourcing phase with a defensible, evidence-based view of the target segment: what is on the market, at what price, with what velocity, and against what recent comparable transactions. This is not a one-time exercise — it is a continuously updated intelligence picture that feeds every property recommendation, every offer justification, and every negotiation stance. A market analysis built on the listing portals alone is structurally biased, because portals show asking prices, not transaction prices, and do not reflect the 15–35% of transactions that occur off-market or via direct developer sales.

> ℹ️ **BUILDING A COMPARABLE SET**
>
> 1. Pull the last 12–24 months of registered transactions in the target micro-market via the Casa Pronta portal (where available) or via lawyer / notary contacts.
> 2. Filter by property type, size band (±20% of target), and condition (where discernible).
> 3. Adjust for orientation, floor, view, lift, parking, condition, and time-of-sale to build a price-per-m² range.
> 4. Compare the asking price of any candidate property against this range. Asking prices 10%+ above the comparable ceiling require explicit justification.
> 5. Document the comparable set in the buyer dossier; it will be the foundation of the offer in Phase 6.


## 3.2  Comprehensive Sourcing Strategy


Sourcing is multi-channel by design. A buyer's agent who relies on the public portals alone will see the same properties every other agent and every direct buyer sees — and will compete for them on the listing agent's terms. The defensible sourcing stack has five layers, used simultaneously: portal scanning, listing-agent network, off-market direct-to-owner, developer-direct (for new build and off-plan), and bank/asset-manager repossessions. A mature practice typically sources 40–60% of shown properties via portals, 20–30% via agent network referrals, 10–20% off-market, and the balance via developers and institutional sellers.

### 3.2.2  Off-Market Sourcing — Tactics and Ethics


Off-market stock is the layer where the buyer's agent adds the most distinctive value, because it is invisible to direct buyers and to less-connected agents. It is also the layer that requires the most discipline, because off-market does not mean uncompetitive — it means the seller has chosen a quiet, controlled sale, and the buyer's agent must respect that. Three off-market tactics are reliable:

- Micro-market letter drops: For a buyer committed to a specific street, building, or micro-area, a discreet, well-written letter to owners of comparable properties can produce 1–3 serious responses from owners who had been considering selling but had not listed. The letter must be honest about who is writing, why, and what is being offered; speculative or misleading letters damage reputation.
- Professional network referrals: Lawyers, notaries, accountants, tax advisors, and architects routinely know of upcoming sales — particularly inheritance and divorce situations where the seller wants discretion. A buyer's agent who builds and maintains these relationships receives a steady flow of off-market leads. The relationship is reciprocal: the agent refers business back, and treats each referral with discretion.
- Door-knocking and building-level enquiries: In buildings with desirable units, a direct enquiry to the condominium administrator or to owners in the building can identify a willing seller who has not formalised the decision. This is labour-intensive and is used selectively for high-value, specific-buyer mandates.

> ⚠️ **OFF-MARKET DISCIPLINE**
>
> Off-market is not a synonym for 'below market'. Off-market sellers typically expect a price comparable to or above portal-listed comparable properties, in exchange for discretion and certainty. Never promise a buyer that off-market means a discount.
> Respect seller confidentiality. Do not share off-market property details with other buyers; do not photograph or distribute without the seller's written permission.
> Document the off-market introduction in writing to the seller's representative, to protect the buyer's agent's commission position if the buyer later attempts to bypass.

# Chapter 4 — Phase 3: Evaluation, Viewings & Shortlisting


Phase 3 takes the candidate pipeline built in Phase 2 and converts it into a shortlist of two to four properties that will move into due diligence. The phase has three subtasks: viewing preparation and execution (virtual and in-person), on-site inspection using property-type-customised checklists, and structured debrief and shortlisting using a scoring matrix. The agent's discipline in this phase protects the buyer from emotional commitment before technical questions are answered, and produces a written record that supports the eventual offer and negotiation.


## 4.1  Viewing Preparation


Every viewing should be preceded by 30–60 minutes of preparation: a desktop review of the property's documents, comparable pricing, and known risks. Walking into a viewing unprepared is the most common source of wasted buyer time and of avoidable surprises. The preparation also signals to the listing agent that the buyer is serious and represented — which subtly shifts the negotiation dynamic from the first contact.


### 4.1.1  Pre-Viewing Document Request


Before the first in-person viewing, request the following documents from the listing agent. A serious, well-represented seller will provide these within 24–48 hours; resistance or delay is itself a flag.


*Table 4.1 — Pre-Viewing Document Request*


| Document | Purpose | What to Look For |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caderneta predial urbana | Property tax record — confirms area, ownership, fiscal description | Owner name; consistent area with listing; no anomalies |
| Certidão predial permanente (or transcript) | Land registry — confirms title and encumbrances | Clean title; no liens; no usucapião actions; no reservations |
| Licença de utilização (or habite-se for older stock) | Mandatory use licence; required for mortgage and insurance | Issued; matches current use; matches property type |
| Certificado energético (EPC) | Mandatory energy performance certificate | Rating; recommendations; date issued (must be <10 years) |
| Ficha técnica de habitação (post-2004 new builds) | Technical data sheet | Construction system; materials; responsibility |
| Condominium documents (apartments) | Last 2 years of minutes; budget; reserve fund; debt certificate | Reserve adequacy; pending works; owner arrears |
| Floor plans (plantas) | Layout, area verification | Licensed area matches actual; no unpermitted alterations |
| Recent utility bills | Confirmation of service; running-cost ballpark | Reasonable consumption; no shared metering |


### 4.1.2  Pre-Viewing Desktop Research


Beyond the seller-provided documents, the agent should conduct independent desktop research before the viewing:

- Google Street View history — examine the street over multiple dates for parking patterns, construction activity, neighbouring commercial turnover, and visible changes to the building facade.
- Câmara Municipal geoportal — confirm zoning, heritage classification, planned public works (road changes, tram lines, metro extensions), and any pending planning applications on or near the property.
- Recent comparable listings — pull 3–5 comparable properties currently or recently listed within 500m to triangulate the asking price.
- Noise and environment — check aircraft noise contours (for properties near airports), rail and metro line proximity, and any known environmental contamination (industrial sites, petrol stations).
- Condominium online footprint — for apartments, search the building address in news and complaint forums for any history of construction defects, disputes, or insurance claims.

> Energy certificate is older than 5 years or pre-dates recent renovation — request a fresh certificate; the EPC is mandatory and the cost is the seller's.

# Chapter 5 — Phase 4: Comprehensive Due Diligence


Due diligence is the phase where the buyer either confirms that the property is what it appears to be, or discovers that it is not. It runs across four tracks — legal, technical, financial, and regulatory — and the buyer's agent's role is to coordinate the tracks, ensure no item is dropped, and report progress to the buyer on a fixed cadence. In an ideal transaction, due diligence begins before the CPCV is signed (so that major red flags surface first) and continues for the post-CPCV period under well-drafted condition precedents. In practice, the bulk of formal DD runs in the 30–90 days between CPCV and escritura, which makes the CPCV's protective clauses the buyer's primary safety net.


*Table 5.1 — Due Diligence Tracks and Owners*


| Track | Owner | Key Counterparties | Typical Duration |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Legal DD | Buyer's lawyer (advogado) | Land registry, tax authority, condominium, listing agent | 2–6 weeks |
| Technical DD | Buyer's agent (coordination) + surveyor / engineer | Surveyor, engineer, architect, energy certifier | 1–3 weeks (parallel) |
| Financial DD | Buyer + mortgage broker + bank | Bank, broker, currency hedging provider | 2–6 weeks (parallel) |
| Regulatory DD | Buyer's lawyer + buyer's agent | Câmara Municipal, IGESPAR, APA, RNAAL | 2–4 weeks (parallel) |


## 5.1  Legal Due Diligence


Legal DD is the buyer's lawyer's domain; the buyer's agent does not perform legal DD but coordinates with the lawyer, ensures the document set is complete, and tracks open items. The lawyer must be independent — meaning not referred by the listing agent and not connected to the seller or developer — and registered with the Ordem dos Advogados. The agent's role is project management: scheduling, document chasing, status tracking, and translation of legal findings into actionable buyer decisions.

> The lawyer is engaged directly by the buyer, not by the agent. The agent introduces 2–3 vetted options and steps back from the engagement letter.
> The lawyer must be independent: not referred by the listing agent, not connected to the seller or developer, not also acting for the seller on any transaction.
> All documents flow to the lawyer; the agent does not filter, summarise, or interpret. The agent's role is to chase missing documents and to translate the lawyer's findings into action items for the buyer.
> The lawyer reports in writing to the buyer; the agent is copied on operational items (timeline, document chasing, status) but not on legal advice.
> If the lawyer and the listing agent know each other socially or professionally, this is normal in a small market — but the lawyer must confirm in writing that they are acting exclusively for the buyer on this transaction.


## 5.2  Technical Due Diligence


Technical DD verifies the physical condition of the property and quantifies any work required. It runs in parallel with legal DD and is coordinated by the buyer's agent, who commissions reports from independent specialists. Three reports form the standard package for any pre-1990 or visibly aged property: structural survey, energy performance review, and (for apartments) a condominium works-history audit. For new builds, the snagging inspection replaces the structural survey and is conducted shortly before handover. For land and rural properties, a topographic and geotechnical survey is added.


### 5.2.1  Structural Survey Scope


The structural survey is conducted by an independent civil engineer (engenheiro civil) or architect. It is not the same as the energy certificate or the ficha técnica — it is an independent assessment of the building's structural and envelope condition. The scope must cover:

- Foundations and load-bearing structure (visible elements; accessible basements; crack patterns mapped).
- Walls (plumbness; cracks; damp penetration; rising damp; efflorescence).
- Floors (level; deflection; timber decay; concrete spalling).
- Roof and roof structure (water ingress; structural integrity; insulation).
- Facades (cracks; render condition; pointing; water damage; evidence of patch repairs).
- Openings (window/door frames; operation; seals; signs of water ingress).
- Installations (electrical; plumbing; gas; heating; ventilation — visible portions only).
- Site and drainage (for houses and rural: surface water management; retaining walls; erosion).
- Indicative remediation costs and a 1–10 risk rating.

### 5.2.2  Energy Performance Review


The energy certificate (EPC) is mandatory and valid for 10 years, but a 2026 buyer should treat any EPC older than 5 years, or any EPC issued before a major renovation, as needing refresh. Beyond the EPC itself, an independent thermal review (typically €250–€600) is recommended when: the EPC is D or lower; major renovation is contemplated; or the buyer is sensitive to running costs and comfort. The thermal review quantifies heat loss, identifies the highest-ROI interventions (typically wall insulation, double-glazing, and solar water heating), and produces an indicative payback calculation that informs both the offer and the post-purchase renovation plan.


### 5.2.3  Condominium Works-History Audit (Apartments)


For apartment purchases, the technical DD includes a structured review of the condominium's works history: what major works have been executed in the last 10–15 years, what is planned, what is funded, and what is the realistic 5-year capital expenditure forecast. This is a softer exercise than a structural survey but is the single most reliable predictor of special-assessment risk. The buyer's agent and lawyer jointly review the last 3–5 years of condominium minutes and the reserve fund balance, and the agent produces a written summary for the buyer.


*Table 5.4 — Condominium Health Scoring*


| Indicator | Healthy | Caution | Red Flag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Reserve fund per unit | >€2,000 | €500–€2,000 | <€500 |
| Owner arrears rate | <5% | 5–15% | >15% |
| Recent special assessments | 0 in last 5 years | 1 in last 5 years | >1 in last 5 years or current |
| Pending major works (next 24 months) | None or fully funded | Planned, partly funded | Approved, unfunded |
| Insurance coverage | Full building + common areas | Adequate but dated | Underinsured or lapsed |
| Owner-occupier ratio | >70% | 50–70% | <50% |
| Recent litigation vs condominium | None | Minor / settled | Active / material |


## 5.3  Financial Due Diligence


Financial DD verifies that the buyer can complete the purchase on the planned timeline and that the cost structure is fully understood. It runs in parallel with the legal and technical tracks and is coordinated by the buyer's agent with the buyer's bank, mortgage broker, and (where applicable) currency hedging provider. The buyer's agent does not provide financial advice but ensures that all required documentation is in the bank's hands, that the appraisal is scheduled, and that the formal mortgage offer is issued in time for the deed.


### 5.3.1  Financial DD Checklist

- Confirm purchase funding sources (cash, mortgage, combination) in writing.
- If mortgage: submit complete application to bank (income proof, tax returns, bank statements, property documents, valuation fee).
- Coordinate bank appraisal (avaliação); verify appraised value supports both the loan-to-value and the purchase price.
- Obtain formal mortgage offer (carta de aprovação) before CPCV signing or with financing condition precedent in CPCV.
- If non-EUR buyer: confirm currency transfer plan, FX spread, hedging strategy (forward contract if material).
- Verify IMT and IS calculations (Chapter 6); confirm funds available for payment at deed.
- Verify notary, registry, and lawyer fees; confirm payment arrangements.
- Verify buyer's agent fee trigger and payment terms.
- Build 5–10% contingency buffer for unexpected costs (DD findings, exchange-rate movement, post-deed works).

> ℹ️ **MORTGAGE COORDINATION STANDARD**
>
> Engage the bank BEFORE the CPCV is signed, not after. Most banks take 4–8 weeks from application to formal offer; CPCV with a financing condition precedent must allow at least this long.
> The bank's appraisal is the bank's, not the buyer's. A low appraisal does not change the price but reduces the loan-to-value, increasing the buyer's cash requirement.
> Maintain at least two bank conversations in parallel; rates, fees, and LTV policies vary materially between Portuguese banks and across the buyer's residency status.
> Non-resident buyers face tighter LTV (typically 60–70% vs 80–90% for residents), higher spreads, and additional documentation requirements.


## 5.4  Regulatory Due Diligence


Regulatory DD verifies that the property complies with the applicable regulatory regimes and that the buyer's intended use is permitted. It overlaps with legal DD but is distinct in focus: where legal DD asks 'is the title clean?', regulatory DD asks 'is the use legal and the alteration plan feasible?'. The four principal regulatory regimes are urbanistic (zoning, buildability, heritage), environmental (contamination, protected habitats, forestry), licensing (use licences, AL licences, professional-use licences), and tax (IMT, IS, IMI, AIMI, IRS/IRC implications).


### 5.4.1  Regulatory DD Checklist by Topic


*Table 5.5 — Regulatory DD Checklist*


| Topic | Verification | Authority / Source | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PDM zoning | Plot's urbanistic classification; permitted uses; buildability parameters | Câmara Municipal geoportal / Plano Director Municipal | Use not permitted; buildability lower than assumed |
| Heritage classification | Property or zone classified (IGESPAR / DGPC); restrictions on alteration | Património Cultural / IGESPAR portal; Câmara | Renovation severely restricted; facade and material constraints |
| Environmental constraints | Protected habitat; contamination (industrial past); watercourse protection | APA (Agência Portuguesa do Ambiente); Natura 2000 | Construction prohibited; remediation required; use restricted |
| Forestry compliance (rural) | Eucalyptus/cleanance; fire-break; protected species | ICNF; AFRC | Mandatory clearance; fire-risk construction restrictions |

| Condominium rules | Use restrictions (no AL; pet restrictions; commercial use) | Condominium regulation | Buyer's intended use prohibited |

| Construction permits (planned works) | Permit feasibility; timeline; neighbour notifications | Câmara; architect | Permit not granted; timeline longer than expected; opposition |


## 5.5  Risk Assessment Matrix


The findings of all four DD tracks must be consolidated into a single risk assessment matrix that the buyer can use to make a go / renegotiate / walk decision. The matrix scores each identified risk by likelihood and impact, and assigns an owner and a treatment. The matrix is reviewed jointly with the buyer and is the basis for any renegotiation of price, conditions, or walk-away decision.


*Table 5.6 — Risk Assessment Matrix Template*


| Risk | Likelihood (1–5) | Impact (1–5) | Score (L×I) | Treatment | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Title defect (inheritance gap) | 3 | 5 | 15 | Cure registration pre-deed; seller bears cost | Lawyer |

### 5.5.1  DD Reporting Cadence


The buyer's agent maintains a live DD tracker and reports to the buyer on a fixed cadence regardless of whether there is news — silence breeds anxiety. The recommended cadence is:

- Twice-weekly 15-minute status call (Tuesday and Friday).
- Weekly written status report: items closed, items open, items blocked, items at risk.
- Immediate notification (within 4 hours) of any red-flag finding.
- Joint review with buyer at 50% DD completion, 80% DD completion, and pre-deed.

> 🚩 **PHASE 4 RED FLAGS — MASTER LIST**
>
> Seller cannot produce a clean certidão predial within 7 days of CPCV — usually indicates unresolved title, inheritance, or encumbrance.
> Property's actual area differs from the caderneta predial by >5% — unlicensed alteration; significant regularisation risk.
> Condominium refuses to issue a debt certificate — almost certainly hiding arrears or unfunded works.
> AL licence has been suspended, expired, or is in a non-renewable zone — material to any rental case.
> Seller is a recently-formed company with no operating history — possible flip structure; investigate beneficial ownership.
> Listing agent pushes to set the escritura date within 30 days of CPCV without DD basis — pressure tactic to compress DD.
> Property was renovated in the last 5 years but has no ficha técnica, no permits, and no invoices — unpermitted work; future liability.
> Energy certificate is missing or expired — mandatory document; sale cannot proceed without it.
> Seller is in arrears on IMI or other property taxes — AT lien attaches to the property; verify cancellation at deed.
> Lawyer reports 'minor issue' without a written opinion — every issue must be documented in writing.
> Listing agent claims 'the bank will accept' the property without the buyer's own bank confirmation — bank appraisal is the buyer's bank's, not the listing agent's.

# Chapter 6 — Phase 5: Financial Planning & Structuring


Phase 5 builds the financial picture the buyer needs to make a confident offer and to complete the transaction without cash-flow surprises. It has three subtasks: a complete total-cost-of-ownership calculation that captures every euro from the offer to the second year of ownership; a financing coordination process that ensures funds are in place for the deed; and a tax-optimisation review that structures the transaction to minimise transfer and ongoing taxes within the law. The buyer's agent does not provide tax or financial advice — that is the role of the buyer's lawyer and tax advisor — but coordinates the inputs and ensures the buyer sees the full picture before the offer is finalised.


## 6.1  Total Cost of Ownership


The single most common budgeting error among foreign buyers in Portugal is to plan around the purchase price alone. The all-in cost of acquiring and owning a Portuguese property includes, at minimum, the purchase price, IMT, stamp duty, notary, registry, lawyer, buyer's agent fee, mortgage arrangement fee, condominium (where applicable), IMI, utilities activation, insurance, and 12 months of routine maintenance. For older properties, a renovation reserve should be added. For new builds, VAT is included in the price but should be confirmed. > ℹ️ **TCO RULE OF THUMB**
>
> For a typical resale apartment acquisition, total one-time transaction costs (excluding mortgage and renovation) are approximately 8–11% of the purchase price: IMT (1–8%) + IS (0.8%) + notary (€500–€1,500) + registry (€200–€600) + lawyer (1–1.5%) + agent (1–2.5%, if buyer-paid).
> For a new build, IMT and IS still apply on the price (which includes VAT). VAT is paid to the developer at the standard 23% rate on residential construction (often blended into the asking price).
> Ongoing annual ownership costs (excluding mortgage) typically run 1.5–3% of property value.


## 6.2  Mortgage & Financing Coordination

The buyer's agent's role is not to provide mortgage advice but to coordinate the process so that the bank, the lawyer, and the listing agent are aligned on timeline and documentation. A mortgage application typically takes 4–8 weeks from submission to formal offer; an additional 1–2 weeks is required to issue final instructions to the notary for the deed. The buyer's agent should manage the mortgage timeline against the CPCV's financing condition precedent.

### 6.2.3  Non-EUR Buyer Currency Considerations


For buyers whose wealth is in a currency other than the euro, currency risk is a material financial planning dimension. Between the CPCV (when the deposit is paid) and the escritura (when the balance is paid), exchange-rate movements of 3–8% are not uncommon. A 5% adverse move on a €700,000 purchase is €35,000 — typically larger than the buyer's agent fee. The buyer's agent should ensure that the buyer understands the options: a spot transfer for smaller amounts; a forward contract (locking today's rate for a future transfer) for larger amounts; or a multi-currency account to spread transfers. None of these is advice — they are options the buyer should evaluate with an FX provider or their bank.


> ⚠️ **FINANCING COORDINATION PITFALLS**
>
> Setting the escritura date before the formal mortgage offer is in hand — high risk of postponement or forfeit.
> Assuming the bank's appraisal will equal the purchase price — appraisals frequently come in 5–15% below agreed price; the buyer must cover the gap.
> Underestimating documentation requirements for self-employed or non-resident buyers — banks request extensive income and source-of-funds proof; start early.
> Paying IMT and IS before the bank has issued the formal mortgage offer — if the mortgage is declined, the buyer has paid non-refundable taxes on a transaction that may not proceed.
> Forgetting that the bank's valuation is the bank's — the buyer may need a separate, independent valuation if challenging the bank appraisal.


## 6.3  Tax Optimisation


The buyer's agent does not provide tax advice — that is the role of the buyer's lawyer and tax advisor (TOC / contabilista certificado). The agent's role is to ensure the buyer is aware of the principal tax considerations, knows which questions to ask the advisor, and structures the engagement of advisors early enough for the planning to have effect. The headline tax considerations for 2026 are summarised below; definitive figures must be re-verified against the year's State Budget and the buyer's specific circumstances.


### 6.3.1  IMT (Imposto Municipal sobre as Transmissões)


IMT is the municipal transfer tax paid by the buyer before the deed. For urban property intended for own permanent residence (habitação própria e permanente), the 2026 progressive brackets (illustrative — verify against current State Budget) apply as follows:


*Table 6.5 — IMT Brackets (Own Permanent Residence, Indicative 2026)*


| Tier (€) | Marginal Rate | Example Property Price | Approximate IMT Due |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Up to ~€100,000 | 1% | €95,000 | €950 |
| €100,001 – €150,000 | 2% | €130,000 | €1,600 |
| €150,001 – €200,000 | 3% | €180,000 | €2,500 |
| €200,001 – €250,000 | 4% | €230,000 | €4,000 |
| €250,001 – €300,000 | 5% | €280,000 | €6,000 |
| €300,001 – €550,000 | 6% | €450,000 | €18,500 |
| €550,001 – €650,000 | 7% | €600,000 | €42,000 |
| €650,001 – €750,000 | 8% | €700,000 | €50,000 |
| €750,001 and above | Single rate 10% on full amount | €1,000,000 | €100,000 |


*Indicative only — verify exact brackets and any 2026 changes against the Lei do Orçamento do Estado. The single 10% rate applies to the entire price above the top threshold, not just the excess.*


For secondary residences and investment properties, the rates are higher (approximately 1% additional at most brackets). For rural properties, IMT is typically 5% flat. IMT is paid via the DUIC (Documento Único de Cobrança) before the deed; the deed cannot proceed without proof of payment.


### 6.3.2  Imposto do Selo (Stamp Duty)


Imposto do Selo is levied at 0.8% on the purchase price (or on the value declared, whichever is higher). It is paid alongside IMT via the DUIC. Some transactions (e.g., certain intra-family transfers) may attract different rates; the buyer's lawyer confirms.


### 6.3.3  IMI (Imposto Municipal sobre Imóveis) — Annual


IMI is the annual municipal property tax, paid by the owner on 31 December each year. The rate is set by each municipality within statutory bounds: 0.3–0.45% for urban property; 0.8% for rustic property. The tax base is the property's taxable value (Valor Patrimonial Tributário, VPT), which is calculated from the caderneta predial and is typically 50–80% of market value. Some municipalities offer reductions for low-income owners, families with children, and energy-efficient properties (eficiência energética) — verify with the local Câmara.


### 6.3.4  AIMI (Adicional ao IMI) — Wealth Surcharge


AIMI is a surcharge on high-value residential property owned by individuals (not companies; companies pay IMI at 1.5% on full asset value). The 2026 indicative thresholds and rates:


*Table 6.6 — AIMI Indicative Brackets (2026)*


| Aggregate VPT of Residential Property (Individual) | AIMI Rate | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Up to €600,000 | 0% | No AIMI due |
| €600,001 – €1,000,000 | 0.7% | On the value in this band |
| €1,000,001 – €2,000,000 | 1.0% | On the value in this band |
| €2,000,001 and above | 1.5% | On the value above €2m |


*Indicative only. AIMI aggregates all residential property VPT owned by the individual. Companies holding residential property pay 1.5% on the full VPT (no threshold). Trust and comparable structures may attract special treatment.*


### 6.3.5  VAT (IVA) on New Builds


For new builds (first transmission within 5 years of completion), the transaction is subject to VAT (IVA) at 23% on the construction value, not IMT on that portion. The VAT is typically embedded in the asking price. The land portion of the price may still attract IMT. The buyer's lawyer and tax advisor confirm the split and ensure VAT is correctly invoiced. For subsequent resales within the 5-year window, the VAT treatment depends on the seller's VAT status.


### 6.3.6  Tax Structuring Considerations


> ℹ️ **TAX STRUCTURING QUESTIONS FOR THE BUYER'S ADVISOR**
>
> Should the property be acquired in individual name(s), a Portuguese company, a foreign company, or a trust/foundation structure? Each has different IMT, AIMI, IRS/IRC, and inheritance implications.
> If the buyer is non-resident, what are the IRS implications of any rental income, and what withholding taxes apply at source?
> If the buyer intends to use the property for AL (short-term rental), what are the categorisation (estabelecimento) and tax-banding implications? How do the post-Maisis-Habitação AL changes affect this?
> If the buyer will inherit the property to non-Portuguese heirs, what are the inheritance tax (Imposto do Selo sobre herança) implications — generally 0% for spouses/descendants, 10% for others — and what planning (e.g., Portuguese will) is appropriate?
> If the buyer's home country has a tax treaty with Portugal, how does double taxation on rental income and capital gains get resolved?
> If the buyer is eligible for any of the regional tax regimes (Madeira IBC, Azores), how do these affect the analysis?
> What is the optimal timing of the deed (end of year vs start of year) for IMI proration and AIMI inclusion?


> ⚠️ **TAX-PLANNING DISCIPLINE**
>
> Tax structuring must be done BEFORE the CPCV is signed, not after. Post-CPCV restructuring is expensive and may attract additional taxes.
> The NHR (Non-Habitual Resident) regime closed to most new entrants at the end of 2023. Buyers relocating in 2026 should not assume NHR benefits; the replacement R&I regime is narrower.
> Company structures have higher ongoing compliance costs (IRC, IES, accounting). The AIMI saving is real but is offset by these costs for smaller holdings.
> Offshore structures in blacklisted jurisdictions attract penal tax treatment in Portugal (30%+ withholding rates). Use EU/EEA structures or properly cleared intermediaries.
> All tax planning must be documented in writing by the buyer's tax advisor; the buyer's agent does not implement tax structures.

# Chapter 7 — Phase 6: Negotiation, Offer & Contracting


Phase 6 is where the buyer's agent adds the most visible economic value. The phase has three subtasks: developing and presenting the offer (Proposta de Compra), drafting and reviewing the promissory contract (Contrato de Promessa de Compra e Venda, CPCV) with strong protective clauses, and protecting the deposit through escrow and well-drafted condition precedents. The Portuguese transaction culture is generally polite and relationship-driven; aggressive Anglo-Saxon negotiation tactics often backfire. The agent's job is to combine firm, evidence-based price justification with respect for the cultural norms — and to ensure that every economic concession is matched by a contractual protection.


## 7.1  Offer Strategy and Tactics


A Portuguese offer is a written document, not a verbal indication. It is normally transmitted to the listing agent, who forwards it to the seller. The offer should be accompanied by a written justification (comparable set, condition analysis, DD findings) and, in the strongest cases, by evidence of funds and a written lawyer engagement letter. The signal sent by a well-documented offer is itself a negotiation tool: it tells the seller that the buyer is serious, represented, and capable of completing.


### 7.1.1  Offer Pricing — Analytical Framework


The offer price should be derived from a defensible comparable analysis, not from the asking price or from the buyer's gut. The framework below produces a pricing range and a recommended offer.


*Table 7.1 — Offer Pricing Framework*


| Step | Analysis | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Comparable transactions | Last 12–24 months; same micro-market; ±20% size; same condition band | Comparable price/m² range; e.g., €5,800–€6,400/m² |
| 2. Asking price comparison | Compare target asking price vs comparable ceiling | Below / within / above market |
| 3. Time-on-market analysis | Days listed; price-reduction history; seller motivation indicators | Discount potential assessment |
| 4. Property-specific adjustments | Floor, orientation, view, condition, condo health, energy rating | Per-property price adjustment |
| 5. DD findings impact | Cost of remediation; risk adjustment for unresolved issues | Price reduction or condition precedents |
| 6. Market context | Buyer vs seller market; competing offers; season | Aggressive vs conservative stance |
| 7. Recommended offer range | Below comparable midpoint (aggressive) to comparable midpoint (fair) | Offer price + ceiling + walk-away |

### 7.1.4  Negotiation Scripts


> **AGENT — TO LISTING AGENT (OFFER PRESENTATION)**
>
> *"I'm sending you a written offer for [property]. Before I send it, I want to give you the headline: it's [€X], which is [Y]% below asking. The offer is supported by a comparable set I'd be glad to walk you through — three transactions in the last 18 months within 500 metres, all in similar condition, at an average of €[Z]/m². My buyer is [cash / pre-approved mortgage], lawyer engaged, and ready to move to CPCV within three weeks. The offer is conditional on satisfactory DD. I'm not in a rush, but my buyer is serious. Can I send it to you now and discuss with the seller tonight or tomorrow?"*


> **AGENT — TO LISTING AGENT (COUNTERING A 'THERE'S ANOTHER OFFER' CLAIM)**
>
> *"I appreciate you mentioning the other offer. I'm sure you understand that my obligation is to my buyer, and we cannot bid against ourselves. If there is a written, signed offer at a higher price, the seller should accept it. If there isn't, our offer stands at €[X] for [48 hours]. We're not going to increase without a written counter from the seller. I'm confident in our pricing — the comparable set is solid — and my buyer is happy to walk away and look at the other two properties on the shortlist."*


> **AGENT — TO LISTING AGENT (MANAGING URGENCY)**
>
> *"I understand the seller wants to move quickly. I'd rather we agreed the right price now than rush to a CPCV that falls apart in DD. My buyer is genuinely committed, but we will not be pressured into waiving DD. If the seller genuinely cannot wait 60 days for DD, then this isn't the right property for us, and I'd rather we end the conversation cleanly. If they can, let's get the CPCV drafted."*


> ⚠️ **NEGOTIATION ANTI-PATTERNS**
>
> Bidding against yourself. If the listing agent says 'there's another offer' but cannot produce written evidence, hold your price. Increase only in response to a written counter from the seller.
> Disclosing the buyer's maximum. Never reveal the buyer's ceiling. The agent's job is to negotiate to the lowest acceptable price; the ceiling exists only as an internal walk-away.
> Waiving DD to make the offer 'more attractive'. A seller who accepts a DD-free offer is a seller who knows DD will surface problems. Always insist on DD.
> Skipping the offer letter and going straight to CPCV. The offer letter creates the audit trail and the basis for the CPCV.
> Negotiating emotionally. Even if the seller or listing agent is rude or aggressive, the buyer's agent stays professional. Emotional responses cost the buyer money.


## 7.2  Promissory Contract (CPCV) — Drafting & Review


The CPCV is the binding bilateral contract that commits both parties to the transaction. Once signed and the deposit paid, the buyer forfeits the deposit if they default without legal justification; the seller pays double the deposit if they default. The CPCV must therefore be drafted with great care and reviewed by the buyer's lawyer. The buyer's agent coordinates the drafting (typically the seller's lawyer drafts the first version; the buyer's lawyer reviews and amends) and tracks the negotiation of clauses to conclusion.


### 7.2.1  Mandatory CPCV Clauses


*Table 7.3 — CPCV Mandatory Clauses*


| Clause | Purpose | Buyer-Side Focus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Parties | Identify buyer(s) and seller(s) fully | Verify identity, marital status, spousal consent, corporate authority |
| 2. Property description | Define the property precisely (caderneta + certidão reference) | Match description to caderneta and certidão; no ambiguity |
| 3. Price and payment terms | State total price, deposit amount, balance, payment schedule | Ensure deposit held in escrow; balance at deed |
| 4. Deposit (sinal) | Confirm deposit amount, payment mechanism, receipt | Escrow account of buyer's lawyer or notary; not direct to seller |
| 5. Deed (escritura) date | Target deed date and place | Allow 60–120 days from CPCV; specify notary and language |
| 6. Condition precedents (condições suspensivas) | Define conditions that must be met for the contract to take effect | DD completion; mortgage approval; clean title; condo debts cleared |
| 7. Representations and warranties | Seller's warranties on title, no debts, no tenants, no works pending | Broad warranties; survival period post-deed |
| 8. Default and penalty (cláusula penal) | Define consequences of default by either party | Forfeit (buyer) / double-deposit (seller) as baseline; consider additional damages |
| 9. Possession / handover | When keys are transferred; condition at handover | Handover at deed; written inventory; utilities transferred |
| 10. Costs and taxes | Allocation of IMT, IS, notary, registry, lawyer fees | Standard: buyer pays IMT/IS/notary/registry; seller pays capital gains |
| 11. Pre-deed deliverables | Documents seller must deliver before deed | Updated caderneta; clean certidão; condo debt certificate; utility clearances |
| 12. Notices | Valid notice channels | Both lawyers as formal notice recipients |
| 13. Dispute resolution | Mediation / arbitration / courts | Mediation first; Portuguese courts; Lisbon or Porto forum |
| 14. Signatures and notarisation | Execution | Both parties; notarised signatures; 2 witnesses |


### 7.2.2  Protective CPCV Clause Library


> **CLAUSE — DEPOSIT ESCROW**
>
> *"The Deposit of €[X] shall be paid by the Promitente Comprador (Buyer) to the Promitente Vendedor (Seller) at the signing of this Contract, by bank transfer to an escrow account held in the name of [Buyer's Lawyer's escrow account], to be released to the Seller only upon (a) signing of this Contract by both parties, (b) confirmation of the Seller's identity and capacity, and (c) confirmation that no condition precedent has been triggered as of the date of release."*
>
> *"The escrow agent shall retain the Deposit for a minimum of [3] business days after signing to permit the Buyer's lawyer to verify the certidão predial and condominium debt certificate. If during this period any material adverse change is identified, the Deposit shall be returned to the Buyer in full, and this Contract shall be deemed null and void without penalty to either party."*


> **CLAUSE — DUE DILIGENCE CONDITION PRECEDENT**
>
> *"This Contract is subject to the suspensive condition that the Buyer's legal and technical due diligence, conducted by [Lawyer name] and [Engineer name], shall confirm to the Buyer's reasonable satisfaction that: (a) the Seller has clean and unencumbered title to the Property; (b) the Property has all required licences and authorisations, including a valid licença de utilização; (c) there are no outstanding debts, taxes, liens, or encumbrances affecting the Property; (d) the condominium (if applicable) has issued a debt certificate confirming zero seller arrears; and (e) no material adverse fact has been identified that was not disclosed in writing to the Buyer before the signing of this Contract."*
>
> *"The due diligence period shall be [60] days from the date of signing of this Contract. If the due diligence does not confirm the above to the Buyer's reasonable satisfaction, the Buyer may, by written notice to the Seller within the due diligence period, declare this Contract null and void, in which case the Deposit shall be returned to the Buyer in full within 5 business days."*
>
> *"If the Buyer does not give such notice within the due diligence period, this condition precedent shall be deemed satisfied."*


> **CLAUSE — FINANCING CONDITION PRECEDENT**
>
> *"This Contract is subject to the suspensive condition that the Buyer shall have obtained a formal mortgage offer from [Bank name] for an amount of not less than €[X] secured on the Property, on terms acceptable to the Buyer, within [45] days from the date of signing of this Contract."*
>
> *"If the Buyer has not obtained such mortgage offer within the said period despite having made a complete and bona fide application, the Buyer may, by written notice to the Seller within [5] business days after the expiry of the period, declare this Contract null and void, in which case the Deposit shall be returned to the Buyer in full within 5 business days."*
>
> *"The Buyer shall use reasonable efforts to obtain the mortgage offer, including applying to at least one alternative bank if [Bank name] declines the application."*


> **CLAUSE — SELLER DEFAULT / DOUBLE DEPOSIT**
>
> *"If the Promitente Vendedor (Seller) fails to execute the public deed (escritura) on the date agreed in this Contract, or otherwise defaults on the obligations hereunder without legal justification, the Seller shall be liable to the Buyer for double the Deposit (i.e., the return of the Deposit plus an equivalent amount as penalty), in accordance with Article 442 of the Portuguese Civil Code."*
>
> *"Without prejudice to the foregoing, the Buyer reserves the right to seek specific performance (cumprimento) in lieu of the penalty, at the Buyer's election."*


> **CLAUSE — PENALTY FOR DELAY**
>
> *"If either party delays the execution of the escritura pública by more than [30] days beyond the agreed date, through no fault of the other party and without legal justification, the defaulting party shall pay to the non-defaulting party a daily penalty of €[X] for each day of delay, up to a maximum of [30] days, after which the non-defaulting party may treat this Contract as repudiated."*


> **CLAUSE — SURVIVAL OF WARRANTIES**
>
> *"The Seller's representations and warranties in this Contract shall survive the execution of the escritura pública for a period of [5] years. The Buyer's right to seek indemnification for breach of any such representation or warranty shall not be affected by the execution of the escritura or by the registration of the Property in the Buyer's name."*


> **CLAUSE — INVENTORY AND FIXTURES**
>
> *"The Property is sold with the furniture, fixtures, and fittings listed in Annex [1] to this Contract, which shall be present and in good working condition at the handover. The Seller shall not remove any item listed in Annex [1] before the handover. Any item missing or not in good working condition at the handover shall be replaced or repaired at the Seller's cost, or the corresponding value shall be deducted from the balance due at the escritura."*


### 7.2.3  CPCV Review Checklist


Before the buyer signs the CPCV, the buyer's lawyer must confirm in writing that the following items have been verified:

- All parties' identity, marital status, and capacity (including spousal consent where required).
- Property description matches caderneta predial and certidão predial exactly.
- Deposit amount, payment mechanism, and escrow terms are clear and protective.
- All agreed condition precedents are included with clear timelines and notice mechanics.
- Default and penalty clauses are symmetric and enforceable.
- Inventory (if applicable) is attached as an annex and is specific.
- Deed date allows sufficient time for DD and mortgage.
- Notary and deed language are confirmed (Portuguese; translation arranged if needed).
- All annexes (floor plans, energy cert, etc.) are referenced and attached.
- Survival of warranties is included.
- Dispute resolution clause is acceptable.
- Notarisation arrangements (witnesses, notary, fees) are confirmed.

> 🚩 **CPCV RED FLAGS**
>
> Deposit paid directly to the seller rather than to an escrow account — high risk of loss if the seller defaults or the property develops a problem.
> No due-diligence condition precedent — the buyer is committing to buy regardless of what DD uncovers.
> No financing condition precedent — the buyer forfeits the deposit if the mortgage is declined.
> Asymmetric penalty clauses (e.g., buyer forfeits deposit but seller only returns deposit on default) — must be symmetric (buyer forfeits, seller pays double).
> CPCV with deed date within 30 days of signing — does not allow realistic DD or mortgage approval timeline.
> Seller refuses to warrant title or licences — major risk; the buyer is accepting the property 'as is' with no recourse.
> Inventory is vague ('all furniture') — disputes over what was included are virtually guaranteed.
> Seller's lawyer drafted the CPCV with the buyer's deposit going to the seller's lawyer's escrow — request neutral escrow (buyer's lawyer or notary).
> CPCV references a property description that does not match the caderneta predial — possible unlicensed alteration.
> Seller asks for part of the price to be paid 'off the books' (dinheiro negro) — illegal; refuse and walk away; this is tax fraud and an AML offence.


## 7.3  Deposit Protection, Contingencies & Timelines


The deposit is the buyer's primary financial exposure in the CPCV phase. Under Article 442 of the Civil Code, the deposit (sinal) operates as both advance payment and penalty: if the buyer defaults, the deposit is forfeited; if the seller defaults, double the deposit is owed to the buyer. The deposit is typically 10–20% of the purchase price. The buyer's agent must ensure the deposit is held in escrow, that the conditions for release are clearly defined, and that the CPCV's condition precedents give the buyer a defensible path to recover the deposit if DD or financing fails.


### 7.3.1  Escrow Options


*Table 7.4 — Deposit Escrow Options*


| Escrow Holder | Pros | Cons | Recommendation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Buyer's lawyer's client account | Buyer-side control; familiar with DD; rapid release mechanics | Buyer bears risk of lawyer mismanagement (rare) | Recommended for most transactions |
| Notary escrow | Maximum neutrality; statutory framework | Slower; higher fees; less flexible | Recommended for high-value or complex transactions |
| Seller's lawyer's client account | Listing agent's preference; common | Buyer's deposit in seller-aligned hands; conflict if dispute arises | Avoid unless seller's lawyer is independent and reputable |
| Direct to seller | None for the buyer | Maximum buyer risk; full loss if seller defaults | Never accept |
| Bank escrow (where mortgage involved) | Bank-controlled; aligned with mortgage release | Bank processes can be slow; higher fees | Suitable for new-build / developer transactions |


### 7.3.2  Contingency Timeline


*Table 7.5 — CPCV-to-Deed Contingency Timeline (Typical 90-Day)*


| Day | Event | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 0 | CPCV signed; deposit paid to escrow | Both parties + lawyers |
| Day 1–7 | Lawyer opens DD; agent commissions surveys; bank application submitted | Lawyer, agent, banker |
| Day 7–30 | DD document chase; surveyor site visit; bank appraisal ordered | Lawyer, surveyor, bank |
| Day 30–45 | DD findings consolidated; risk matrix updated; mortgage offer expected | Lawyer, agent, bank |
| Day 45–60 | DD condition precedent decision: confirm, renegotiate, or terminate | Buyer + lawyer + agent |
| Day 60–75 | Renegotiation (if any); final mortgage offer; insurance arranged; notary booked | Lawyer, banker, agent |
| Day 75–85 | Pre-deed verification; final inspection; funds transferred to notary | Lawyer, agent, buyer |
| Day 85–90 | Escritura; balance paid; keys handed over; registration initiated | All parties |
| Day 90–120 | Land registration completed; utilities transferred; insurance active | Lawyer, agent, buyer |


> ℹ️ **DEPOSIT RECOVERY PATHWAYS**
>
> If DD condition precedent is triggered and notice given within the period, deposit returned in full within 5 business days — clear contractual right.
> If financing condition precedent is triggered despite bona fide application, deposit returned — but the buyer must demonstrate good-faith effort (multiple banks, complete applications).
> If the seller defaults, double-deposit payable to buyer — enforceable via summary action; in practice may require litigation if seller refuses.
> If buyer defaults without condition-precedent cover, deposit forfeited — no recovery.
> Title insurance (limited availability in Portugal) can cover specific risks; discuss with the buyer's lawyer if available.

# Chapter 8 — Phase 7: Transaction Execution & Closing


Phase 7 is the period from CPCV signing to the escritura and the immediate post-deed registration. It is a logistics-intensive phase in which the buyer's agent becomes project manager, coordinating the lawyer, the bank, the notary, the seller, the listing agent, the surveyor, the insurer, and the condominium. The phase succeeds on disciplined timeline management, a written pre-deed verification checklist, and a structured deed-day protocol. Failure modes are concrete: a missing document delays the deed by days; an unresolved condition precedent forfeits the deposit; an unfunded tax payment blocks the escritura.


## 8.1  Full Timeline Management from CPCV to Deed


The buyer's agent maintains a master timeline from CPCV signing to escritura, with each milestone, its owner, its target date, and its actual date. The timeline is reviewed in every status call (twice weekly minimum) and is the single source of truth for the transaction. Slippage on any milestone must be flagged within 24 hours and a recovery plan agreed.

### 8.1.2  Critical Path Analysis


Three items typically determine the critical path: DD completion (driven by seller document production and surveyor scheduling), mortgage approval (driven by bank processing and appraisal), and IMT payment (which requires the DD to be substantially complete because the IMT is paid on the agreed price and property description). The buyer's agent should identify the longest of these three within the first 10 days and ensure that track is started earliest.


> ⚠️ **COMMON TIMELINE KILLERS**
>
> Seller delays producing DD documents (especially condominium debt certificates and updated caderneta where alterations have been regularised). Mitigation: written document request with date-stamped follow-ups; CPCV condition precedent tied to document delivery.
> Bank appraisal comes in 5–15% below agreed price. Mitigation: maintain a parallel bank conversation; pre-discuss bridge financing with the buyer; pre-agree with seller a price-adjustment mechanism if appraisal falls below a stated threshold.
> Surveyor discovers material defects that require pricing renegotiation. Mitigation: commission the survey early (Day 3–10) so renegotiation has time before deed.
> Buyer's source-of-funds documentation is incomplete. Mitigation: collect all AML documentation during Phase 1 onboarding, not at the deed.
> Notary has no available slot for 4–6 weeks. Mitigation: book notary provisionally at CPCV signing, firm up at Day 60.


## 8.2  Multi-Party Coordination


The Portuguese closing involves, at minimum, the buyer, the seller, both lawyers, the notary, the bank (if mortgage), and the condominium. For off-plan transactions, the developer and the developer's bank also feature. For corporate buyers, the company's signatory and any power-of-attorney holders must be present or represented. The buyer's agent is the coordination hub: every counterparty should know the agent as the single point of contact for status, scheduling, and issue escalation.

### 8.2.2  Coordination Rhythm


The coordination rhythm intensifies as the deed approaches. The recommended cadence is:

- Days 0–30: twice-weekly 15-minute status call with buyer; weekly email update to all counterparties.
- Days 30–60: twice-weekly buyer call; twice-weekly 5-minute check-in with each of lawyer, banker, surveyor.
- Days 60–80: daily 10-minute buyer update; weekly coordination call with lawyer + banker; notary booking confirmed.
- Days 80–90: daily buyer update; daily lawyer contact; 48-hour and 24-hour document verification; final inspection scheduled.
- Day of deed: agent attends; coordinates witnesses, translator, signing order, key handover.

### 8.3.2  Final Inspection Protocol


The final inspection is conducted 1–3 days before the deed, with the buyer (or the buyer's agent if the buyer is not in Portugal) and ideally the seller or listing agent present. The inspection confirms that: (a) the property is in the condition agreed in the CPCV; (b) the inventory items are present and in working order; (c) no items have been removed that should remain; (d) no damage has occurred since the DD survey; (e) the keys, remotes, mailroom keys, and any access devices are present. The inspection is documented in a written final-inspection report, signed by both parties (or their representatives), with photos of any defects.

>

>
> *"Inventory (Annex 1 to CPCV): All items present and in working order, except [list any missing or damaged items]."*
>

## 8.4  Deed Day Protocol


The day of the escritura is the culmination of weeks of coordination. The buyer's agent attends in person, manages the logistics, and ensures the buyer is briefed on the sequence of events. The deed is read aloud by the notary in Portuguese (translation provided simultaneously for non-Portuguese-speaking buyers); parties and witnesses sign; taxes and balance are confirmed paid; keys are handed over; photos are taken. After the deed, the lawyer initiates land registration, the agent coordinates utility transfer and condominium update, and the buyer takes possession.

> ℹ️ **DEED DAY — AGENT'S PRE-DEED BRIEF TO BUYER**
>
> Bring original passport and NIF; arrive 30 minutes early; dress is business casual.

> Two witnesses are required — your agent or a family member can serve.
> Keys and remotes are handed over after all signatures; the agent will inventory them with you.
> Plan a quiet afternoon after the deed — the buyer is often emotionally drained and should not be scheduling renovation viewings or major meetings.
> The certified copy of the escritura is sufficient for utility transfer, condominium registration, and most immediate purposes; the original goes to the land registry for registration.

> 🚩 **PHASE 7 RED FLAGS**
>

> Final inspection reveals missing inventory or damage — withhold corresponding amount from balance at deed; obtain seller's written acknowledgement.
> Mortgage funds not confirmed in notary's account at start of deed — postpone; do not proceed without confirmation.
> Source-of-funds documentation incomplete at deed — notary has statutory AML obligation to refuse; verify 7 days before deed.
> Seller attempts to deliver property 'as is' contrary to CPCV — refuse; insist on inventory and condition matching CPCV; document any discrepancy in writing.
> Notary refuses to read the deed in the presence of a sworn translator — find another notary; non-Portuguese-speaking buyers have the right to understand the document they are signing.

# Chapter 9 — Phase 8: Post-Purchase & Relationship Management


Phase 8 covers everything that happens after the deed: handover, utilities, insurance, warranties, renovation project management, property management, rental setup, and the long-term relationship that produces referrals and repeat business. The phase is structurally different from the preceding seven: those were linear, transaction-focused, and bounded by the deed. Phase 8 is open-ended, relationship-focused, and bounded only by the buyer's ongoing satisfaction. A buyer's agent who treats Phase 8 as an afterthought forfeits the long-term economic value of the engagement — which, for a well-run practice, exceeds the transactional fee.

Handover takes place either at the deed (most common) or shortly after (where the seller retains possession for an agreed period). The handover is documented in a written handover protocol signed by both parties.

### 9.2.3  Warranties


Portuguese law provides two principal warranty periods for new construction: a 5-year decenal warranty (garantia decenal) covering structural defects, and a 1-year snagging warranty (garantia de boa execução) covering minor defects in finishes and installations. For off-plan and new-build purchases, the buyer's agent must ensure the developer delivers:

- Termo de responsabilidade (responsibility statement) by the builder / engineer / architect, signed and notarised.
- Ficha técnica de habitação (technical data sheet).
- Final licença de utilização (use licence).
- Energy performance certificate.
- Insurance policy covering the decenal warranty (seguro decenal) — mandatory under Decree-Law 113/2013 for new residential construction since 2014.
- Snagging list (lista de defeitos) — typically buyer has 30 days from handover to identify defects; developer has 30 days to remedy or compensate.

> ⚠️ **SNAGGING DISCIPLINE FOR NEW BUILDS**
>
> Conduct a snagging inspection within 5 days of handover, ideally with an independent architect or surveyor — not the developer's representative.
> Document every defect in writing with photographs; transmit to developer by registered letter within the snagging window.
> Reserve a portion of the final payment (if structured) until snagging is complete; without financial leverage, developers routinely defer snagging.
> For off-plan, ensure the CPCV included a penalty clause for handover delay and a snagging retention; without these, the buyer has limited leverage post-handover.


## 9.3  Renovation & Project Management


For many buyers, the property requires renovation — ranging from cosmetic (paint, flooring, kitchen) to structural (load-bearing changes, extension, full gut). The buyer's agent is not the project manager, but is the connector: introducing architects, contractors, and project managers; verifying licences and insurance; coordinating with the condominium for any works affecting common areas; and providing a sanity check on budgets and timelines. The agent should maintain a vetted panel of at least two architects, two general contractors, and one project manager per region of operation.

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

### 9.3.2  Heritage & Permit Considerations


Properties within classified heritage zones (centro histórico, IGESPAR-classified buildings, or properties with individual património classification) face significant renovation restrictions. Permits may require:

- Approval from the Câmara Municipal urbanism department.
- Approval from the DGPC (Direção-Geral do Património Cultural) or IGESPAR for classified properties.
- Specific materials, colours, and techniques for facades, windows, roofs, and visible elements.
- Archaeological oversight during excavation works in some historic centres.
- Restrictions on internal layout changes affecting structural or character-defining elements.

> ℹ️ **PERMIT TIMELINE REALITY**
>
> Heritage permit timelines in central Lisbon and Porto typically run 4–8 months, sometimes longer. Buyers contemplating major renovation in historic centres must budget both time and money for permit complexity.
> Engage an architect with documented experience in the specific heritage zone — generic experience is insufficient. The Câmara and DGPC have working relationships with specific architects who have navigated their processes successfully.
> Pre-purchase: include in DD a written assessment by the architect of what is and is not likely to be permitted, so the buyer's renovation budget is realistic.


## 9.4  Property Management & Rental Setup


For buyers who will not occupy the property year-round, the agent coordinates the setup of property management and (where applicable) rental operations. Three models are common: owner self-management (with a local caretaker for non-resident owners), long-term rental, and short-term AL (alojamento local). Each has different licensing, tax, and operational requirements.

### 9.4.2  AL Licence Considerations (2026)


The Mais Habitação package significantly altered the AL regime. As of 2026, the key considerations are:

- AL registration is required for any short-term (≤30 days) rental of residential property to non-residents.
- New AL licences are suspended in designated 'containment zones' (áreas de contenção) — primarily central Lisbon (Baixa, Chiado, Alfama, Bairro Alto, Príncipe Real, etc.) and central Porto (Ribeira, Aliados, Cedofeita) — unless the property already held a licence or qualifies for a specific exemption.
- Existing AL licences are subject to periodic review (renewal every 5 years); non-renewal is possible in containment zones where the Câmara determines 'pressure on housing' is excessive.
- AL licence must be displayed in the property; guest identification must be collected and reported to SEF (now AIMA); noise, waste, and behavioural rules apply with penalties for non-compliance.
- AL income is taxed under specific IRS rules; non-resident owners face withholding tax and may not deduct expenses in the same way as resident owners.

> ⚠️ **AL PRE-PURCHASE VERIFICATION**
>
> If the buyer's investment case depends on AL income, AL licence validity must be verified in Phase 4 DD — not assumed from the listing.
> Existing AL licences do not automatically transfer to the new owner; the buyer must re-register (typically straightforward but not instant).
> A property advertised as 'AL-ready' or 'with AL potential' in a containment zone may be unlicensable; treat with caution.
> The tax treatment of AL income under the post-Mais Habitação regime should be confirmed with the buyer's tax advisor before completion.

A well-managed buyer's agent practice derives 30–50% of revenue from repeat business, referrals, and ancillary services (project management oversight, rental setup, portfolio advice, second-property searches). ### 9.5.2  Referral System


Referrals are the dominant client-acquisition channel for a mature buyer's agent practice, typically generating 50–70% of new mandates. The referral system should be active, not passive — agents should ask for referrals explicitly, make it easy to refer, and acknowledge every referral with a written thank-you regardless of whether it converts.

- Ask for referrals explicitly at three moments: 30 days post-deed (when buyer is happiest); 6 months post-deed (when buyer has settled in and is talking about the experience); and at the anniversary review.
- Maintain a referral log: source, date, contact, outcome, thank-you sent.
- Offer a referral acknowledgement: a thank-you note, a small gift, or (where legally and ethically permissible) a referral fee. Note that some jurisdictions restrict referral fees; in Portugal, referral fees between agents are subject to disclosure requirements.
- Build a 'referrable experience': every client touchpoint should be designed to produce a story the buyer will tell. The story is the marketing.

### 9.5.3  Service Expansion


As the practice matures, the agent can expand services beyond transactional buyer representation into adjacent offerings:

- Annual portfolio reviews for multi-property owners.
- Property management oversight (not direct management; coordination of managers).
- Renovation project sponsorship (architect + contractor coordination).
- Sale-of-property representation (when the buyer becomes a seller — often under a different fee model).
- Family-office coordination for HNW multi-generational buyers.
- Relocation services (school placement, healthcare navigation, residency coordination).

> ℹ️ **THE LONG-TERM VALUE CALCULATION**
>
> Average transaction fee on a €700k property: €10,000 (1.5%).
> Average referral value (one referral converted per year, same fee): €10,000.
> Average repeat transaction within 5 years (second home, upsize, downsize, child's first home): €10,000–€15,000.
> Average ancillary services (project oversight, rental setup, annual reviews): €2,000–€5,000/year.
> Total 5-year client value: €60,000–€100,000+ — vs. €10,000 if the agent walks away at the deed.
> Retention is not a 'nice to have'. It is the dominant economic driver of a buyer's agent practice.

## 10.1  Historical / Old Properties (pre-1950)


Historical properties — the 18th- and 19th-century buildings of central Lisbon (Baixa, Chiado, Alfama, Príncipe Real), central Porto (Ribeira, Cedofeita, Bonjardim), and the historic cores of Coimbra, Évora, and others — are the segment where buyer's agent expertise adds the highest relative value. These properties combine character and location with significant technical, regulatory, and renovation complexity. The buyer who falls in love with a Pombaline building in Baixa without understanding the structural, heritage, and energy implications is the buyer most likely to spend double the purchase price on renovation within five years.


### 10.1.1  Typical Buyer Profile

- Lifestyle buyer seeking character and location; willing to invest in renovation.
- Investor targeting short-term AL (where licensed) or long-term premium rental.
- Buyer with prior renovation experience or strong architect relationship.

### 10.1.2  Distinctive Due Diligence Considerations


*Table 10.1 — Historical Properties: Distinctive DD*


| Dimension | Consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Structural | Pombaline (Lisbon Baixa) timber cage structure; gaioleiro (Porto) timber floors; crack pattern mapping; settlement history; timber decay; rising damp; facade plumbness |
| Heritage classification | Centro histórico zoning; IGESPAR individual classification; DGPC approval for any alteration; facade material restrictions; roof form; window type |
| Seismic | Lisbon seismic zone; pre-1950 buildings often without seismic retrofit; post-1755 Pombaline buildings have inherent seismic design (gaiola pombalina); verify with structural engineer |
| Energy | EPC typically F or G; poor insulation; high heating/cooling cost; original single glazing; ventilation issues |
| Licensing for alterations | Any structural or facade alteration requires Câmara permit; DGPC approval may also apply; permit timeline 4–12 months |
| Utilities | Original electrical and plumbing often sub-standard or non-compliant; full rewire/replumb typical; capacity for modern load may require panel upgrade |
| Condominium (if apartment) | Building may have shared structural concerns; common-area maintenance history; facade restoration history; pending works |


### 10.1.3  Common Red Flags

- Visible structural cracks >3mm, especially diagonal or stepped — full structural survey required before any commitment.
- Recent facade paint over a section — often masking water ingress or render failure.
- Sagging floors — timber decay or deflection; lift survey required.
- No energy certificate or EPC F/G with no renovation since — energy retrofit will be mandatory for many uses post-2026 EU directives.
- Unlicensed interior alterations (walls removed, openings cut) — regularisation required; Câmara may refuse retrospective licence.
- Heritage classification plaque on or near building — DGPC approval will be required for any work; budget 6+ months for permits.
- Seller cannot produce pre-1990 renovation permits — assume unpermitted; high regularisation risk.

### 10.1.4  Recommended Specialists

- Structural engineer with documented experience in Pombaline or gaioleiro buildings.
- Architect with prior DGPC approvals in the specific historic centre.
- Conservation-aware contractor (not all contractors understand heritage work).
- Surveyor experienced in pre-1950 stock (different checklist from new build).

### 10.1.5  Cost-of-Ownership Nuances

- Renovation cost typically €800–€2,500/m² for full retrofit (vs €400–€1,000/m² for new build).
- Ongoing maintenance higher (older materials, more frequent repainting, facade inspection).
- IMI may be lower (VPT based on older matriz values), but AIMI may apply to high-value central properties.
- Insurance premium may be higher (older stock, seismic risk, water damage susceptibility).

### 10.1.6  Negotiation Tactics

- Quantify structural and energy retrofit cost; present as price reduction rationale.
- Cite permit timeline risk if buyer intends alteration — long lead time is a real cost.
- For long-on-market historical properties, aggressive discount (12–18%) supported by comparable transactions and renovation cost estimates.

### 10.1.7  Region-Specific Notes

- Lisbon Baixa Pombaline: uniform building stock; seismic design; facade uniformity strictly controlled.
- Lisbon Alfama: narrow streets; access constraints for renovation vehicles; high tourist flow.
- Porto Ribeira: UNESCO buffer zone; very strict heritage controls; renovation requires specialist contractors.
- Coimbra Alta: university-owned land constraints; selective heritage controls.
- Évora historic centre: UNESCO World Heritage; DGPC approval required for most alterations.

## 10.2  New Builds & Off-Plan


New build and off-plan properties — concentrated in Lisbon's Parque das Nações, Loures, Odivelas, and peripheral expansion; Porto's Antas, Campanhã, and Matosinhos; the Algarve's modern resort developments; and the Silver Coast's emerging coastal developments — appeal to buyers seeking turnkey condition, energy efficiency, modern amenities, and (for off-plan) payment-plan flexibility. The DD profile is fundamentally different from resale: instead of inspecting an existing building, the buyer is assessing a developer's reliability, the construction specifications, the legal framework of guarantees, and the project's completion trajectory.


### 10.2.1  Distinctive Due Diligence Considerations


*Table 10.2 — New Build / Off-Plan: Distinctive DD*


| Dimension | Consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Developer reliability | Track record (completed projects, on-time delivery, snagging reputation); financial health; corporate filings; references from previous buyers; bankruptcy / litigation history |
| Construction specifications | Materials; structural system; MEP; insulation; finishes; what is included vs optional; upgrade pricing |
| Completion guarantee | Bank guarantee (garantia bancária) or insurance policy covering deposit and progress payments in event of developer default — mandatory under Decree-Law 156/2005 for residential off-plan |
| Payment plan | Structure (% at reservation, % at CPCV, % at construction milestones, % at handover); alignment with construction progress; conditions for non-payment if milestones slip |
| Penalty clauses | Late delivery penalty (typically 0.05–0.2% per day, capped); specification change penalty; handover condition penalty |
| Licences | Alvará de licença de construção issued; project approved by Câmara; DUQP (declaração de utilidade pública) if applicable |
| Decenal warranty & insurance | Seguro decenal (10-year structural warranty insurance) — mandatory under DL 113/2013; termo de responsabilidade; ficha técnica de habitação |
| VAT treatment | First transmission within 5 years subject to VAT (23%) — typically embedded in price; verify invoicing and IMT implications |
| Snagging rights | Buyer's right to snagging inspection within 30 days of handover; developer's remediation obligation within 30 days; reservation of final payment until snagging complete |


### 10.2.2  Common Red Flags

- Developer with no completed projects or only recently-formed company — high default risk.
- No bank guarantee or insurance for progress payments — non-compliant with DL 156/2005; refuse to proceed.
- Vague specifications ('high-quality finishes') without brand and material specifics — future disputes guaranteed.
- Reservation contract requiring non-refundable deposit >10% before CPCV — disproportionate buyer risk.
- Aggressive 'early-bird' pricing with 48-hour deadline — manufactured urgency; nearly always negotiable.
- Handover date 'estimated' without liquidated damages for delay — buyer bears all timeline risk.
- Sales representative unable to produce alvará de licença de construção — licence may not yet be issued; significant delay risk.

### 10.2.3  Recommended Specialists

- Lawyer with documented experience reviewing off-plan contracts and DL 156/2005 compliance.
- Independent snagging surveyor (not the developer's representative) for pre-handover inspection.
- For high-value off-plan: independent quantity surveyor to verify construction progress against payment milestones.

### 10.2.4  Cost-of-Ownership Nuances

- VAT (23%) included in price; IMT and IS still payable on the price.
- Lower immediate renovation cost (turnkey); lower ongoing maintenance for first 5–7 years.
- Higher VPT (tax value) than older stock — IMI may be higher per m² despite lower rate.
- Condominium fees may be higher (modern amenities: pool, gym, concierge, 24h security).

### 10.2.5  Negotiation Tactics

- Price discount is often harder than concessions — push for parking, storage, finishings upgrades, appliance packages.
- Negotiate penalty clause upward (default is often minimal); demand 0.1% per day minimum, capped at 5–10% of price.
- For slow-selling developments, negotiate deposit reduction, longer payment plan, or handover-condition clauses.

### 10.2.6  Region-Specific Notes

- Lisbon: Parque das Nações mature; Loures / Odivelas active pipeline; historic centre constrained.
- Porto: Antas / Campanhã redevelopment active; Matosinhos coastal premium.
- Algarve: branded residences (Vale d'Oliveiras, Quinta do Lago extensions); off-plan common; verify developer track record.
- Silver Coast: emerging developments; lower entry price; verify developer solvency carefully.

## 10.3  Land & Development Projects


Land purchases are the most specialist segment a buyer's agent handles. The buyer is typically a self-builder, a small developer, or an investor in a future project. The DD intensity is very high because the value of land is determined not by what is on it but by what can legally be built on it, and the urbanistic regime in Portugal is detailed, regionally variable, and frequently ambiguous. A buyer's agent who is not deeply familiar with PDM (Plano Director Municipal) zoning, urbanistic parameters, and the construction licensing regime should refer the buyer to a specialist land broker or to the buyer's lawyer for the urbanistic work.


### 10.3.1  Distinctive Due Diligence Considerations


*Table 10.3 — Land & Development: Distinctive DD*


| Dimension | Consideration |
| --- | --- |
| PDM zoning | Plot's classification: urbano (urban), urbanizável (urbanisable), or rústico (rustic). Only urbano and urbanizável generally buildable; rústico has very limited buildability |
| Urbanistic parameters | Índice de utilização (building ratio); índice de implantação (footprint ratio); altura máxima (max height); taxa de ocupação do solo; setbacks; minimum plot size |
| Access | Public road frontage vs private easement; road classification; maintenance responsibility; right of way documentation |
| Utilities | Water, electricity, sewage, telecom at plot boundary; if not present, cost of extension (often €10k–€100k+) and timeline |
| Topography | Slope; retaining wall requirements; cut/fill volume; geotechnical conditions; drainage |
| Environmental | Protected habitat (Natura 2000); watercourse protection (levee); contaminated land (former industrial); forestry restrictions |
| Subdivision rules | Whether plot can be subdivided; minimum lot size; condóminio horizontal rules |
| Construction licence feasibility | Pre-application enquiry to Câmara; expected permit timeline; typical 6–18 months |
| Project feasibility study | Architect's concept design within parameters; indicative construction cost; ROI model |


### 10.3.2  Common Red Flags

- Plot classified as rústico — very limited buildability; usually only agricultural support buildings.
- No public road access — easement may be unclear, contested, or revocable.
- Utilities >100m from plot — extension cost often exceeds plot value.
- Plot in Natura 2000 zone — EIA may be required for any construction; permit timeline 12–24 months.
- Slope >30% — significant earthworks and retaining wall cost; may render project uneconomic.
- Seller cannot produce caderneta rústica or definitive PDM extract — title or zoning may be unclear.

### 10.3.3  Recommended Specialists

- Urbanistic lawyer (advogado with documented Câmara experience in the specific concelho).
- Topographer (topógrafo) for boundary survey and topographic plan.
- Geotechnical engineer for soil bearing capacity and foundation design input.
- Architect for pre-application concept and feasibility study.

### 10.3.4  Negotiation Tactics

- Aggressive discount (15–25%) supported by urbanistic constraints and infrastructure costs.
- Condition precedent on pre-application enquiry result — gives buyer walk-away if buildability is below assumed.
- Long DD period (90–120 days) to allow topographic and geotechnical work.

## 10.4  Luxury Properties (€1.5m+)


The Portuguese luxury segment — Quinta do Lago and Vale do Lobo in the Algarve; Cascais, Estoril, and Sintra's prime areas; Lisbon's Príncipe Real, Lapa, Chiado, and Avenida da Liberdade; Porto's Foz do Douro; and the branded residences of Comporta and Melides — operates under different dynamics from the general market. International buyer competition is intense, transaction sizes justify deeper DD investment, and the buyer profile (HNW, time-poor, expectation of concierge service) demands a different service model.


### 10.4.1  Distinctive Due Diligence Considerations


*Table 10.4 — Luxury: Distinctive DD*


| Dimension | Consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Title | Often complex (corporate structures, multi-jurisdiction, family trusts); deeper title chain verification; beneficial ownership disclosure |
| Tax structure | Optimal acquisition structure (individual vs company vs holding); AIMI implications; pre-acquisition tax structuring essential |
| Privacy & security | Discreet marketing; non-disclosure agreements; security assessment (gated, alarm, CCTV, staff vetting) |
| Amenities & service | Concierge; security staff; housekeeping; pool and garden maintenance; staff quarters; service entrance |
| Lifestyle | Proximity to golf, marina, equestrian, international school, private healthcare, airport |
| International competition | Buyer competing with global HNW; often multi-currency; often time-pressured; specialist luxury networks |
| Concierge services | Pre-arrival arrangements; staff recruitment; private aviation; school placement; healthcare navigation |


### 10.4.2  Common Red Flags

- Title held in opaque offshore structure — penal tax treatment under Portuguese anti-abuse rules.
- Hidden encumbrances (rights of way, view protections in favour of neighbours) — material to privacy and use.
- Recent unpermitted alterations to character property — regularisation may require demolition.
- Inadequate security for the buyer's profile — significant retrofit cost.
- Condominium or community rules incompatible with the buyer's intended use (events, staff, vehicles).

### 10.4.3  Recommended Specialists

- Senior lawyer with cross-border and HNW experience.
- Tax advisor with Portuguese and buyer's home-jurisdiction expertise.
- Security consultant (for properties where security is material).
- Concierge service provider for ongoing lifestyle support.

### 10.4.4  Cost-of-Ownership Nuances

- AIMI surcharge on high-value residential property held by individuals.
- Higher staffing costs (concierge, security, housekeeping).
- Higher insurance (high-value contents, speciality cover).
- Higher condominium fees (luxury amenities).

### 10.4.5  Negotiation Tactics

- Personal rapport with seller is critical; HNW sellers often value discretion and certainty over maximum price.
- Modest discount (3–5%) supported by comparable analysis; larger discounts rarely achieved on prime unique properties.
- Concessions on contents (art, furniture), staff continuity, or closing timeline often more valuable than price.

## 10.5  Resale / Traditional Homes


Resale apartments and houses in modern (post-1990) buildings are the workhorse of the Portuguese residential market: the 2- and 3-bed apartments of Lisbon's Avenidas Novas, Telheiras, and Lumiar; Porto's Antas and Campanhã; Cascais and Sintra suburban stock; the Algarve's established resort apartments. The DD profile is the closest to the 'standard' of the preceding chapters — and is the segment where condominium health is the single most reliable predictor of post-purchase satisfaction.


### 10.5.1  Distinctive Due Diligence Considerations


*Table 10.5 — Resale / Traditional: Distinctive DD*


| Dimension | Consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Condominium health | Reserve fund per unit; owner arrears rate; recent and pending works; insurance; owner-occupier ratio; recent litigation |
| Community rules | Pet restrictions; AL restrictions; commercial use; quiet hours; renovation restrictions; visitor parking |
| Common areas | Lift condition; entrance; garage; terrace; pool/garden if applicable; recent refurbishment history |
| Building age band | 1990–2010: typically sound structure but energy performance weaker; 2010–2020: better energy, often higher condo fees; 2020+: often still under builder warranty |
| Recent refurbishment | Verification that any refurbishment was licensed; ficha técnica updated; building permit history |


### 10.5.2  Common Red Flags

- Condominium reserve fund <€500/unit — special assessment likely within 24 months.
- Owner arrears >15% — chronic under-funding; recent or pending litigation likely.
- Recent unpermitted works in the unit — buyer inherits regularisation liability.
- Condominium regulation prohibits the buyer's intended use (AL, pet, home office).
- Lift >25 years old and not scheduled for replacement — special assessment likely.

### 10.5.3  Recommended Specialists

- Standard surveyor for building condition.
- Condominium document audit (agent + lawyer).
- For older resale (pre-1990): structural engineer per §10.1.

## 10.6  Rural / Quinta / Farm Properties


Rural properties — quintas, herdades, and casais in the Alentejo, central Portugal, the Silver Coast interior, the Algarve Serra, Madeira, and the Azores — appeal to lifestyle retirement buyers, rural-tourism investors, and agricultural purchasers. The DD profile is the most property-type-specific of any segment: water rights, access, forestry compliance, and habitational licensing are routinely misunderstood and produce the most expensive post-purchase surprises in the entire market.


### 10.6.1  Distinctive Due Diligence Considerations


*Table 10.6 — Rural / Quinta: Distinctive DD*


| Dimension | Consideration |
| --- | --- |
| Water rights | Source: borehole (furo), well (poço), spring (mina/nascente), cisterna (cistern), mains; depth; flow rate; potability test; licence (DRA feeding license); irrigation rights from ribeira/rega |
| Access | Public road frontage vs easement (servidão); access road condition; maintenance responsibility; winter passability; right of way documentation |
| Habitational licence | Main residence must have licença de utilização for habitation; rural ruins without licence cannot be reconstructed under simplified regime; verify before purchase |
| Agricultural subsidies | PAC (Common Agricultural Policy) entitlements attached to land; transfer formalities; entitlement value |
| Forestry compliance | Eucalyptus clearance; firebreak obligations; protected species (sobreiro, azinheira); ICNF restrictions |
| Land classification | Rústico vs urbano; agricultural vs forestry vs mixed; building rights very limited on rustic land |
| Boundary | Cadastral verification (DG Território); marker inspection; encroachment by neighbours |
| Septic & utilities | Fossa séptica condition; mains electricity (or solar/wind with battery); mains water (or borehole); telecoms |
| Environmental | Natura 2000; protected habitats; watercourse protection; contamination from prior agricultural use (nitrates, pesticides) |


### 10.6.2  Common Red Flags

- No documented water rights — water is the single most valuable asset on a rural property.
- Access via verbal easement only — neighbour can revoke or block; demand registered servidão.
- Rural ruin marketed as 'rebuildable' — verify with Câmara; many ruins cannot be reconstructed under current rules.
- Property classified rústico with no urbanistic viability for the buyer's intended construction.
- Eucalyptus within 50m of habitable building — fire-risk clearance mandatory; non-compliance carries penalties.
- No fossa séptica licence or non-compliant system — environmental and habitability risk.

### 10.6.3  Recommended Specialists

- Agricultural / rural property lawyer (not a general Lisbon lawyer).
- Hydrologist for water source assessment.
- Surveyor with rural experience (different checklist from urban).
- Forestry consultant if eucalyptus, pine, or protected species present.

### 10.6.4  Cost-of-Ownership Nuances

- Lower IMI (rustic rate 0.8% on typically low VPT).
- Higher maintenance cost (access road, fencing, water systems, septic, forestry).
- Higher insurance (rural fire risk; isolated property; slower emergency response).
- Higher management cost if non-resident (caretaker, gardener, security).

### 10.6.5  Region-Specific Notes

- Alentejo: large herdades; water scarcity; cork and olive traditional; emerging wine tourism.
- Silver Coast interior: smaller quintas; mixed agricultural; closer to coast for second-home use.
- Algarve Serra: cooler, wetter; eucalyptus and pine; rural tourism potential.
- Madeira: steep terrain; levadas (water channels) rights; construction cost extreme.
- Azores: dispersed rural; seismic and volcanic risk; construction cost high; pasture land value.

## 11.1  Fiduciary Duty & Ethics


The fiduciary duties owed by the buyer's agent to the buyer are not aspirational; they are enforceable obligations whose breach can expose the agent to civil liability, regulatory sanction by IMPIC, and reputational damage. The six core duties — loyalty, confidentiality, disclosure, accounting, obedience, reasonable care — are described in Chapter 1; this section addresses the practical ethical dilemmas that recur in buyer representation and the standards a serious agent applies to their resolution.


*Table 11.1 — Common Ethical Dilemmas and Resolution Standard*


| Dilemma | Wrong Response | Right Response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listing agent offers higher commission if you bring your buyer | Accept silently | Disclose to buyer in writing; obtain informed consent; do not let commission influence property recommendation |
| Buyer wants to bid against themselves after a verbal 'other offer' | Coach buyer to increase | Hold firm; demand written counter from seller; explain bidding-against-self dynamic |
| You spot a defect during viewing that the buyer misses | Stay silent; let DD surface it | Disclose immediately in writing; document the disclosure |
| Buyer wants to waive DD to make offer more attractive | Comply with buyer instruction | Counsel against in writing; if buyer insists, document the counselling and the buyer's written acknowledgement |
| Seller's lawyer is your friend; offers to 'smooth the path' | Accept the favour | Decline; maintain arm's-length; document any interactions |
| You hold a listing on a property your buyer might also consider | Show it without disclosure | Disclose listing relationship in writing before showing; obtain buyer's written consent to the conflict |
| Buyer asks for off-the-books payment to reduce fees | Accept | Refuse; AML and tax offence; report if persistent |
| Previous client's property is similar to current buyer's target | Share previous client's information | Confidentiality survives engagement; share only public information |


> ⚠️ **THE 'WAFER-THIN CONFLICT' STANDARD**
>
> If there is even a wafer-thin conflict between the agent's economic interest and the buyer's interest, the agent discloses in writing and obtains the buyer's informed consent before proceeding.
> Informed consent means: the buyer understands the conflict, understands the alternatives, and consents in writing. Verbal consent is not consent.
> When in doubt, disclose. The cost of over-disclosure is a brief conversation; the cost of under-disclosure is a regulator complaint or a lawsuit.

## 11.6  Compliance — AMI / IMPIC / GDPR / AML


The buyer's agent operates within a regulatory framework that has tightened materially over the past decade. Non-compliance is not a theoretical risk: IMPIC inspections are routine, GDPR complaints are increasing, and AML controls at the notary are a hard gate that cannot be circumvented. The agent must understand and operate within four principal compliance regimes.


### 11.6.1  AMI Licence & IMPIC Registration

- Mediador imobiliário licence issued by IMPIC under DL 118/2013.
- Annual renewal; CPD (continuous professional development) hours required.
- Public registry verification (Portal do IMPIC) by client before engagement.
- Mandatory disclosure of AMI number on all marketing materials, contracts, and communications.
- Fundo de Garantia dos Mediadores: consumer-protection backstop; mandatory membership.

### 11.6.2  GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)

- Written data protection policy; named data protection officer (or designated responsible).
- Lawful basis for processing (typically: contract performance, legal obligation, legitimate interest).
- Data subject rights: access, rectification, erasure, portability, objection.
- Data retention schedule: BRA + transaction documents 10 years; client dossier 6 years post-engagement; marketing data 24 months from last contact.
- Data breach notification within 72 hours to CNPD (Comissão Nacional de Proteção de Dados).
- No sharing of client data with listing agents without explicit consent.

### 11.6.3  AML (Anti-Money Laundering)

- Source-of-funds documentation for every transaction; enhanced DD for non-resident buyers.
- Identification and verification of buyer (passport; NIF; proof of address).
- Beneficial ownership identification for corporate buyers (UBO register check).
- Suspicious transaction reporting to UIAML (Unidade de Informação Financeira).
- No cash transactions above statutory thresholds (typically €1,000 for real estate); refuse cash offers.
- Cooperation with notary AML checks at deed; complete documentation pre-deed.

### 11.6.4  Consumer Protection

- Disclosure of conflicts of interest in writing before any transactional engagement.
- Right of withdrawal for off-premises contracts under Portuguese consumer law (decálogo do consumidor).
- Mandatory pre-contractual information (fee structure, scope, term) before BRA signing.
- Clear pricing; no hidden fees; commission source disclosed in writing.

## 11.8  Fee Models — Detailed Treatment


Fee models are detailed in Chapter 2.4; this section supplements with the operational discipline around fee setting, fee disclosure, and fee collection.


### 11.8.1  Fee Setting Principles

- Fee must be commensurate with work: a 1.5% success fee on a €300k property (€4,500) is barely economic; the same fee on a €2m property (€30,000) is generous.
- For low-value transactions, prefer flat fee or retainer.
- For high-value transactions, prefer flat fee or capped success fee; pure % is disproportionate.
- Cap success fee at offer-acceptance to remove perverse incentive against price negotiation.
- Always disclose fee source (buyer vs shared listing commission) in writing.

### 11.8.2  Fee Collection

- Invoice on the trigger event defined in BRA (offer acceptance, CPCV, deed).
- For success fees: invoice at deed, payable within 7–14 days.
- For retainers: invoice monthly in advance, payable within 7 days.
- For flat fees: invoice in tranches tied to milestones.
- Issue receipts; maintain VAT compliance; provide annual statement to buyer for tax purposes.

# Appendix C — Sample Scripts, Clauses & Red Flags Quick Reference


This appendix consolidates the sample scripts, protective CPCV clause language, and the master red-flag list into a single quick-reference document. The scripts are illustrative; the agent should adapt them to the specific transaction and buyer. The clauses are templates for the buyer's lawyer to adapt. The red flags are organised by phase for fast scanning during a live transaction.


## C.1  Sample Client Questions by Phase


### C.1.1  Phase 1 — Onboarding


> **AGENT — MOTIVATION PROBE**
>
> *"Walk me through what brought you to consider Portugal. Was there a specific trigger — a visit, a friend, a tax change, a retirement timeline?"*
>
> *"What does success look like for you in 12 months? In 5 years?"*
>
> *"What would make you walk away from this process? What are the things you absolutely don't want to compromise on?"*


> **AGENT — TIMELINE PROBE**
>
> *"What's the latest acceptable date by which you'd want the keys?"*
>
> *"Is that date hard or soft? What happens if we miss it by a month?"*
>
> *"Are there external dependencies — a lease expiry, a school year, a visa application — that drive the timing?"*


> **AGENT — RISK APPETITE PROBE**
>
> *"How do you feel about buying off-plan? About a property that needs full renovation? About a property with a heritage listing?"*
>
> *"If we found a property that was 90% right but had one issue — say, an F energy rating or a less-than-ideal orientation — how would you weigh that against a 5% price reduction?"*


### C.1.2  Phase 2 — Sourcing


> **AGENT — PROPERTY REACTION PROBE (AFTER VIEWING)**
>
> *"Forget the price for a moment. If this property were free, would you want to live in it? Why or why not?"*
>
> *"What surprised you — positively or negatively — about what you saw today versus the listing?"*
>
> *"On a scale of 1 to 10, how would you rate this property against [property viewed last week]? What's the difference?"*


> **AGENT — PRICING REALITY PROBE**
>
> *"Based on the comparables I've prepared, properties like this in this micro-market have transacted between €[X] and €[Y] per square metre in the last 18 months. The asking price is €[Z]/m². How does that match your expectation?"*
>
> *"If we were to offer, where would you start? My recommendation would be [range], based on [evidence]."*


### C.1.3  Phase 4 — Due Diligence


> **AGENT — DD FINDINGS BRIEFING**
>
> *"I want to walk you through the due diligence findings before we discuss next steps. There are [N] items I'd classify as material. Three of them are normal for this type of property and I'd recommend we accept them. Two are more significant and I'd recommend we either renegotiate the price or walk away. Can we go through them now?"*


> **AGENT — WALK-AWAY RECOMMENDATION**
>
> *"I want to be direct: based on the structural survey and the title review, I do not recommend we proceed with this property at the current price. The cost of remediation, combined with the unresolved title issue, takes the effective cost above the comparable ceiling. If you decide to proceed against my recommendation, I'll need that decision in writing for my file, but my professional advice is that we walk away and look at the other two properties on the shortlist."*


### C.1.4  Phase 6 — Negotiation


> **AGENT — OFFER AUTHORISATION**
>
> *"I'm ready to send the offer. The headline is €[X], with a deposit of €[Y] in escrow, a 60-day DD condition, and a 45-day financing condition. The offer is supported by the comparable set I sent you last week. Before I send it, I need your written authorisation — can you confirm by email?"*


> **AGENT — COUNTER-OFFER DISCUSSION**
>
> *"The seller has countered at €[X], which is €[Y] above our offer and €[Z] below their asking price. Based on the comparables and the DD findings, my recommendation is to hold at our original offer or, at most, increase to €[X+5%]. I do not recommend matching their counter — it would take us above the comparable ceiling for a property with [issue]."*

## C.5  Objection Handling Scripts


> **AGENT — BUYER: 'I WANT TO MAKE A HIGHER OFFER TO BE SAFE'**
>
> *"I understand the instinct, but let me push back. The comparable set shows we're already at the upper end of fair value. Increasing the offer would take us above the comparable ceiling, and we'd be over-paying from day one. If there's a written counter from the seller, we can consider a small increase. Without one, we're bidding against ourselves, and that's the single most expensive mistake a buyer can make."*


> **AGENT — BUYER: 'THE LISTING AGENT SAYS THERE'S ANOTHER OFFER'**
>
> *"I've heard that before. The question is whether it's a real, written, signed offer — or whether it's a pressure tactic. I've asked the listing agent for written evidence, and they haven't provided any. Until they do, our offer stands. If they come back with a written counter, we'll discuss. Until then, we hold firm."*


> **AGENT — BUYER: 'I LOVE THIS PROPERTY, CAN'T WE JUST SKIP DD?'**
>
> *"I know you love it, and that's exactly why we need DD. The DD isn't there to find reasons not to buy — it's there to confirm that the property is what we think it is, so you can buy it with confidence. Skipping DD on a property you love is the worst possible time to skip it, because emotion is overriding judgment. We can compress the DD timeline if you want, but we cannot skip it."*


> **AGENT — BUYER: 'THE SELLER WANTS THE DEPOSIT PAID DIRECTLY TO THEM'**
>
> *"I understand that's what the seller wants, but it's not what we'll do. The deposit goes into a neutral escrow account — either my lawyer's client account or the notary's. That way, if anything goes wrong between CPCV and deed, the deposit is protected and can be returned to you within days. Paying the seller directly gives up your strongest protection, and I won't recommend it."*


> **AGENT — BUYER: 'SHOULD I JUST USE THE LISTING AGENT SINCE THEY KNOW THE PROPERTY?'**
>
> *"The listing agent does know the property — and they're contractually bound to the seller to get the best price for it. They cannot represent you; at best they can act as a dual agent, which means they cannot fully advocate for either side. If you want someone in your corner — someone whose only job is to protect your interests — you need your own agent. That's the role I play. I'm happy to work alongside the listing agent, but I work for you."*
